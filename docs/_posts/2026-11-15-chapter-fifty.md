The printer took two sheets at once. Sarah heard them go through together and put her hand out to stop the second one falling. It was blank. The other came out with the bottom of Mo’s signature cut off.

“Leave a bit of room round it,” Betty said.

Sarah changed the setting and printed it again. The plate number was in a box at the top of the sheet. Betty fetched the sheet Jo had left and read the plate number back to Sarah from that. At the bottom, beneath Mo’s signature, there was a second set of initials. Sarah enlarged the email to see if Mo had said whose they were.

“They’re the fitter’s. He read the bench clock.”

Betty took the sheet Sarah had written that morning and laid it alongside the new one. Mo had written down what both clocks said when the power came back, with a row for each battery. Betty worked across Sarah’s first line, bench clock and then monitor, and found both times on Mo’s row for the old battery. She did the same for the new one.

Sarah picked up the stapler. “Where do you want these?”

Betty brought out the sideways printout with the Wednesday reading. She put it between the two sheets and found the six-fourteen line. Then she pulled it clear of both of them.

“I want this the way they sent it,” she said.

“It’s the same file on the laptop.”

“Can they send it again?”

Sarah set down the stapler and went back to the depot’s email from Friday the twentieth. Beside *Collection receipt* was the file they’d opened to read the watts. Sarah opened it again so Betty could see the headings.

“I can ask them to keep the copy they took off the monitor, and this attachment as they sent it.”

“Both,” Betty said. “If somebody asks why I said six fourteen, I was looking at the one they sent us.”

Sarah began the email with the plate number. She wrote *original download* and stopped.

“Can you ask for the one from Thursday without saying it’s the first one?” Betty asked. “We know when they wrote it in the book.”

Sarah typed *Thursday 19 November, 11:23*, straight from the depot book. She turned the screen so Betty could read the request. On the line below she asked them to keep the readings attachment from the depot email of Friday the twentieth, the one sent alongside *Collection receipt*. Betty asked her to send it.

Anne came to the table with the envelope. She’d written her answer beside the sum, in the space Betty had left. Betty lifted the flap and found the figure she’d written there when she first worked it out.

“Did you get the same?” Betty asked.

Anne held the flap up so she could see both at once. “Yes. Mine took more room.”

Betty took the blank sheet that had gone through the printer and slid it under the envelope. Anne drew a line down the middle. She put the two answers at the top, one on either side, and began to show Betty how she’d got hers.
