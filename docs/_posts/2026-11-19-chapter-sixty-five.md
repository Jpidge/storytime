On Wednesday Sarah sent the equipment reply before they had lunch. She attached a saved copy of it, the fitter’s sheet and the log file to a new message, with Jo’s visit number above the equipment reference. Betty asked her to leave the sent message open until she’d checked all three attachments were there.

At five past three Jo rang. Betty was putting the clean knives into the drawer. She laid the last one on the worktop.

“I’ve seen the note,” Jo said. “I’ve got the equipment reply open too.”

Betty asked her to read the first line of the note. Jo did. It was the sentence the woman had read back that morning.

“And the sheet I signed?”

“That’s here as well.”

Betty sat down. Jo said she wanted to come round and go through it with her. She could do tomorrow, Thursday morning.

“What would we be going through?”

“The instruction on your sheet. You asked whether it still applied.”

Betty drew the envelope towards her. The pencil was underneath it. She took it out and held it without writing.

“Can you tell me that now?”

Jo said the monitor had been collected. Betty wouldn’t be asked to put it back in the socket to repeat what they’d already tested.

“I know it’s gone,” Betty said. “I’ve still got what you told me to do.”

There was a pause. Betty heard Jo move something on her desk.

“The equipment reply doesn’t tell us what was connected here in November,” Jo said.

“No.”

“So I can’t change the sheet to say there was nothing there.”

“I’m not asking you to.”

Sarah had come to the kitchen door. Betty turned her chair far enough to see her and put the phone on speaker.

“I want you to put what I’m meant to do now,” Betty said.

Jo asked whether she had the sheet in front of her. Betty took it out. She read the instruction from beside her signature, slowly, keeping her place with the pencil. When she finished, Jo read it back from the photograph.

“That was for the test with the monitor,” Jo said. “That test is finished.”

“Could you put that by it?”

“I can add a dated note to the visit. I can send you a copy.”

Betty asked whether someone opening the photograph would see the new note. Jo said the photograph would stay as it was. The note would be on the visit with the equipment reply. She could put the date of Betty’s sheet in the note so it was clear which instruction it closed.

“Read it before you send it,” Betty said.

Jo took a little while. Sarah leaned against the door frame. The knife on the worktop was still wet near the handle.

Jo read that the instruction on the sheet dated nineteenth of November had applied to the test using monitor 4173. That test had ended with the monitor’s collection. There was no continuing instruction under that test to leave the connected equipment in place.

Betty asked her to read the last sentence again. Then she asked for Thursday’s time.

“Ten thirty,” Jo said. “Would you like Sarah there?”

Betty looked up. Sarah nodded.

“Yes,” Betty said. “Send her the note too.”

After the call Betty laid her sheet on the table. She put the pencil across it, then moved the pencil aside so it covered none of the writing. In the kitchen she dried the knife and put it in the drawer. She shut the drawer with her hip and went back to wait for Jo’s copy.
