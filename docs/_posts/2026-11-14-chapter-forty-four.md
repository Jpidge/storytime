Bill brought the two plugs in a paper bag. He put the bag on the chair by the socket, then took it off again when Betty came in.

“I thought we could see what it counts,” he said.

Betty looked inside. There was a night-light and a timer with its pins pushed down all the way round. “Which one does what?”

“The light gives it something to count. I’ve set the timer to stay on. Its clock runs even with nothing plugged into it. We can see whether my monitor picks up the clock alone.”

Sarah took the depot’s reply out of the printer. It was dated Monday the twenty-third. She read the line about the monitor to Bill: the figure in the watts column came from the socket on the front of the block. The block’s own supply was taken before that socket. She clipped the reply to Friday’s printout.

“Then it can’t be the block drawing it,” Betty said.

“It shouldn’t show the block’s draw,” Bill said. “The other question is what it shows when there’s nothing on the front.”

He tipped the bag so she could see the night-light and the timer together. His own monitor was in his coat pocket. He used it at the bench to check what he was leaving on overnight. It was grey, with a dial where Jo’s had had a light.

Sarah pointed to the picture at the bottom of the depot’s reply. “Jo took that photo before she packed it,” Sarah said. “It says so underneath.”

Bill set his beside the picture. He turned it over and read the model number. Sarah read the number from the picture. The last two letters were different.

“We can still see what yours does,” Betty said.

Bill nodded. “That won’t tell us what Jo’s did.”

Bill plugged his monitor into the wall and left the socket on its front empty. Betty turned her chair beside the socket so Anne could reach the plug without leaning across her. The display ran through its figures, then showed zero. Anne had come in and was standing with one hand on the back of the chair.

“Can I put the light in?”

“Wait,” Betty said. “I want to see it again.”

They sat through a second zero. Betty let Anne plug in the night-light. Its little window came on so faintly she put her palm above it to see. The display stayed at zero until Bill had got all the way up from his knees. Then it showed one point eight.

Anne took her hand away. “That was on before the number.”

Sarah wrote down what Anne had said. She started a new line for the model number. Bill turned the monitor so she could read the last two letters herself.

“Do you want to try the timer now?” he asked.

Betty watched the night-light. “Do that one again first. I want to see if it’s late every time.”

Anne unplugged it. The window went dark straight away. Betty kept her eyes on the display. As soon as it went back to zero she told Anne to put the light in. Sarah watched the second hand. This time the number came up almost at once.

“Same number?” Anne asked.

“One point eight,” Betty said. “It just got there sooner.”

Sarah put both runs on the same page. They could see the light come on both times. They had no way to see whether its draw had settled before the number appeared. Betty turned her chair back towards the socket and left it there.
