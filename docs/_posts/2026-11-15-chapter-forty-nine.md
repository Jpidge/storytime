On Monday the thirtieth Betty had the envelope open on the table when the phone rang. She had been trying to work out the sum on the back without turning up the flap to see the answer. She put the kitchen pencil down and drew its string clear of the envelope.

Mo said she had the monitor on the bench. She had asked someone to stand with her and watch the bench clock.

“Can you send us what you wrote?” Betty asked.

“I can. Do you want me to tell you first?”

Sarah brought a chair up beside Betty. Betty moved the phone to the middle of the table.

With the old battery still in, Mo had set the monitor beside the bench clock. She let them run together until both showed ten past nine. Then she disconnected the monitor. When she powered it again a minute later, she left the computer lead on the bench.

“The bench clock said nine eleven. The monitor still said nine ten.”

“What did it do with the new battery?” Sarah asked.

“I put it in and at twenty past we disconnected the monitor again. When I switched it back on a minute later, both clocks said nine twenty-one.”

Betty turned the envelope over. There was no room beside the sum for all four times. Sarah pulled a sheet from the printer tray and laid it across the envelope.

“Is that what happened here on Wednesday the eighteenth?” Betty asked.

“I can write down what it did with that battery this morning,” Mo said. “I can’t get it back to Wednesday.”

Betty asked for the sheet with both tests on it. Mo said she would include the times and the number from the plate on the front, 4173. After they hung up, Sarah copied the four times onto two lines. Betty watched to see where Sarah would put Wednesday. There was room below the second line. Sarah wrote *Monday 30 November, depot* at the top instead.

Anne came in from the hall. She took off one shoe and held it upside down. A small piece of grit fell onto the floor. She got down on one knee to look for it.

“Did they find the missing minute?” she asked.

“They made it lose a minute,” Betty said. “They watched it go.”

Anne found the grit and set it on the windowsill. She sat down to put her shoe on again. Then she pulled the envelope towards her and took up the pencil.
