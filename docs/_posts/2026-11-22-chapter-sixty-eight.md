The printer, on the low stand beside the table, began while Jo was still putting her coat on. Sarah stood beside it, holding the first page by the edge so it would not fall behind the table. Betty waited for the second.

“Is there another?” she asked.

Sarah looked at the laptop. “One more.”

Jo came back from the door. The third page had only the end of the new note and the date beneath it. Betty laid the three pages in a row. Yesterday’s two were still beside the envelope, without their clip.

“Which one have they got?” she asked.

Jo left her coat open. “The earlier entry is still there. This goes with it.”

“Will they see it?”

“If they open the visit.”

Betty looked at the third page. Sarah had caught it before it reached the floor. There was a grey mark along one edge where the printer had taken it through.

“And if they don’t?”

Jo pulled the chair out again.

Sarah moved the laptop away from the printer and put it on the table. On its screen the new note ended where it should. Betty could see the date without turning a page.

“Who did you send the first one to?” she asked.

Jo asked whether she meant yesterday’s copy.

“The one that put me there.”

Jo sat down. She opened the record and scrolled back. Betty watched her hand stop, move again, then stop at a different place.

“I can check where it was sent,” Jo said. “That isn’t the same as knowing who’s read it.”

“Check that first, then.”

Sarah brought Jo’s cup back. She had taken the tea bag out. Jo thanked her without looking away from the screen.

Betty picked up the third printed page and put it behind the second. The first stayed where it was. She could not remember whether the clip would hold three; yesterday she had tried it with two. She brought the corners together and fitted the clip over them. When she lifted it, the bottom page slipped a little.

“Leave them down,” Sarah said. “I’ll find a bigger one.”

“Not yet.”

Betty set them flat. She would leave the new copy spread out until she had finished comparing the dates. Jo had found something and was reading it twice.

“There’s an address here,” she said. “I need to find out whether a correction goes through to it automatically.”

“Can you ask?”

“Yes.”

“While you’re here?”

Jo glanced at the time, then took her phone out of her coat pocket. Sarah drew the chair nearer the socket, but Jo shook her head; there was enough charge.

Betty took the small clip off the three pages. She put it back on yesterday’s two and laid them to the left of the new copy. The date on the old note was visible. So was the date on the new one. Betty left the new pages loose, side by side; she had not finished with them.

Jo waited through the ringing. When someone answered, she gave only her own name. Then she stopped and looked across the table.

“May I give them your details?”

“Tell me who it is first.”

Jo asked the person on the phone for their name and department. Betty pulled the pencil towards her. Sarah turned over a clean sheet and put it under Betty’s hand.
