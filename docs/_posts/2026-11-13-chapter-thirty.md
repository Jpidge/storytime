The man read the number a second time. This time Sarah got it down. She read it back and heard him say yes before he asked who she was.

“Sarah Miller. I was there for the first test.”

“Then you know where the card is.”

“I saw the card you marked for the test. You still had it when we left.”

“That was the test card,” he said. “This is the one they sent out this morning.”

Bill asked him to put his sheet on the desk. For a while Sarah could hear only the two of them moving around in the office. Then Betty spoke from close to the phone.

“Mine’s here,” she said.

Sarah waited for Betty to say where. There was a little knock, like the edge of a card against the receiver.

“Did it let you in today?” Sarah asked.

“I haven’t been out yet.”

The installer asked Bill if he could open the box. Bill said yes, and Sarah heard the tape come away in one pull. Then Bill asked the man to wait. He wanted to write down what was in it before anything came out.

“Read it from where you’re standing,” Bill said.

The installer read the label from the flap. It gave the same number Sarah had just written down. Inside was a reader wrapped in a sheet of brown paper. There was a cutout in the packing beside it, about the size of Betty’s card. The man lifted the box toward the window and looked into the cutout.

“Empty,” he said. “The packing list says the card goes there.”

“Was it open when you got it?”

“You heard me open it.”

“I heard the tape. Was there any tape underneath?”

Sarah heard the box go down on the desk. The man said no. Bill wrote something in the book and read it back to him. He had put *cutout empty when opened*. The man asked him to put the time beside it.

Sarah looked at the receipt. “Can you tell which number belongs to the box and which one belongs to the visit?”

“I can tell you what’s printed,” the man said. “The one on the box is on today’s sheet.”

“And yesterday’s?”

Bill read it from his copy. The installer asked him to pass it over. While he looked at it, Anne came in from the garden with the empty peg bag. She hung it on the handle of the kitchen door and eased the handle up so the latch wouldn’t click over the man’s voice.

“I didn’t get this page yesterday,” the installer said.

“You wrote it,” Bill said.

“I wrote it on the tablet. I didn’t get a copy when it went through.”

Bill waited. “Do you want that one?”

There was a rustle at the far end. Then the installer asked if Bill could make him a copy with the time on it. He said he could take the box back with the paper inside. He would leave the reader on the door as it was.

Betty asked if he wanted to see her card before he went.

“Only if you want to show me.”

A moment went by. Sarah heard the same small knock against the receiver.

“Can you read it from there?” Betty asked.

“Just the end. I’d have to take it from you to read the rest.”

“Then read the end.”

He did. Bill wrote the digits in the book and read them back. Betty said he’d got them right. Nobody asked Betty to hand the card over. Sarah had the pen ready, but there was nothing to add to the receipt.
