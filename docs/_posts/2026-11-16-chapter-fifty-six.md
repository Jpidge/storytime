Mo sent the results on Friday the fourth of December. Betty was cutting bread when Sarah said there was an attachment. The slice caught on the blade and tore where she eased it off. She put the knife down with the blade towards the wall.

“Does she say how long?”

“Half an hour after it settled.”

“After what settled?”

Sarah read the note below the table. Mo had started the monitor with the front socket empty. She had left the bench computer disconnected and watched the display until it read zero. Then she’d started the half hour. Mo had copied thirty rows from the monitor’s log, each a minute apart. All of them said zero.

Betty pulled up a chair. “What did it say before the zero?”

Sarah scrolled back up. There was a second table on the first page, just two lines long. On the first line Mo had entered the time on the bench clock. Beside it she’d written *0.4 W on display*. On the second line, nine seconds later by the same clock, she’d written *0 W on display*.

“Nothing in the front then either?” Betty asked.

Sarah read back the line at the top of the page. The socket had been empty from before Mo switched the monitor on.

Betty picked up her piece of bread and set it down on its other side. “So it can say it with nothing there.”

“The display can,” Sarah said. She went back to the thirty rows. “The point four is in Mo’s note. It isn’t in the log rows she sent.”

“Because she started that part after it went to zero.”

Sarah took her hand off the trackpad. They looked at the two short lines. It was possible for both of them to be right on the same sheet.

“Can she get the beginning out of it?” Betty asked.

Sarah started a reply. Betty went back for the bread knife. She cut a thinner slice this time, which didn’t catch on the blade, and brought both slices to the table.

Sarah had asked whether the monitor kept any rows from the start of Friday’s run, before the thirty zeroes. She turned the screen towards Betty.

“Ask if there’s a row for while she could see the point four,” Betty said. “Even if what it says is zero.”

Sarah added the question. Betty read it once more. Then she pushed the plate across so Sarah could have the slice that had come away whole.
