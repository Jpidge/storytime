There was a dry place to put a foot for most of the way down. Betty knew which ones would still be dry on the way back. When Sarah stepped on a pale patch that looked like sand, Betty caught her by the cuff and waited while she pulled her shoe free.

“Did you see it move?” Betty asked.

“I saw where somebody had been.”

“That was me yesterday.”

Sarah turned to look. The gatehouse window reflected the sky. She could see Anne on the steps with her arms around the book. David had gone up to move the car. From here it was possible to see both ends of the road at once.

“Do you go all the way to the water every day?”

Betty shook her head. “Only if I can’t tell from the window.”

At the end of the flattened strip there was a plank with two bolts through it. One bolt held it to a post, and the other had nothing under it. Betty stepped past the loose end. Sarah put her foot where Betty had put hers. The grass went flat under her sole and then came up again behind her.

“Here,” Betty said.

Sarah could see the water coming over the bottom of the post. A little piece of foam went downstream and caught on a blade that was lying the other way. It hung there for a moment. Then it slid under.

Behind them the gatehouse door opened. The installer called that he had the blank ready. Betty turned and showed him her bent card, holding it above her shoulder so he could see that she still had it.

“Can he wait?” Betty asked.

Sarah called up that they were coming. Betty stayed where she was.

“Look at the bolt,” she said.

The water had reached the head of the lower bolt. Sarah watched until she saw a bright edge form above it and then fall away. Betty went back two steps and picked up a broken length of reed. She laid it across the track at the edge of the water.

“Will that stay?” Sarah asked.

“Long enough.”

They walked back in single file. At the foot of the steps Betty put her old card to the reader. There was no click. Through the fence Sarah could see the installer kneeling with his case open.

“You said you’d wait,” she called.

He lifted his head. “I waited to take it off. That’s still the old one.”

Betty drew the card away. A drop of water ran from the bent corner down the back of her hand. The face of the card was wet. She wiped it on the dry part of her sleeve and tried again. This time the latch moved. She held the door for Sarah.

On the steps the installer turned his tablet so they could see the grey number beside a little picture of a card. He had drawn a line on the blank with a pencil so he could tell it from the others.

“That’s the one I wrote,” he said. The clock at the top of the tablet turned to 2:00. “Can I swap the reader now?”

Sarah looked at Betty. Betty was looking through the fence at the reed. From the steps Sarah couldn’t see it at all.

“Can you put the old one back if you have to?” Sarah asked.

“As long as I keep the plug on it.”

Bill came down to hold the door open while he worked. Anne put the book on the landing and held it there with her heel. Nobody spoke until the installer passed the old reader through the bars to Bill. There was a little dirt rectangle behind it where the paint hadn’t faded.

The new reader lit up when he connected it. He took the marked card from his pocket and went out through the door. Bill let it shut. The installer pressed the card flat against the reader. A green light came on. He pulled on the handle twice. Both times the latch held.

“Can you open it from there?” he asked Bill.

Bill pushed the bar. The installer came through and tried the handle from the outside once more with Bill holding the bar down. It opened.

“The bar still works,” he said.

Sarah waited for him to put the card to the reader again. He had already slipped it into his pocket. “It didn’t open for the card,” she said.

“What does it say on your screen?” Sarah asked.

He picked up the tablet. A line had appeared at the bottom. He turned it for her. The time was right. The number was the one on the blank. She asked Anne to open the book. At the top of the page, above the old number, it said *South Gate*. In the same place on the tablet’s new line it said *Pier Six*. The installer moved his thumb as if to wipe it away, but the words stayed.

“Is that somewhere else?” Anne asked.

Sarah nodded. She could hear David coming down from the road. She took a picture of the tablet with her phone, then another with the marked card beside it. The installer watched her do it.

“Do you want me to try it again?”

“Could you put the old one back first?”

He looked at Bill. Bill held it out, plug dangling. The installer took it with both hands. Betty went down to the door and looked through. After a moment she came back up to the step where she could see over the fence.

“Where’s your reed?” Sarah asked.

“Look where I put it,” Betty said. The track was under water now. “I’ll have to put it higher tomorrow.”
