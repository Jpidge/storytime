Helen rang while Sarah was looking for an envelope big enough to take the sideways sheet without folding it. Betty nodded towards the phone. Sarah put it on the table and turned up the sound.

“Have they asked you for anything?” Betty said.

“No. I was going to ask what you wanted me to do with the email.”

“The one from the depot?”

“The one to dispatch. I kept the message where they read back what to do at the front door.”

Betty looked under the telephone. She drew the old envelope out until she could see Anne’s *ring once* on the flap. The paper had worn thin where they’d handled it, just under the tick.

“Could you ask them to ring me?”

Helen waited. “What do you want them to ring about?”

Betty picked up the sheet she’d signed. For a moment she thought she could give Helen the first line and Helen could tell it to whoever answered. She put it down again.

“I want to know where I can send this. Six fourteen is what I read on the printout. Quarter past six is what I remember setting on the oven timer. If I send it to you, will it go on their record as something you told them?”

Helen said she didn’t know. She could ask them where they put things that came from the person at the address.

“Ask them to tell me,” Betty said. “I can write down where it goes.”

Sarah pulled a chair up. She found a white envelope in the drawer with the printer paper. Betty put her hand on it before Sarah could tuck the flap in.

Helen rang back before lunch. Dispatch had offered to take the details down over the phone. Helen had asked whose name would go on the note if she gave it. They said hers would go in the caller box. She asked for somewhere Betty could send her own account. They gave her an address, which she could forward.

“Read me the name it goes to,” Betty said.

“It starts with *visits*,” Helen said, and read the rest. Betty asked whether it was the one that had sent the replacement reference. Helen went back to that message. It said *dispatch* before the at sign. The part after it was the same.

Betty had Sarah write the new address on the white envelope even though they wouldn’t be posting it. Under it she put the address of the house. She read that back to Helen herself, including the number. When they’d finished, she slipped her statement inside with the sideways sheet, leaving the flap up. From her chair she could reach in and take out either one.
