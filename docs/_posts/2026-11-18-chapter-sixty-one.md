The woman from visits asked if Betty could wait while she opened Mo’s message. Betty took the iron off the end of the table and put it on the draining board. When she sat down again the woman was reading the collection number back to her.

“That’s it,” Betty said. “Has she sent the photograph too?”

“I’ve got the photograph and the file.”

Sarah had put the laptop where Betty could see it. Betty put the envelope beside the trackpad to make room for her elbow.

“Can you put them with my sheet?” she asked.

“Yes. I can add them to the visit. What would you like the note to say?”

Betty looked at Sarah. They hadn’t written anything for the woman to put in a note.

“Could you read what’s there now?”

“Equipment collected.” The woman waited a little. “That’s under outcome.”

“What’s under the thing I asked?”

The woman asked Betty to hold. There was music for a little while, then the click at the end that sounded as if someone had hung up. Betty said hello. The woman said she was still there. She’d opened the message from November and found the photograph of Betty’s signed sheet.

“I can read what you asked on the photograph,” she said. “When I close it I’m back on the collection.”

“Who opens it after you?”

“If it stays on this visit it’ll be there if someone looks up the collection.”

Sarah moved her hand away from the laptop. Betty could see the print Sarah’s finger had left on the screen that morning.

“Can you send the question to somebody who has to answer it?” Betty asked.

The woman said she could open an equipment query. She asked whether Betty wanted to report a fault. Betty drew the envelope closer. She could hear the woman waiting with her hand on the keys.

“Put that I want to know what it was measuring,” she said. “Send whoever takes the query my signed sheet and the fitter’s bench sheet. Send them the whole log as well.”

The woman read the question back and asked which number to call if somebody needed to speak to her. Betty gave her the number she was calling from. Then she asked for the new reference. She wrote it under the depot date on the envelope, slowly enough that the woman had to wait between pairs of digits.

When the call ended Sarah reached over to shut the laptop. Betty took the envelope away first. She looked back up the four lines on the envelope. The number for her message and the number for Jo’s visit were at the top. The question had a number of its own now.
