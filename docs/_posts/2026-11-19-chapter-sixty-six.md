Jo’s copy arrived at three forty-six that afternoon. Sarah opened it on the laptop and Betty fetched her glasses from the kitchen. There were two pages. The first had Jo’s name and the date, Wednesday the ninth of December. The second began with the last sentence of the note.

“Can you get that on the first page?” Betty asked.

Sarah made the print smaller. In the preview the sentence moved up underneath the rest. Betty leaned forward to read it, then sat back.

“Too small.”

Sarah put it back. She printed both pages and laid them side by side. Betty read the note through, including the last sentence on its own page. Jo had put the date of the signed sheet and the monitor’s number in it, just as she’d read them out.

“Have you got yours?” Betty asked.

Sarah checked her phone. “Yes.”

Betty picked up the first page. The second stayed on the table. She put the first one down again and went to the drawer for a paper clip.

Sarah asked if she wanted the original sheet clipped behind the new note. Betty took it out of the envelope and put it beside the two pages. Her signature was at the bottom. Above it was the instruction Jo had read back that afternoon.

“Keep that by itself,” Betty said.

She clipped Jo’s two pages together. In the top corner of the second one she wrote *2*, then went back and put *1* on the first. She lifted them by the clip to see whether it held. The lower corners spread apart and came back together when she put them down.

“Do you want to ring her?” Sarah asked.

“What for?”

“To say we’ve got it.”

“Tomorrow,” Betty said. “She can see it tomorrow.”

Sarah began putting the other papers into their piles. She left the sideways printout where Betty could reach it. Betty picked it up and looked at the time in the row. She put the signed sheet back into the envelope first, then slid the printout in beside it. Jo’s new note went in last, still clipped.

At the window the light had gone off the houses opposite. Sarah said they would need the hall light if they went out now. Betty asked where they were going.

“I thought I’d get some bread. You could come.”

Betty looked down at her slippers. Her shoes were upstairs where she’d left them after fixing the draught strip.

“Let me get my shoes.”

She took the envelope upstairs with her, then stopped on the landing. There was no room for it in the bag she used for the shops without folding the new pages. She came back down and laid it on the kitchen table.

Sarah was waiting by the door with her coat on. Betty put her own coat over her arm and went back to move the envelope away from the edge of the table. She left it beside the pencil. At the door she pulled on her coat and bent to look at the draught strip. It was holding. She stepped over it, waited while Sarah turned the key, and asked her to take the longer way to the shop.
