Anne handed Bill the timer. He was trying to get it back into his coat pocket when Mo’s picture came through. Sarah opened it on the laptop and set the printout below the screen. The flag sat in a grey strip to the left of the figures. Above it was the word *status*.

“Can I see the one before it?” Betty asked.

Both rows were in the picture. The first had nothing in the grey strip. Sarah pointed to it, then read the time on each row. Betty followed on the printout with the end of her glasses.

“If it had been off for ten minutes, there’d be ten lines missing,” Anne said.

“There would if Jo’s monitor kept time while it was off,” Bill said. “I don’t know if it does. Ask Mo what happens to its clock when it starts again.”

Sarah read the label in the grey strip aloud. *Startup*. She wrote it beside the words she’d taken down from Mo, then gave Betty the pencil so she could put a circle round the row she meant.

“Can you ask her if that happens when somebody pulls the plug?” Betty said.

Sarah started to type. Betty watched the words appear.

“Or if the power goes off,” she said.

Sarah added that and asked what time the monitor used after a restart. “Anything else?”

“If it can happen with the plug still in and the power still on.”

Bill took the timer back out of his pocket and set it on the folded bag. The pencil mark was still where Anne had left it. He turned it so she could see the arrow.

“Would you test that one at the bench?” Anne asked.

“I could. I’ve got a pad by the meter. We can put both readings on the same page.”

“Can I come and see where you write it?”

Bill looked at Betty. “After tea, if you’re finished here.”

Betty pulled the envelope towards her. There was a patch on the flap where the paper had worn thin under the tick Anne made after Jo shut the gate. She picked it up and held it against the light from the window. The mark was still quite plain from the front. From the back it was only a small dent.

Sarah turned the laptop so Betty could read what she’d written. Betty read to the bottom, then asked her to put six thirteen and six fourteen in the first line, where Mo would see them before she opened the picture. Sarah moved them up and sent it.

Anne picked the timer up by its edges. “Am I taking this?”

“Put it by your shoes,” Bill said. “I’ll carry the bag.”

She took it into the hall. Betty listened until she heard it touch the floor and then asked Sarah to leave the picture open. She wanted to copy the word in the grey strip before the screen went dark.
