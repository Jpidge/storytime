Betty felt for the string before she went home. The bag was drawn shut, with the knot just outside her pocket. Anne held the kitchen door while she buttoned her coat.

At the door Betty said, “Tell them the bell works.”

Sarah had already put the receipt away. “Does it?”

“It did when I came out. I rang it with the door open.”

Anne waited till Betty had gone before asking if they could go round and ring it themselves.

“We can go round,” Sarah said. “Once she’s had time to get there.”

The phone rang while Anne was putting her shoes on. It was Bill. He had Helen’s message open on the little computer at the end of his bench. He wanted to know if he could shut it down.

“Has anybody answered?”

“I’ve got a woman here who says she can tell me where the picture came from.”

“Is she there with you?”

“On the other phone. It’s Ruth from the help desk. Helen sent her the picture and gave her my number when she couldn’t stay on the line. Hang on and I’ll put her on the bench.”

There was a scrape, and then a woman said hello from a little way off.

“I can see the same picture,” she said. “I’ve got the whole window here.”

“Can you tell me which site it’s for?”

“Pier Six. It says so up at the top. The bit you’ve got starts below that.”

Sarah got the receipt out. She had to put the phone between her ear and shoulder to open it.

“Can you read me the number next to it?”

The woman read it slowly. Sarah followed the digits with her pen. They were the ones Helen had read.

“Is that where you’re sending somebody?” Sarah asked.

“I don’t send anybody. I can see what screen the picture’s from.”

“What can you see for South Gate?”

“I’d have to close this one first.” The woman paused. “I can send you the whole picture before I do.”

“Send it to Helen, please. On the message she already sent.”

Bill came back on after the woman hung up. He asked where on his sheet he should put what she’d said. Sarah looked at the receipt. She wrote *Pier Six* beside the question about the picture.

“Put it with the picture,” she said. “We know which screen she was looking at.”

Bill read his entry back. Sarah checked it against hers, then drew another line beneath the answer. “We still need the address on the booking itself,” she said. She wrote *Where is the visit booked?* and left a space after it.

Helen rang before they went out. The full picture had arrived on the same thread. She read the site name and number across the top. Sarah put a tick beside Pier Six.

Anne was standing with one foot on the mat and one on the step. “Is it time?”

Sarah found her shoes. On the way over Anne asked if they ought to knock first, in case Betty was doing something.

“Let’s try the bell once,” Sarah said.

Anne pressed it and stood clear of the door. They heard two notes from somewhere inside, the second lower than the first. A key turned. Betty opened the door with the little key still in her hand.

“I heard it from the back,” she said.

Anne stepped inside to see how far back she meant. Sarah stayed on the step a moment and looked at the number fixed to the brick. It was the number Betty had given her. “What do I tell them about the bell?” Anne asked.

Sarah looked from the number on the brick to the plain button beside the door. “Tell them where it is. If they’ve got the right address, they’ll find it.”
