Jo arrived at ten thirty on Thursday the tenth of December. Betty opened the door before Sarah could get up. Jo wiped her shoes on the mat and waited while Betty moved the shopping bag off the chair.

The bread they’d bought yesterday was on the table. Sarah took it into the kitchen. Betty drew Jo’s two pages out of the envelope and put them where the loaf had been, still held by the clip.

“That’s what you sent.”

Jo took her coat off. “Yes.”

“Have you got the same thing?”

Jo opened the visit on her screen and turned it so Betty could see. The last sentence was there without a page break. Betty followed it to the end, then drew her own copy back towards her.

Sarah came in with the cups. Jo moved her screen to make room. She asked Betty whether there was anything else she wanted them to go through.

Betty took out the sideways printout. Sarah fetched the account she had written on the first of December from the drawer where they kept it. Betty had signed it after Sarah read it back to her that day; the signed sheet in the envelope was the earlier one, dated the nineteenth of November. Betty put the December account down nearest Jo.

“I told Sarah this afterwards,” she said. “It’s got the day I told her at the top.”

Jo read it. The two lines said Betty had been in the kitchen, near the clock and the socket; they did not say she had looked at either. When Jo reached them, she stopped moving her finger.

“Do you remember anything more now?”

“No.”

Sarah sat down. Betty could hear the kitchen clock from where she was sitting. She waited until Jo looked up from the page.

“Have you put six fourteen in yours?” Betty asked.

Jo opened another part of the visit. She took a moment to find the time.

“It’s in the record from the monitor.”

“Where does it say I was?”

Jo looked back at Betty’s account. “You’ve said you were in the kitchen.”

“I told Sarah that on the first. Does yours say how you got the minute?”

Jo brought the earlier entry up again. There was a line beneath the recorded time. She read it silently, then asked whether Betty would like her to read it aloud.

“Yes.”

Jo read the entry as it stood. It placed Betty in the kitchen at the time recorded by the monitor. Betty put a finger on the date at the top of her own account.

“I didn’t tell you that minute.”

“No,” Jo said. “It came from the reading.”

“And the reading didn’t see me.”

Jo moved her hand away from the screen. Sarah had put a cup beside her. The tea bag was still in it, with the paper tag lying on the table.

“I need to add where that time came from,” Jo said.

Betty kept her finger on the date. “And when I told Sarah where I was.”

Jo asked if she could take a photograph of the account. Betty said she could, then asked what she would write beside the earlier entry.

Jo began with the recorded time. She stopped and started again. This time she named the monitor’s record as the source of the minute, and Betty’s account of the kitchen as something given on the first of December. She added that Betty did not recall looking at the clock.

“Or the socket,” Betty said.

Jo added that too. She read the whole note back. Then she asked Betty whether she could save it.

“Let me read it.”

Jo set the screen flat on the table. Betty had to turn it a little to keep the window out of it. Sarah took the cups away from her elbow. Betty read from the top, leaving her own signed account beside the screen until she reached the date at the bottom.

“That’s what I told her,” Betty said. She pushed the screen back. Jo saved the note and asked if Betty wanted a copy. Betty took the clip off yesterday’s two pages and laid it beside the envelope, ready for the new one.
