By two on Tuesday Betty had cut the loaf down to its heel. There was one slice too thick for the toaster and one so thin Sarah could see the board through it in places. She put the thick one on Betty’s plate.

“Mine always comes out like that,” Betty said. “I know which end to start from. I forget by the time I get there.”

Anne had put the chair by the socket square with the wall. She sat in it to eat. From there she could see the plate on the unit without bending her neck.

When the phone rang, Betty answered before Sarah had got up.

“This is Nita at the depot. Martin left me a note about a test unit.”

Betty took her glasses off the table and went to stand beside Anne. “Have you got the number?”

Nita read four one seven three. Betty said yes. Then Nita read the address, with twenty-six at the beginning. Betty said yes to that too.

“I can send somebody on Thursday,” Nita said. “Would you be in between nine and eleven?”

“Can they take it out of the socket?”

“I’ve got a fitter for the round. I’d like to check one thing with you first. Does the cable go straight into the wall?”

Betty looked down. The cable went behind the leg and doubled back towards the socket. There was a small white block between its plug and the wall.

“There’s something in between,” she said.

“What does it look like?”

Betty gave the phone to Sarah and moved her chair. The block had a red light and a strip of tape with a date on it. “Twelve November,” Betty read. Sarah repeated it into the phone.

“That’s ours too,” Nita said. “The fitter’s note for four one seven three says he put a monitor on the plug on the twelfth.” “I’ll put it on the sheet. He’ll take the unit and the block. He’ll leave the socket free.”

Betty took the phone back. “Will I see that on his sheet when he gets here?”

Nita said she could send Betty a copy now, if she had somewhere to send it. Sarah gave Nita her email address. Betty asked Nita to put Betty’s name on the first line.

Nita read the job back before she released it. Thursday, nine to eleven. A fitter to disconnect and collect the unit and the block from number twenty-six. Front door. Ring once and wait.

“He’ll have that last bit?” Betty asked.

“He’ll have the same sheet I’m sending you.”

After the call Sarah’s phone made the sound it made when a message came in. She opened the attachment and set it on the table between them. Betty followed the lines with the end of her glasses. “Disconnect and collect test unit four one seven three and plug monitor,” she read. She checked the Thursday slot, the house number and the words about waiting at the front door. “It’s what she said.”

Anne had turned the envelope to the side with the old sum. She pulled it closer to the table leg so the pencil would reach.

“Thursday,” Betty said. “Nine to eleven.”

Anne wrote it in the space she’d left. Betty waited for her to finish before she asked for the pencil. “This bit’s for me,” Betty said. Beside the time she wrote *leave chair*.
