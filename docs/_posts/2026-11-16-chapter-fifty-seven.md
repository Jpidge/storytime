On Monday the seventh of December Sarah opened Mo’s reply at the table. Betty had set two mugs out. She took hers away before Sarah turned the laptop round, because the bottom was wet and she didn’t want a mark on the paper they’d left there on Friday. She stood the mug on the tea towel.

Mo had sent the beginning of the monitor log. The first line was marked *Startup*. It had a time and a space under the heading for watts.

“Is that a zero?” Betty asked.

“It’s just where it says it started,” Sarah said. “Mo says it doesn’t take a reading for that line.”

Sarah moved down one line. The time had advanced a minute. There was a zero there, and another one a minute after that.

“Which line was it on when she saw the point four?”

Sarah read the note underneath. Mo had written the two display readings by the bench clock. She hadn’t checked the monitor’s clock against the bench clock on Friday. There was nothing to tell them if the two times went together.

Betty got Friday’s sheet. She rested it against the side of the laptop so she could see the two pages from where she sat. The paper bowed and came away from the screen.

“She saw it,” Betty said. “I can see where she wrote it.”

Betty wiped the bottom of her mug on the towel. Sarah set it against the foot of the sheet to hold it in place. “Yes.”

“Can you put your finger on where the machine wrote it?”

Sarah kept her hands on her lap. “I can point to where the machine says it started.”

Betty sat with that for a while. Out in the hall the post came through the letter box. She could hear the top envelope slide off the others and land by itself.

Mo had asked at the bottom whether they needed the monitor kept on the bench. It was due to go back into the stores cupboard. Sarah read that part aloud twice because Betty asked her to.

“Will somebody else have it?”

“They lend them out from there.”

“Then ask her to leave ours on the bench for now,” Betty said. “Could the fitter write down both clocks while Mo watches the display? They can see where that goes against the first reading it keeps.”

Sarah opened the reply box. She typed *Please hold monitor 4173 for another test*. Betty asked her to read it back, then got up for the post. When she came back she moved her mug off Friday’s sheet. The paper was dry underneath. There was no mark.
