The time Ellis sent was eight forty in the morning. He had put the plate number under it and written that stores had found another monitor for the job. Betty read the message before she went downstairs. By eight forty she had made the tea and brought her own sheet out of the envelope. She put it face down on the table so she wouldn’t spend the whole time reading it.

The phone rang at nine minutes past nine. Ellis said they’d had to wait for a bench. He had the fitter with him now. There was a noise like paper being dragged across the mouth of the phone and then a woman said hello.

“I’ve got two meters here,” she said. “One between the wall and your monitor, and the other between your monitor and a lamp. The lamp’s off. If I turn it on you’ll hear the switch.”

“Can you do the bit with nothing in the socket first?”

The fitter said yes. Betty could hear her put the phone down. When she came back she said she’d taken the plug for the lamp and the second meter out of the monitor. There was nothing in its socket now. She switched the setup on at the wall.

“Four tenths on the wall meter,” the fitter said.

“And on mine?”

“It’s still starting.”

Betty turned her sheet over. Sarah came in and took the lid off the teapot. Betty put a finger on the date to keep the sheet from moving when Sarah set the lid down beside it.

“Now it says point four,” the fitter said. “Still nothing in the socket.”

She left the phone where it was. Betty could hear a pen being put down and then picked up again.

“It’s dropped to zero,” the fitter said.

“Both of them?”

“Yours has. The one at the wall still reads four tenths.”

“Will mine stay there?”

“With the socket empty it will. I’ve got yesterday’s sheet here. It took a few seconds longer to settle this time, but it did the same thing.”

Betty took her finger off the date. She asked the fitter if she would put down what the meter at the wall said after the other one had gone to zero. The fitter said she would. She asked Betty to hold on while she plugged the lamp and its meter back in. Then she turned the lamp on. There was a click.

“Six watts on yours and six on the lamp meter,” the fitter said. “Six point four at the wall.”

“If somebody had only the readings off mine,” Betty said, “would they be able to tell when you put the plug in?”

“I’ve written down when I did it.”

“I mean if they didn’t have your sheet.”

The fitter said she’d have to look at what the monitor had saved. Betty heard her ask Ellis to bring the screen round. Sarah poured the tea and put the empty pot back between them. When the fitter spoke again she had moved away from the phone. She read the first saved row aloud. It was the startup row. There was a time in it but the watts box was blank.

“That first row just says when it came on,” the fitter said. “The next one’s zero. Once it’s running it saves the socket reading. It never saves what I’m getting off the wall meter.”

“Does it say anywhere that there was nothing plugged in?” Betty asked.

“No. I’ve got that on the paper.”

“Could you send the paper with the file, then?”

Ellis came back on and said he could send them together. Betty asked him to put them with her question as well. He said he had the query open.

“Can you put the bit about the empty socket in the answer itself?” she asked. “In case whoever reads it doesn’t open the file.”

Ellis said yes. She heard him ask the fitter to say it once more. Betty left her sheet face up until he’d finished taking it down.
