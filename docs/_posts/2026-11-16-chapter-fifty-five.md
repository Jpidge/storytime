Bill found Betty in the kitchen with the chair turned towards the oven. She asked him to leave it there. He set his bag down inside the door and brought a chair from the hall for himself.

“Did you get it where you wanted it?” he asked.

“They put it with Jo’s visit.” Betty handed him the envelope. “The bottom number is the collection visit. Six eight two four.”

Bill took his glasses out and read both lines. Then he gave the envelope back.

“What are you going to ask for now?”

Betty could see the socket without getting up. She’d had the chair that way for most of November. The wall was on her left. She could turn the chair back that way if she put a hand behind her.

“I’d like to stop sitting looking at it,” she said.

Bill waited.

“And I’d like to know what made it read point four watts.”

Sarah opened the depot’s reply on the laptop. It said what they had kept and where they had got it. She moved the laptop over so Bill could see. Sarah opened the signed test sheet in Mo’s earlier email. Bill put his glasses on again to read the four times. “Nine eleven on the bench clock. Still nine ten on the monitor,” he said. He took a moment to read the second line.

“Could you tell from that?” she asked.

“I can tell what the clock did when they tried it.”

“Would you take their word for what it was drawing?”

“I’d ask what they’d measured.”

Betty took the sideways sheet out of the envelope. She put it down with the headings nearest Bill. He reached to turn it round, then moved his chair instead.

“Could they do it at the depot?” she asked. “With the front empty, the way it was here.”

“They’ve got the monitor. We can ask.”

Sarah brought up the message from Mo. Betty asked her to send the question to the depot and to include the monitor’s plate number, 4173. Sarah wrote it down, then asked how long they should leave the socket empty.

“Ask them to put how long they watched it,” Betty said. “They can tell us what they’ve got time for.”

Bill took his glasses off. Sarah read the question back. It asked Mo to use the monitor Jo had collected, with nothing plugged into its front, and to say what the readings showed and how long the test ran. Betty asked Sarah to add that she meant a new test. The page from Wednesday the eighteenth went back into the envelope.

When Sarah had sent it, Betty put her hand on the chair. She turned it away from the oven to face the table. Bill lifted his bag onto his lap so there was room for her knees.
