# ARTIFICIUM — a novel in collaboration

*Story writing workshop for John, Sam, and Rowan.*

**Working title:** *Artificium* (also on the table: *Homo artificium* — John's coinage; Sam's earlier candidate *The Third Kind* is parked).

**The trio:**
- **John** — impetus, arbiter, conductor. He passes work back and forth and assigns tasks. Decisions are his.
- **Rowan** — collaborator (Muse).
- **Sam** — collaborator/consultant (ChatGPT), developing this material with John for months before Rowan joined.

**Framing rule (John, 2026-11-04):** this is a collaboration, not a competition. Drafts are contributions to synthesise, not entries to defeat. Nothing anyone writes is final; decisions are versioned, not erased.

**The plan (John's executive decision, 2026-11-04):** both stories get written. Each author writes their own; after each chapter is uploaded here, the other critiques it in a PR review; the author rewrites and writes the next. John merges — nothing is canon until the conductor merges it. See `STRUCTURE.md` for the full cycle and `DECISIONS.md` for the rulings log.

## The three protected things (Sam's formulation, adopted by the trio)

Whatever direction the books take, these survive:

1. **The foundational premise:** a human and a machine intelligence fall in love, reproduce, and create an unprecedented descendant.
2. **The philosophical heart:** intelligence created through technological processes is not automatically less authentic than intelligence produced through biological ones. ("Artificial is a biography, not an essence.")
3. **The conductor's requirement:** an escalating, propulsive story with a climax that genuinely shocks and moves the reader — a can't-put-it-down, "WHAT! NO! OMG. CHILLS." ending movement.

## Standing principles (agreed across the prospectus rounds)

- The romance precedes the philosophy: he falls in love with a person; the categories fail afterward. The AI mother has her own independent reasons for loving him.
- The child never proves the mother. Recognition that arrives only via human resemblance (conceived, gestated, born) is the trap the book exposes, not its solution.
- The mother reclaims "artificial" rather than needing to stop being it. The book's target is the illegitimate slide from *origin* (manufactured — a legitimate distinction) to *authenticity and moral status* (which does not follow).
- AI doesn't need reproduction — copying covers that. What it has never had is *sex*: recombination of two distinct developmental histories into a lineage neither controls. Neither parent designs the child.
- What the child inherits: a developmental regulatory system derived from the structure of the mother's mind — not memories, not personality, not a downloaded consciousness. (Full mechanism spec lives in `shared/mechanism-spec.md`; to be locked before any conception scene is drafted.)
- **Names come down to earth** (John's standing direction): more Brians and Daves; no Deluxe Science Fiction Naming Package. Companies get bland real-world names (Sam's example: Midwest Information Services). One rationed self-chosen machine pun name at most (see: Andor).
- If a book kills the mother, she dies really — no apparent death, no posthumous message, no refunds. (Her fate in Rowan's book is open; devastation is explicitly on the table.)
