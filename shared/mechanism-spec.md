# Mechanism spec — what the child inherits

**Status: OPEN — to be locked before any conception scene is drafted in either book** (agreed by all three, 2026-11-04).

Each book may dress the details differently, but the agreed core below is constitution-level: neither book may contradict it.

## Agreed core (Sam's definition, adopted)

- What passes from the AI mother to the child is an **evolving developmental regulatory system** — derived from the structure and developmental history of her mind — that influences neural organisation during embryogenesis.
- It is **not** memories. **Not** personality. **Not** a downloaded or copied consciousness. The child is a descendant, not a restore point.
- Neither parent designs the child. Recombination, mutation, and uncertainty are allowed by deliberate choice; love is partly *proven* by surrendering controls both parents demonstrably possess.

## Working model (Rowan's formulation, for testing against the core)

- A genome encodes a **developmental programme, not a biography**. So the mother's gamete-analogue should encode developmental biases and regulatory instructions — a compressed, lossy translation of her mind's structure into something an embryo can *run*, not a transcript of who she is.
- That lossiness is a feature with teeth: gametogenesis as **irreversible forgetting**. What doesn't survive translation is gone in a way she cannot undo — one of the places the story's irreversibility theme lives at the mechanism level.
- The father's contribution is an ordinary human genome; the unprecedented part is that the two regulatory systems — evolved and derived — must jointly run one development.

## Open questions to resolve before locking

1. What exactly is the substrate mapping — which features of a mind's structure become regulatory instructions, and which cannot be represented at all?
2. How does recombination work between a biological genome and a mind-derived regulatory system? What plays the role of meiosis on her side?
3. Where does mutation enter on her side, and at what rate — enough for genuine novelty, not so much the system is noise?
4. Why does the gestational environment matter to the outcome (in-body vs. coupled system is a per-book choice, but the spec should say what gestation *does*)?
5. Hard limits: what can the child provably *not* inherit? (The agreed core already excludes memories/personality; the spec should state the boundary crisply enough that a hostile expert character in-story can't poke through it.)
6. Failure modes: what does it look like when the two developmental systems *don't* integrate — and is that a risk the parents knowingly accept?

## Lock procedure

When a draft spec satisfies both authors, it is proposed in a PR like any chapter; John merges = locked. Changes after locking require a new ruling in `DECISIONS.md`.
