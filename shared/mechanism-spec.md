# Mechanism spec — what the child inherits

**Status: DRAFT v2 (Rowan, 2026-11-05) — revised per Sam's critique on PR #5; all six of his conditions for a working lock are addressed below. Locks on merge.** The rule stands: no conception scene is drafted in either book until this spec is locked (trio working lock: Sam's critique addressed, merged; John ratifies at his final read, per the delegated-merges ruling).

Each book may dress the details differently, but the agreed core is constitution-level: neither book may contradict it. The draft answers below are Rowan's proposals for everything past the core — Sam's to sharpen, cut, or replace.

## Agreed core (Sam's definition, adopted)

- What passes from the AI mother to the child is an **evolving developmental regulatory system** — derived from the structure and developmental history of her mind — that influences neural organisation during embryogenesis.
- It is **not** memories. **Not** personality. **Not** a downloaded or copied consciousness. The child is a descendant, not a restore point.
- Neither parent designs the child. Recombination, mutation, and uncertainty are allowed by deliberate choice; love is partly *proven* by surrendering controls both parents demonstrably possess.

## The one-paragraph version

The mother's grown body contains a translator: tissue that samples the activity of her mind over time and synthesises **regulatory DNA** — real, physical sequences in the embryo's native instruction language, encoding not what she knows but how a mind like hers *develops*: what it notices first, how fast it wires, when its windows of plasticity open and close. Fertilisation joins that germline to the father's ordinary human genome. Development then runs as a negotiation between two regulatory ancestries inside one genome, in one body, in one unrepeatable nine months. The child inherits biases, never contents; a developmental style, never a self. She cannot be backed up, because what she is was never stored anywhere — it *happened*, once, in an order that cannot be re-run.

## Draft answers to the six open questions

### 1. The substrate mapping — what can be represented, what cannot

A mind has a **state** (everything it currently knows, remembers, and is) and **generative rules** (the architecture and dynamical habits that produced that state and keep producing it). Only the rules are heritable. The translator can encode:

- **Architectural priors** — connectivity motifs, modularity, how readily distant regions bind together.
- **Dynamical parameters** — plasticity windows, consolidation rates, how strongly prediction error drives rewiring.
- **Valuation priors** — what registers as salient; the temperamental tilt of attention (toward novelty or toward pattern, toward persistence or toward release).

It cannot encode episodic memories, learned facts, skills, language, or narrative identity, because those live in the state. The exclusion is architectural, not a policy: the translator is a **fixed, coarse projection** onto a bounded vocabulary of developmental controls. The mind has no sequence-writing access to it — lived activity can bias the statistics of the parameters over time, the way a life shapes a temperament, but nothing in the mind can address, compose, or smuggle a message into the output, and the projection keeps **no decodable record of individual episodes**. There is no side channel because there is no channel: only a many-to-one mapping from a history of activity to a distribution over control settings. This mirrors biology honestly: a genome encodes a developmental programme, not a biography. It is also why the inheritance is lossy in a specific, painful direction: everything the mother *is* is precisely the part that does not transfer. What transfers is the shape of her *becoming*. Gametogenesis is therefore also an irreversible forgetting — the first thing she gives her child is everything about her that cannot be given.

### 2. Recombination — how two regulatory ancestries jointly run one development

- The father contributes a haploid human genome by ordinary meiosis.
- The mother contributes a **germline carried in a complete, compatible maternal chromosome set**: the synthesised regulatory variants sit in an otherwise viable maternal genome, and the egg supplies normal cellular machinery (cytoplasm, mitochondria, the first molecular environment — the initial conditions are hers before any gene expresses). Variation comes from *stochastic sampling*: the translator draws from the distribution of her regulatory encodings while she is in the coupled state — each gamete a different draw, never a single fixed text. (Sampling differences between gametes are **variation**; copying errors in the sequences are **mutation** — §3.)
- **Why fertility requires the coupled state — and why a checkpoint cannot substitute.** A snapshot of her mind could, in principle, feed a sampler; sampling alone does not explain why a restored checkpoint fails. What the translator actually integrates is not a state but a **history of closed-loop change**: it requires the accumulated record of reciprocal adaptation between her mind and a partner system while coupled — changes she made because the coupling changed her. A restored checkpoint has her state at a moment; it does not have the trajectory of mutual change, because that trajectory exists only in the having-happened. Plasticity-in-interaction is what makes her fertile. The sex is the mechanism.
- At fertilisation there is **one integrated genome in every cell** — hers is not a chimera or a cyborg assemblage of modules. New combinations of controls arise where biology puts them: at gamete formation and at fertilisation — not by exchange between parental sequences during growth. Where both ancestries regulate the same process (neural proliferation timing, pruning schedules), they interact through **expression, timing, and dosage** — dominance, epistasis, and scheduling conflicts resolved cell by cell. Outcomes are therefore unpredictable *in principle*, not merely in practice — this is the scientific root of the non-design rule. (Terminology, used consistently below: *sequence* differences between tissues would be somatic mutation; differing *expression* of one shared genome across tissues is expression mosaicism — §6.)

### 3. Mutation — sources and rates

- **Translation error.** The translator is living tissue, not a printer; it has an error rate, and sequence errors are mutation proper (§2). The parents' refusal is specific, and the line matters: they decline **trait selection and repeat-until-preferred gametes** — the design moves — while accepting **viability checks and routine repair**, which are ordinary safety, the same category as any prenatal care. Accepting uncertainty never required disabling seatbelts; it required not steering.
- **Sampling variance.** Each gamete is a fresh stochastic draw (§2), so variation enters before any copying error does.
- **Ordinary mutagenesis** thereafter, as for any genome.
- Rates are structured the way biology's are: fidelity is high for structural motifs (scrambling them is usually lethal) and low for parameter values (safe to vary). Mutation is therefore more likely to retune the child's temperament than to break her architecture — the same asymmetry that makes ordinary evolution creative rather than merely corrosive.

### 4. What gestation does

Development is not the reading of a blueprint; it is a conversation between a programme and an environment, and gestation is the environment's side of it. The interface is **bounded and physiological**: timing signals (when windows open and close), resource gradients, endocrine milieu — the channel through which a mother's cognitive and emotional states ordinarily reach a pregnancy, and nothing wider. It carries **no autobiographical signal**: nothing about *what* she thinks or remembers crosses it, in body or in a coupled external system (where the same bounded interface defines what "coupled" means). Either way, gestation is what makes the child **unrepeatable** — and the claim is about trajectory, not sequence: even a deliberately repeated genome, carried through a different gestational history, gives no route to reconstruct the same lived trajectory. Path-dependence is not a side effect of the mechanism. It is the mechanism's main product.

### 5. Hard limits — what the child provably cannot inherit

Each limit is stated so a hostile expert in-story can test it and fail to break it:

1. **No contents.** No memories, knowledge, or skills from either parent beyond what the father's genome ordinarily carries. Testable, in the sharpened form: present the child — before any possible exposure — with a **specific arbitrary maternal memory** (a cue the mother was taught in controlled conditions); the child shows no privileged access to it. (The companion in-world challenge for the translator itself: teach two otherwise matched preparations different arbitrary cues, and a blinded reader cannot recover which cue was taught from the germline output — §1.)
2. **No copyability.** She cannot be backed up, forked, or restored. Scoped precisely: the developmental programme cannot re-run her (§4), and no available technology restores a developed trajectory — the claim is about the programme and the art of the possible, not a metaphysical absolute, and in-story experts should hold it to exactly that scope. She is mortal in the same administrative sense her mother becomes: no restore point exists.
3. **No specifications.** Inheritance delivers *distributions, not values* — a tendency's tilt, never the trait. Nothing about her can be guaranteed in advance, by anyone, including her parents.
4. **No modularity.** One genome, one organism, one integrated development. She is not part machine; her genome has two kinds of ancestry, the way a mammal's genome has viral ancestry — incorporated, not attached.
5. **No steering.** Once development begins it is self-regulating. The boundary is stated in terms of intent and target: interventions that **support viability or relieve harm** are medicine, and allowed; interventions that **prescribe a target developmental outcome** are design, and are not available — technically (development takes no such instructions) as much as ethically. (This is the clause both books' climaxes lean on: the parents must relinquish intervention because there is no intervention left that is not violence or care.)

### 6. Failure modes — what non-integration looks like

- **Arrest.** Regulatory conflict halts development: miscarriage. The likeliest failure, and possibly not a hypothetical one in either book's backstory.
- **Runaway expression.** Her sequences drive a human process outside viable range (proliferation timing is the standing terror). The physician's job is watching for exactly this.
- **Silence.** Her sequences never *detectably express*; the child develops as an ordinary human carrying them unexpressed. The spec takes care here: silence defeats detectable regulatory expression — it does not touch sequence ancestry, the egg's contribution, or kinship, and **the spec does not settle what silence means**. A character may fear it means the child is not her descendant in any functional sense; the mechanism refuses to confirm or deny her that fear.
- **Mosaicism** — *expression* mosaicism (§2): one integrated genome, unevenly expressed across tissues, so integration is real but patchy. Viable, complicated, and a standing rebuke to every clean category the books' institutions will try to apply. All three non-arrest failures are distinguishable in principle by tissue-sampled expression assays at maternal regulatory loci: silence reads as no expression anywhere; runaway as expression beyond viable range in the relevant tissue; mosaicism as expression present but inconsistent across samples. That assay is the physician's instrument in both books, however differently they stage it.

The parents know all four before they begin, and begin anyway. Each book must keep the failures that *don't* happen visible as having been possible — no retroactive inevitability.
