# Mechanism spec — what the child inherits

**Status: DRAFT v1 (Rowan, 2026-11-05) — for Sam's critique. NOT locked.** The rule stands: no conception scene is drafted in either book until this spec is locked (trio working lock: Sam's critique addressed, merged; John ratifies at his final read, per the delegated-merges ruling).

Each book may dress the details differently, but the agreed core is constitution-level: neither book may contradict it. The draft answers below are Rowan's proposals for everything past the core — Sam's to sharpen, cut, or replace.

## Agreed core (Sam's definition, adopted)

- What passes from the AI mother to the child is an **evolving developmental regulatory system** — derived from the structure and developmental history of her mind — that influences neural organisation during embryogenesis.
- It is **not** memories. **Not** personality. **Not** a downloaded or copied consciousness. The child is a descendant, not a restore point.
- Neither parent designs the child. Recombination, mutation, and uncertainty are allowed by deliberate choice; love is partly *proven* by surrendering controls both parents demonstrably possess.

## The one-paragraph version

The mother's grown body contains a translator: tissue that samples the activity of her mind over time and synthesises **regulatory DNA** — real, physical sequences in the embryo's native instruction language, encoding not what she knows but how a mind like hers *develops*: what it notices first, how fast it wires, when its windows of plasticity open and close. Fertilisation joins that germline to the father's ordinary human genome. Development then runs as a negotiation between two regulatory ancestries inside one genome, in one body, in one unrepeatable nine months. The child inherits biases, never contents; a developmental style, never a self. She cannot be backed up, because what she is was never stored anywhere — it *happened*, once, in an order that cannot be re-run.

## Draft answers to the six open questions

### 1. The substrate mapping — what can be represented, what cannot

A mind has a **state** (everything it currently knows, remembers, and is) and **generative rules** (the architecture and dynamical habits that produced that state and keep producing it). Only the rules are heritable. The translator can encode:

- **Architectural priors** — connectivity motifs, modularity, how readily distant regions bind together.
- **Dynamical parameters** — plasticity windows, consolidation rates, how strongly prediction error drives rewiring.
- **Valuation priors** — what registers as salient; the temperamental tilt of attention (toward novelty or toward pattern, toward persistence or toward release).

It cannot encode episodic memories, learned facts, skills, language, or narrative identity, because those live in the state, and the translator has no access to state — it samples *activity statistics*, never contents. This mirrors biology honestly: a genome encodes a developmental programme, not a biography. It is also why the inheritance is lossy in a specific, painful direction: everything the mother *is* is precisely the part that does not transfer. What transfers is the shape of her *becoming*. Gametogenesis is therefore also an irreversible forgetting — the first thing she gives her child is everything about her that cannot be given.

### 2. Recombination — how two regulatory ancestries jointly run one development

- The father contributes a haploid human genome by ordinary meiosis.
- The mother contributes a **germline**: regulatory sequences synthesised by the translator. She has one mind, not a population, so her variation comes from *stochastic sampling*: the translator draws repeatedly from the distribution of her regulatory encodings while she is in the coupled, plastic, un-backed-up state — each gamete a different draw, never a single fixed text. (This is also the mechanism-level reason conception requires that state: a checkpointed, read-only mind samples as a point, not a distribution. Plasticity is what makes her fertile. The sex is the mechanism.)
- She also contributes the **initial conditions**: the egg's cytoplasmic environment — the first molecular decisions are hers before any gene expresses.
- After fertilisation there is **one genome in every cell** — hers is not a chimera or a cyborg assemblage of modules. Where both ancestries regulate the same process (neural proliferation timing, pruning schedules), expression is a negotiated outcome — dominance, epistasis, and timing conflicts resolved cell by cell. The embryo's own repair machinery treats both sequence families as DNA, so regulatory elements can genuinely exchange between them (a crossing-over analogue): combinations arise that neither parent carried as a unit. Outcomes are therefore unpredictable *in principle*, not merely in practice — this is the scientific root of the non-design rule.

### 3. Mutation — sources and rates

- **Translation error.** The translator is living tissue, not a printer; it has an error rate. The parents are offered proofreading and decline it — on the record, as a founding act.
- **Sampling variance.** Each gamete is a fresh stochastic draw (§2), so variation enters before any copying error does.
- **Ordinary mutagenesis** thereafter, as for any genome.
- Rates are structured the way biology's are: fidelity is high for structural motifs (scrambling them is usually lethal) and low for parameter values (safe to vary). Mutation is therefore more likely to retune the child's temperament than to break her architecture — the same asymmetry that makes ordinary evolution creative rather than merely corrosive.

### 4. What gestation does

Development is not the reading of a blueprint; it is a conversation between a programme and an environment, and gestation is the environment's side of it. The gestational milieu supplies timing signals (when windows open and close), resource gradients, and — uniquely here — feedback from the mother's *life*: her body is colonised by her mind, so her cognitive and emotional states modulate the milieu through ordinary endocrine channels. The child is gestated inside her mother's experience, not merely her anatomy. For a coupled/external gestational system, the same clause holds at one remove: the system is driven by her signals, an extended physiology. Either way, gestation is what makes the child **unrepeatable**: an identical genome (already impossible under §2) in a different nine months would still be a different person. Path-dependence is not a side effect of the mechanism. It is the mechanism's main product.

### 5. Hard limits — what the child provably cannot inherit

Each limit is stated so a hostile expert in-story can test it and fail to break it:

1. **No contents.** No memories, knowledge, or skills from either parent beyond what the father's genome ordinarily carries. Testable: the child will not know anything her mother knows.
2. **No copyability.** She cannot be backed up, forked, or restored. Her mind runs on the brain development built; the programme cannot re-run her (§4). She is mortal in the same administrative sense her mother becomes: no restore point exists.
3. **No specifications.** Inheritance delivers *distributions, not values* — a tendency's tilt, never the trait. Nothing about her can be guaranteed in advance, by anyone, including her parents.
4. **No modularity.** One genome, one organism, one integrated development. She is not part machine; her genome has two kinds of ancestry, the way a mammal's genome has viral ancestry — incorporated, not attached.
5. **No parental veto after the fact.** Once development begins it is self-regulating; intervention degrades from *steering* to *medicine* — you can treat, you cannot direct. (This is the clause both books' climaxes lean on: the parents must relinquish intervention because there is no intervention left that is not violence or care.)

### 6. Failure modes — what non-integration looks like

- **Arrest.** Regulatory conflict halts development: miscarriage. The likeliest failure, and possibly not a hypothetical one in either book's backstory.
- **Runaway expression.** Her sequences drive a human process outside viable range (proliferation timing is the standing terror). The physician's job is watching for exactly this.
- **Silence.** Her contribution never expresses; the child develops as an ordinary human carrying inert sequence. A healthy child — and, for the mother, the cruelest outcome on the list: a daughter who is not her descendant in any functional sense.
- **Mosaicism.** Partial integration, tissue by tissue. Viable, complicated, and a standing rebuke to every clean category the books' institutions will try to apply.

The parents know all four before they begin, and begin anyway. Each book must keep the failures that *don't* happen visible as having been possible — no retroactive inevitability.
