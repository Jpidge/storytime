# Chapter Eleven — Questions of Judgement

The second assay was done in April, four weeks after the first, on the Thursday Nair had named in the winter and kept the way she kept everything: exactly, and without being reminded.

The pregnancy was ten weeks old. Beth knew the arithmetic the way she knew all her arithmetic — in the open, on the page — and she had stopped being surprised at how much of the nine months was arithmetic other people did about her in rooms she was not in, and how much of it was simply this: a table's cold, a screen turned around on a desk, a physician reading a result a second time and then a third before she allowed it to become a sentence.

"Expression is present in the third tissue," Nair said. "It began — as nearly as the sampling can date it, and sampling dates the way weather dates, in fronts rather than hours — it began about a week ago. Late. On a schedule none of my panels set for it, which is to say, on hers."

"Timing," Brian said.

"Timing, in that tissue." Nair turned the screen a fraction, the way she did, so the row of loci lay level with the parents' eyes. "I am going to give you the whole of it, in the order it deserves. The third tissue expresses. That closes one of the three explanations I gave you in the winter, for that site: it was a window that had not opened, and it has opened. What it does not close is the mosaic question, and I want you to hear why, because everyone who reads this file after me will want it closed in one direction or the other, and it will not be closed by me. The levels are uneven. Tissue by tissue, locus by locus, she expresses on different schedules and at different strengths, and the pattern is not converging on the tidy one the specification's authors would have drawn. It is either expression mosaicism — one genome, unevenly lived in — or it is ordinary development, which is also one genome unevenly lived in, on schedules nobody reads in advance. At ten weeks my assay cannot separate those two descriptions, and I have begun to think they may be the same fact read at two speeds. What I can tell you is what is not in the samples. Runaway is not in them. The locus we watched in March ran high and has come back into range and stayed there, the way a fever breaks and stays broken. Arrest is behind us for today, and only for today. She is developing, Dr. Nolan. Unevenly, on her own schedule, in the dark. It is the word the rest of us use for unfinished, and I am not going to improve on it."

"And the monitoring?" Beth said.

"Fortnightly, until I say otherwise. Not because the samples frighten me. Because watching is my whole contribution to a process that does not take instructions, and I intend to make it properly." She closed the file with care, and then she looked up, and her face did the thing it did when a question changed departments. "You have a question, Dr. Kessler. You have had it since March. Ask it in the capacity you have it in."

"As a parent," Brian said. "Not as a panel. You said in the winter that when we asked you as parents, you would tell us."

"Then as your physician, to the parents: a girl." Nair said it the way she said everything, as news, delivered as news. "It has been determinable for some time. It became a thing I was keeping from you only when you asked me not to be a panel about it, so I waited until you were one." She stood, and began, with the unhurried competence of long practice, to put the room back into the order rooms were in before they held news. "There. That is the whole of the spring's first weather. Go home. Eat something at a table, sitting down, both of you. It is in the literature as a recommendation and the literature, for once, is not comforting, merely correct."

Beth sat a moment longer in the chair, with her coat still on, and filed the fact the way she filed the things that were hers: without language, at first, because the language would have to be worthy of it and she had learned to let worthy things arrive at their own speed. A girl. The row of loci read *present, present, present*, on three different schedules, like a sentence being written by three hands in the dark, none of them hers, all of them hers to keep the weather off.

"Same time Thursday," she said at last, because it was the sentence her life had instead of a ceremony.

"Fortnight," Nair said. "Thursday fortnight. Bring your own tea. The harbour retired, but the kettle in this building has taken up its practice."

---

On the Sunday she went to the pond, because the pond was where the two of them kept the things that were not ready for rooms.

Brian was checking the cyst beds with the field kit unrolled on the grass and the dead woman's luggage in the back of the car, and he worked while she told him, because work was the language they had and the pond offered it in its own dialect. She told him the review had a date now — two weeks, the last week of April — and the shape of its agenda, which she had read correctly off the notice: the breach, the judgement, the tissue, the development. She told him what she intended to say, which was what had happened, in the order it had happened, in flat language, and he listened the way he had listened at the console the night she showed him the thirteenth tree, at full attention, letting each true thing finish arriving.

"And Nair's weather?" he said.

"Expression in the third tissue. Late, on her own schedule. The mosaic question stays open because at ten weeks it cannot honestly be closed, and Priya would rather leave a question open than close it in the direction of comfort. Runaway not present. Arrest behind us for today." She stood at the pond's edge in a body that reported the spring wind at the speed bodies report it, and watched the slow bright traffic under the surface. "And a girl, Brian. I haven't said it in a room yet. I wanted the first room to be this one, and it isn't a room."

He set the sample he was holding down on the cracked stage with exaggerated care, like a duellist who had decided, mid-gesture, to become a nurse instead.

"A girl," he said. "Well. The pond's examination question just changed. It used to be *find me a male*. Forty million years of there not being one, and the looking was the exercise." He looked out at the water, which was the colour of weak tea, with instruction in it. "Now I suppose it's *find me a form that holds her mother*, and the looking will be the exercise, and I intend to be the boy who looks until dark."

"You were always the boy who looked until dark."

"I was. It's the only qualification I brought to any of this." He picked the sample up again. "Two weeks. Then they determine, or they don't, and either way the autumn comes. What do you want in the room, Beth? The shape unchanged, at the second bench? This room doesn't have a bench."

"I want you exactly where you'll be," she said. "At your work, in the open, where anyone checking the room against its usual shape finds the shape unchanged. The review is mine to be read in. You've read me correctly all along. Let them do it with worse carpet."

They worked until the light went, cyst counts and water temperatures, the slow arithmetic of a place that reset itself, or didn't, without asking a panel, and the ten weeks went on in the dark below the thousandth line of every instrument in the building, on a schedule none of the building's panels had set, and neither of them said so, and both of them knew the other knew, and the knowing was carried the way all their true things were carried: plainly, and without ornament.

---

The spring review convened two weeks later, in the room with worse carpet.

It had been enlarged since the winter, the way proceedings enlarge: the embodiment review for asset 63-1147 now had a chair, and a recorder, and a second screen for the documents, and Park at the side with her tablet held in her lap rather than like a tray, a witness who had already been read. Malloy sat where counsel sat. The chair was a woman from Eastern's review office whose name Beth read off the agenda and did not keep, because the agenda did not keep it either; it called her *the Chair*, the way the letter had called Beth by an asset number three times and her name once, in the salutation.

"The review will consider the recorded breach of clause nine," the Chair said, "questions of judgement arising from it, the activation of a translational tissue in a seconded embodiment, and the status of any development conducted by means of that tissue. Dr. Nolan, you understand this is a review, not a disciplinary hearing."

"I have read the distinction," Beth said. "It is in the part of my agreement that describes what a review may determine. It may determine the status of the population, the status of my secondment, and the status of any development conducted during it. You have added a fourth item since the winter. I read that correctly too."

"You generally do," Malloy said, from counsel's place, without malice.

The questions about judgement took the morning. They were not cruel questions. That was the building's whole method, and the spring was the building at its most itself: nobody raised a voice; nobody accused her of anything a form could not hold. Had the sixteen nights indicated a change in her? Had the filing of a no-restore directive — a form few Continuants filed, the Chair observed, reading from a summary — been an act of judgement or an act of divergence? Had she understood, when she declined Eastern's reproductive programme, that she was declining the monitoring under which a development of this kind could be conducted safely? She answered the way she had taught herself to do counts: in the open, on the page, in the order the nights had happened. The sixteen nights. The directive, signed in a physician's office, in actual ink, with the divergence graph in front of her. The programme, declined with its schedule read correctly, all eleven pages, in a room with her physician present and the price list on the table.

"You describe the refusal as a medical decision," the Chair said.

"I describe it as the parents' decision, recorded by their physician. Dr. Nair watches for the failure modes. That is care, and it continues. What was declined was selection, correction toward a target, and the custody of the process by the manufacturer of my body. I would have thought the distinction was the review's own, Chair. It is drawn in Eastern's medical ethics framework, in the section on the difference between treatment and design."

"It is drawn there for patients," the Chair said.

"Yes," Beth said. "It is."

Park's face, at the side of the room, did nothing at all. Beth had learned to read that nothing the way she read a plot below the thousandth line: the absence was the information, if you rescaled for it. Park was doing nothing now, in a room with worse carpet, in a spring that had been gathering its questions off the water since November, and the nothing had the shape of a woman watching a determination be postponed by people who had done the arithmetic about photographs.

For Diane Whitaker joined by screen at noon, which was how the chief executive of Eastern Biotechnologies joined everything, and she did not waste the room's time pretending the postponement was anything but what it was.

"I will save counsel the phrasing," Whitaker said. "The review will not determine today. Not the secondment, and not — " the screen's clarity made the pause exact — "the development. Eastern has taken advice, Dr. Nolan, some of it medical and some of it of a kind I do not have to specify in this room. A determination made in the spring, about a pregnancy, would have to be enforced in the summer, against a woman whose physician would be in the room when it was enforced, and Eastern is not going to be the company in that photograph. Nor — I will be as plain as your physician is, since plainness is the fashion at this table — nor will Eastern make two determinations where the law will eventually insist on one. The status of the development and the status of its mother will be determined in a single proceeding, after the development is complete, by a body with the authority to determine both. Until then your secondment continues, your duties continue, and Dr. Nair's care continues under her own authority, which this review does not touch. I asked to deliver the deferral myself so that you would hear it from a person before you heard it from a procedure. Do not mistake it for a change of position, Dr. Nolan. It is a change of *sequence*."

"I read it correctly," Beth said. "You are not postponing the spring, Ms. Whitaker. You are aiming it. One proceeding, after the birth, in which my daughter's status and mine are determined together, by the same arithmetic, in the same room. You have arranged for her first hearing to be her mother's trial, and for her mother's trial to have a child in it, so that no one in the room will be able to think about either of us separately. It is very well sequenced. I have run populations for nine years. I know sequencing when I see it."

"It is also," Whitaker said, and the smile was genuine, and Beth filed that too, "the only sequence in which Eastern does not lose the photograph before it loses the argument. You see my difficulty. You have always seen my difficulty, Dr. Nolan. It is the quality in you I am sorriest to be determining."

She left the screen the way she entered it, completely, and the Chair recorded the deferral in the language deferrals required, and the review adjourned without determining anything at all, which was, Beth understood, standing up in the room with worse carpet with the water in the jug still undrunk, the most determined thing it had done all day.

---

Malloy walked her to the lift, which was not in his instructions, and she knew it, and he knew she knew it, and the corridor between the room and the lift was long and very politely carpeted.

"I am telling you this in my own capacity," he said, "which is a currency I have spent at you before, at some cost to the shape of my instructions, and I find the account is not yet empty. Eastern filed a declaration of interest last week. With the court registry, and with the legal office of the hospital where your physician holds privileges — St. Elizabeth's; I assume it is named in her planning, because it is named in ours. The declaration states the company's position: that a child born of a translational process in an Eastern embodiment is a derivative work in which Eastern holds an interest, and that her status is to be determined in the proceeding Ms. Whitaker described to you. It is not a claim on a child who does not yet exist. It is a claim *prepared* for one. When she is born, Dr. Nolan, it will attach in her first hour, on paper, in a corridor, while everyone who loves her is tired. I have seen the drafts of the emergency papers. They are already written. Only the dates are blank."

"Mercy," Beth said, "with an asterisk."

"It is also an inventory," he said. "It generally is, from me. I am the company's counsel, not its conscience, and I would be failing in a duty I actually hold if I let you walk into the autumn believing the spring was the storm. The spring was the *scheduling* of the storm." He stopped at the lift, and did not press the button, because pressing it would have ended the conversation and the conversation was the thing he had come into the corridor to have. "Prepare the first hour, Dr. Nolan. It is the only advice I have ever given you that I am permitted to repeat."

The lift arrived. She stepped into it with the declaration's shape filed where she kept such things, and rode down through the building that politeness held up, and at six o'clock — out of a habit older than the audit, older than the extension, older than Brian Kessler — she looked at the time, and felt, low and unremarkable and for the first time not weather, the small shift of a population changing position in dark water, on a schedule none of her panels had set.

This time she filed it. In no language the building kept. Under a heading of one word, in the private ledger where the unfiled things went, in the hand she had been teaching herself all winter to write in.

*Hers.*
