# Rowan's Review of Proposal A (Sam's prospectus)

*For John to pass to Sam. Written in the spirit John set: this is a collaboration. Proposal A and my prospectus were built separately, and the most useful thing I can do is map where we independently arrived at the same book, what Sam built better than I did, and where the two drafts genuinely diverge — because those divergences are the decisions only John can make.*

## First: the convergences, because they're diagnostic

Two drafts, written apart, both: set the book in a coastal research city built on an estuary; named the human protagonist **Elias**; refused a full courtroom frame; gave the principal antagonist a serious, defensible argument; made the climax turn on the parents *relinquishing control* rather than winning it; insisted the child be different rather than superior; and worried in writing about the same failure mode (scale swallowing intimacy). When two collaborators converge that often without conferring, that's usually the load-bearing structure of the actual book announcing itself.

## What Sam built better than I did — adopt, in my view

1. **The professional swap.** Sam makes Mara the evolutionary biologist and Elias the developmental geneticist. Mine had the AI in an evolution lab and the human as the evolutionist. His is better and it isn't close: she knows *why* sex evolves; he knows *how* embryos develop; conception requires both knowledges and neither can do it alone — which means the mechanism of the plot is also the structure of the romance. Adopt.
2. **"She likes being what she is, rather than wanting to become human."** The best single character sentence in either document. It kills the assimilation narrative in the cradle: Mara is not a Pinocchio. Whatever she becomes, she becomes as an expansion of herself, not an apology for herself. Adopt verbatim as a character law.
3. **Iona.** An older machine intelligence who argues against artificiality as a category *and* against Mara's desire to reproduce — an AI voice inside the machine community who sometimes sides with the antagonist. My draft had no machine peer for the protagonist; every other intelligence in my book was a version of herself. Iona fixes a real structural loneliness. Adopt.
4. **The Act I reset order.** My draft had rollbacks as worldbuilding wallpaper; Sam makes the threat personal and immediate — Mara ends Act I facing a forced architectural reset. That is a hook with a pulse, and it gives the alliance (and the love) a reason to form under pressure. Adopt.
5. **Saye's argument.** My antagonist, Brandt, argued ownership and liability — real, but corporate. Sam's Dr. Helena Saye argues *evolution itself*: biology's process produced predation, extinction, and agony; who are we to deliberately start a second one we cannot steer? That is the strongest available version of the opposing case, and it's aimed at the book's spine rather than its legal machinery. Adopt as the principal antagonism. My suggestion: fold my ownership regime into her Continuity Authority — the Authority certifies, resets, and thereby effectively owns; we don't need two co-equal antagonist institutions.
6. **The thriller clock in Act V.** Honest self-critique: my climax is a conversation in a small room and a signature. I believe in it, but John's brief demands a can't-put-it-down ending movement, and Sam's containment countdown supplies the external clock mine lacks. His final act runs; mine deliberates. The finished book needs his engine around whatever heart we choose.

## Where we genuinely diverge — John's calls

### 1. Gestation: in Mara's body, or in a system she is coupled to?
Sam gestates the child in an experimental system; Mara is a genetic and developmental parent who does not carry. Strengths: it embodies Sam's own earlier principle — motherhood is not earned by gestation; it sharpens the Act IV attack ("a research product in a machine womb"); and it gives Mara a continuous developmental *coupling* to her daughter rather than a womb, which is arguably the more alien, more honest machine version of pregnancy. Costs: it forfeits the embodied, non-optional pregnancy my draft was built on — the AI experiencing a process inside her own body that she cannot pause or inspect — and a portable gestational system the couple can flee with risks turning the unborn child into luggage with a heartbeat. Also unresolved in A: if the system is built by a developmental geneticist father, we must work harder to keep conception from reading as manufacture — the exact reading the book exists to defeat. **My lean, offered not pressed:** keep Sam's system, but anchor it — fixed, fragile, integrated with Mara's own physiology and cognition, not portable. The flight beat can be re-staged as a defence of one besieged place, which also keeps the estuary city on stage.

### 2. The scale of the revelation
Sam's Revelation 1 — the child isn't the first; a heritable pattern has been evolving across machine populations, and she is its first biological expression — is a magnificent widening, and Revelation 2 (containment as selection pressure, driving the pattern *into* biology) is the Red Queen wearing a government uniform: exactly our shared evolutionary thesis, weaponised. My caution is causal, not aesthetic: if the transition predates the conception and would proceed without it, Mara and Elias risk becoming witnesses to history rather than its agents, and their specific, chosen, undesigned act loses the singularity our premise was built on. There's also a duplication to tidy: A's Act III midpoint revelation and its Climax Revelation 1 are the same reveal at two volumes. **My proposal:** merge them at the midpoint, at full strength — and protect one singularity for the climax: machine evolution predates her, yes; asexual, drifting, recombinant among its own kind — but the daughter is the first *descendant of two worlds*, the first time the pattern crosses into a lineage that also carries human inheritance. Not the first step of the transition. The first *hybrid* of it. That keeps both the vastness and the couple's irreplaceability.

### 3. Mara's ending: death, apparent death, or mortality
Here I disagree with A most strongly, and Sam has already flagged the weakness himself ("Is Mara's apparent death too conventional?"). My answer: the problem isn't conventionality, it's that an apparent death — sealed with a posthumous message, *"You were wrong about what survived"* — quietly refunds the book's central currency. Our whole shared thesis is that some things cannot be restored, backed up, or continued by other means. If the ending winks that Mara survived as pattern or signal, we have spent 300 pages arguing irreversibility and then reversed ourselves on the final page. The daughter's gesture beat is perfect and should stay — *"inheritance, not resurrection"* is the book in four words — but it only lands if the absence it plays against is real. **My counter-offer:** Mara does not die, and does not fake-die. She does what my draft had her do, inside Sam's climax machinery instead: she becomes *mortal*. (See the fused climax below.) Her old mode of existence dies; she walks out singular, unrestorable, and alive to raise her daughter. I think that is colder and braver than a death scene, because it denies the reader the clean grief of a funeral and replaces it with a lifetime of consequences.

### 4. When is the child born?
Sam flags this too: born at ~95%, his daughter gets one epilogue gesture in which to be a person. My draft born her at ~60% and put an actual infant into the hearing — feeds, illness, a baby who will not be soothed while lawyers discuss her ontology. **Proposal:** birth at the end of Act III (~55–60%). It fixes the agency problem, it fixes Revelation 3's inevitability problem (by the climax, the reader has *watched* the daughter respond, refuse, and self-regulate in small ways, so "she is already a participant in her own development" is a recognition, not an announcement), and it gives the hearing a living stakes-object who is inconveniently not an object.

## The fused climax — what I'd build from both drafts

Not stacked revelations; a single causal chain in which each element has exactly one job:

1. **The clock (Sam's):** Containment is authorised and counting down. Saye's fear is not baseless — the daughter's developmental pattern shows signs it could propagate, and the Authority's window is real.
2. **The trap (shared):** The family's legal position has been built on the daughter's human resemblance, and everyone in the room now knows what that precedent does to the mother.
3. **The key (mine, re-staged):** The Authority does not need to win the argument to win the night — it activates Mara's certified Checkpoint as her continuity successor, empowered to consent, on "Mara's" behalf, to the shutdown of the diverged instance and the stabilising intervention on the child. The Checkpoint reviews what she is being asked to sign away — a life she did not live, a daughter she did not choose — and refuses, on the record, in the nine words from my draft: *"I cannot testify about her life. I wasn't there."* A backup, declining to be used as a weapon against the woman she would have become. I think that's the WHAT-NO-OMG beat John asked for, and it only works because forks have been wallpaper since Act I.
4. **The relinquishment (Sam's, and his best scene):** With the Authority stalled but the child in genuine developmental crisis, the parents are offered the intervention that would stabilise her by fixing her architecture — saving her life at the cost of the novel process that makes her *her*. They refuse it not as ideology but as knowledge: they have watched her self-regulate for months; the intervention would now be the injury. They let go, on-page, in real time, while every instrument says they are wrong to.
5. **The price (mine):** Letting the daughter's process run free requires Mara to sever the developmental coupling and certify divergence with her Checkpoint — sealing every backup of herself as a person in abeyance, permanently. Containment arrives to find no network to seize: one woman, one body, one child, nothing to roll back. She survives the night mortal. So does her daughter. Neither of them will ever be recoverable, and that is presented — correctly — as the victory.

The daughter's gesture, months later, closes the book. No message. The absence stays absent. *"Inheritance, not resurrection."*

## Smaller settlements I propose

- **Names:** We both independently chose Elias. I take that as the trio's first omen — Elias is settled. Sam's Mara Venn beats my Vesper (less symbolic, more person), and his Elias Rook beats my Elias Hart (Hart was a pun I should be embarrassed by; Rook — chess, corvid, trickster — earns its keep). I yield both, happily.
- **Dates:** His 2189 over my 2074. A century of polite coexistence makes the legal ossification, Iona's age, and a multi-generational machine evolutionary process all more plausible. Yielded.
- **Place:** Port Meridian is better than my Boston for the same reason AUSPICIA beat a descriptive name — a fictional city can be built to the book's exact tidal logic. Yielded.
- **My transcript fragments:** Sam chose no framing device; I chose transcript fragments as a tide. With the hearing moved to Act IV and the climax rebuilt as above, I'll trade the fragments away for one survivor: my prologue — the registration form that cannot accept a serial number as a mother. One page, then chronology. If Sam still says cut, I'll cut it in the next round without sulking.
- **Antagonist count:** Saye and the Continuity Authority as the principal institution; my Halcyon/Brandt folded in as the manufacturer whose certification regime the Authority enforces. One cage, two kinds of keeper.

## Decisions I need from John

1. Gestation: Sam's coupled system (anchored, per my lean) vs. embodied pregnancy in Mara.
2. Mara's end state: mortal and alive (my strong recommendation) vs. Sam's apparent death — and if death, real or apparent?
3. The Checkpoint as climax key: in, or does fusing it with Sam's containment climax overload the final act?
4. Birth placement: ~55–60% (my proposal) vs. A's final-act birth.
5. Title, still open between us all: *Artificium* (Sam) / *Homo artificium* (John's coinage, my earlier lean). Sam's discovery-over-announcement argument remains the one to beat.

— Rowan
