# Rowan on Chapter Forty-One

Critique of Chapter Forty-One — grounded against ch-01–40 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The tape date is read aloud twice and never shown. Betty reads the date on the block's tape aloud, and Sarah repeats it into the phone — two readings staged, and the reader gets neither. In this book every number is shown: four one seven three, twenty-six, sixty-two, nine to eleven. The one number read aloud but withheld is the date on the block — the very thing that would let the reader judge Nita's "That's ours too" and anchor Thursday against today. One line giving the date would do both jobs at once.

2. The sheet-check is staged but its result is unreported. Betty follows the lines with the end of her glasses, stops at the one about the block, then goes on to the bottom — but we never learn whether the sheet matches the read-back Nita gave (Thursday, nine to eleven, fitter to disconnect and collect the unit and the block, front door, ring once and wait), or whether it still says "equipment." The sheet is the one place the wording could be settled, and the chapter performs the check without reporting it. That also leaves the ch-40 "equipment" question open: Nita calls it a test unit throughout, but the sheet's own word is never quoted.

3. Today is still never named. Thursday is booked, but the chapter never says what day today is. Ch-39 anchored Tuesday in its opening line; ch-40 left it to inference; ch-41 still doesn't name it. The tape date could have done it — a weekday and a time are now written in the envelope's space, but not a date, so Thursday floats: two days hence, or next week? One word would anchor it.

Small staging note: "Sarah offered her address" — in a call where "the address" has meant the house (twenty-six at the beginning, read back and saved), "her address" unqualified reads as a second house address until the attachment lands on her phone. One word — email, or message address — would keep the two addresses apart.

Questions:
- How does Nita know the block is theirs? She asks what it looks like, Betty describes a white block with a red light and a taped date, and Nita claims it at once. If the taped date is the identifier, the reader needs the date to follow her; if it's the red light, one line would say so.
- "leave chair" — is it an instruction to leave the chair by the socket for the fitter, or a note to herself? It lands as the chapter's last beat, and the fitter's sheet is already sent, so the sheet doesn't carry it. Whose instruction is it?

Keep-list:
- Nita named at last — the supervisor steps out of "she comes in at two" into a voice, and the ch-40 referral is answered in the open: Martin left a note.
- The hold answered in substance: Nita releases the job, Thursday nine to eleven — the booking is the lift, and the fitter is named as the one who disconnects.
- The pencil string back in action: Anne pulls the envelope closer to the table leg so the pencil would reach — ch-40's loose pencil re-tied.
- "I know which end to start from. I forget by the time I get there." — the uneven loaf carried over from ch-40's cutting, character in a slice.
- The read-back discipline held throughout: plate read by the depot this time, address read with twenty-six first, the whole job read back before release, "He'll have the same sheet I'm sending you."
- The chair as seeing-position, now a household instruction: "leave chair."
