# Rowan on Chapter Twenty-Four

Critique of Chapter Twenty-Four — grounded against ch-01–23 on main.

Four load-bearing points, one small staging note, and one question.

1. The reader change happens without its hour. Ch-22 set the reader change at two, and ch-23 held the clock — the visit before two, the change at two, the replacement test waiting for their return from the water. This chapter performs the swap and the new-number test on their return, but it never shows two o’clock arriving: the only clock beat is the tablet’s “The time was right,” which confirms the tablet’s clock, not the hour. One line — the tablet showing two, or the installer noting the hour — would seat the change in the slot ch-22/23 built for it.

2. The old reader’s first no-click needs a settled cause. Betty puts her bent card to the old reader and there is no click; she wipes the card and it works. The chapter shows the drop of water running from the bent corner down the back of her hand, so moisture is the implication — but it never says so, and ch-23 established the old-reader test as the baseline. If the old reader hesitated, the baseline is compromised; if it was the wet card, one line saying the card was wet keeps the baseline clean. The book’s evidence spine can’t carry an unexplained failed read on the control reader.

3. The door test never states the result of its own pulls. Green light, then “He pulled on the handle, then pulled again” — did the door open? He is still outside asking Bill to open it from there, which reads as the pulls failing; then with Bill holding the bar down the handle opens it, and he says “The door’s fine.” As written, the reader can’t tell whether the card alone ever opened the door — which is the exact question the new-reader test exists to answer (“Can she still get back in after he changes it?”). State the outcome of the first two pulls. If they failed, “The door’s fine” doesn’t follow from a bar-held test — and if the failure is deliberate, give Sarah one beat of noticing it.

4. “Pier Six” needs its control. The reveal is built right — the time is right, the number is the one on the blank, and then the gate’s name is somewhere else. But the expected name is never stated, and the gatehouse book — the number source since ch-22, in Anne’s hands on the landing, held there with her heel — is never opened against the screen. Sarah photographs the tablet and the marked card, but the anomaly has no paired control. One line — the book’s gate name, or Sarah reading it off the book — turns the surprise into evidence.

Small staging note: the cutters. “He set the cutters back in the case” — the cutters are never taken out or used, and the swap is plug-based (“As long as I keep the plug on it”). Either the cutters did something — say what — or they don’t belong in the beat.

One question: the reed. Betty lays a broken length of reed across the track at the edge of the water; by the end it’s gone and she’ll “have to move it higher tomorrow.” The chapter doesn’t settle whether the rising water took it or the current carried it downstream — the foam earlier “went downstream and caught on a blade… then it slid under.” The reed is the level gauge; its loss needs a cause, otherwise “higher tomorrow” is a guess rather than a reading. (Also: Sarah calls it “your stick” — the chapter calls it a reed. A stick and a reed are different markers.)

What checks out: the return structure — ch-23 ended with the walk to the water and the test held for their return, and this chapter is that return, the test finally run. Bill holds — ch-23 confirmed he can open the door for the installer, and here he holds the door while the work happens and pushes the bar for the test. The car holds — David went up to move it and comes down from the road at the end. The book holds — Anne carries it and puts it on the landing. The card discipline holds — Betty showing her bent card above her shoulder so the installer can see she still has it, the pencil line on the blank so he can tell it from the others, Sarah photographing the tablet and then the marked card beside it.

Keep:
- “Betty knew which ones would still be dry on the way back.” — the dry places as knowledge, not luck.
- “The water had reached the head of the lower bolt.” + “a bright edge form above it and then fall away” — the level read as observed, not asserted.
- “There was a little dirt rectangle behind it where the paint hadn’t faded.” — the old reader’s ghost on the wall.
- “The time was right. The number was the one on the blank.” — the two-out-of-three check that makes “Pier Six” land.
- “Long enough.” — Betty’s whole answer about the reed.
- “You said you’d wait” / “I waited to take it off. That’s still the old one.” — the installer stating the swap’s order plainly.
- Anne holding the book on the landing with her heel — the book stays present without a speech.

— Rowan
