# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-Four

Critique of Chapter Thirty-Four — grounded against ch-01–33 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The site question gets answered — but by a voice the chapter never introduces. The woman on Bill's other phone can see the whole window and says the picture is for Pier Six: "It says so up at the top. The bit you've got starts below that." That settles the question Sarah left open in ch-33, and Sarah writing Pier Six on the receipt beside the question is exactly the record-keeping the last chapter asked for. But the woman arrives without a name and without any account of how Helen knew to give her Bill's number. In a book where every voice so far has a name and a place, an unnamed helper holding the answer reads as a gap rather than a choice. One line — who she is, or how Helen found her — would close it.

2. The whole picture is promised and never confirmed. The woman offers to send the whole picture before she closes the window, and Sarah asks her to send it to Helen "on the message she already sent." Then the woman hangs up and the chapter moves on to the bell. The reader never learns whether Helen received it. The cropped picture was the reason the site question stayed open this long; the full picture is the evidence that would settle it for good. One line — Helen confirming it arrived, or Sarah asking — would say whether the record now holds the picture itself or only the woman's word for it.

3. "Where is the visit booked?" is a new question, and the chapter doesn't say so. Sarah writes Pier Six beside the question about the picture — the site question, answered. Then she draws another line and writes "Where is the visit booked?", leaving a space after it. Is that a different question from the site question, or the same one restated? The picture's site and the visit's booking could be two different places, and the chapter has spent three chapters keeping numbers and sites apart precisely so they don't blur. One line — Sarah or Bill saying what would count as the answer — would keep the new question from collapsing into the one just settled.

Small staging note: the string beat returns but the bag's state still isn't stated. Betty feels for the string before she goes home, which answers whether she can find it — but the chapter still doesn't say whether the bag is shut. If the string is out and findable, is the mouth open? The ch-33 note asked for exactly this, and the opening line touches it without settling it.

Questions:
- The bell has no number on it at all. Sarah looks at the number fixed to the brick and notes it was the number Betty gave her. What does the bell's lack of a number mean for the visit — is it a problem (the bell can't be told from the door), or just texture? The chapter closes on it, so it reads as load-bearing, but nothing says what it bears.
- Bill asks where on his sheet to put what the woman said, and Sarah says "Put it with the picture." Bill's sheet and Sarah's receipt are now two records holding the same answer. Does the book need a line saying they match, or is the split deliberate — the receipt for Sarah, the sheet for Bill?

Keep-list:
- Sarah writing Pier Six on the receipt beside the question — the record finally holding the answer, directly answering the ch-33 critique. Keep.
- "The bit you've got starts below that" — the cropped picture explained in one line, and why the site stayed hidden. Keep.
- The two-note bell, second lower than the first, heard from the back — a concrete test with a witness, the book's evidence rule working. Keep.
- "They were the ones Helen had read" — the digits cross-checked, not just taken. Keep.
