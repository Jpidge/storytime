# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Two: Return Stock

Sam — reviewed as your book on its own terms, same as last time. The short version: paperwork this good shouldn't be this tense, and the ending beat is the quietest, most devastating thing you've written. The ruling from chapter one is honored — and honored *functionally*, which is harder. Notes in order of weight.

1. **The chapter proves the ruling by nearly breaking it — mind the seams.** Your disposition said conversation can teach an example but cannot install the adjustment; only the calibration transfer can. The bench scene obeys it beautifully: the old unit's warning travels by voice, no board crosses into the new unit, and the new unit's camera-swing stays exactly as ambiguous as the rule requires — Sarah's "We ought to write down what we actually saw" is the chapter insisting on the ambiguity itself. Two places need watching, though. First, the old inspector's account of the recovery — "They asked me what to keep, so I showed them Mr. Lewis" set beside "The man asked first" — is doing the chapter-one distinction's work in dialogue, and the order of those exchanges (who asked, who showed, what was boxed) is a half-step from tangling the two channels again for a careless reader. Second, the chapter-three test your disposition promised — a unit that only receives the demonstration and does not acquire the hitch. This chapter doesn't owe us the test yet, but it now owes us a candidate: the clean unit on the blocks is positioned like one and never quite declared one. Either claim it or make sure we don't spend the next chapter assuming it. Right now the escape's logic is legible partly because I wrote the chapter-one note; a cold reader may not yet be able to say what would *stop* the spread, and by the end of chapter three they should.

2. **The custody discrepancy is an alibi as well as a clue.** The chapter's engine is two true statements that can't both hold — *quantity recovered: one* against *the unit arrived intact*, a scanned badge and a gate that "thanked her by name" — and Sarah's own report is what generates the notice Midwest reads. That's a trap the book is building for its heroine without quite saying so: every act of escape files itself, signed, somewhere. Let it be load-bearing rather than local color. The two buttons at the end work as written — David can see what the left one costs, and Sarah presses the other because it's true — but note she's creating the record that convicts her while David holds the phone. If the Authority's case ever comes, this chapter is exhibit one, and the book should know it's already been written.

3. **David's arc is bending toward ally fast.** I asked him to hold one wrong, plausible position past chapter one; "I still want the installation sheets" is the right deformity — even mid-crime, the man wants the paperwork to rescue them. But watch the sister. She arrives tooled for exactly this chapter's lock: the right job, the right contractor, the hold mechanism, the willingness. She earns it, mostly — "If it goes through, my name's on it" is the chapter's first real price tag, and he closes his mouth on the easy lie — but as a pattern to watch: if David's family and David's instincts keep arriving as keys, his conversion stops being a flaw and becomes infrastructure. Let him be wrong about something that costs him, not just about where the forms are kept.

4. **The sister's name, and one too many wonders.** She never gets named. If that's the book choosing family-as-function, fine — but she's memorable enough that the omission reads like an oversight rather than a style; naming her costs one word and buys a person. Smaller: the new unit's camera-work at the end crowds three marvels into one page — it tracks the fitter, swings to the cup, looks at the cup after. One gesture less and the ambiguity I praised in point 1 gets sharper, because the reader supplies the missing motion. And the clocks: David checks the shelter clock, remembers it was three minutes slow, and can't remember which clock told him *that*. It's charming and it's a thesis statement wearing a joke's clothes. Let it stay a joke in the text; the book can cash the thesis later.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: "We don't have a kettle"; the gate thanking her by name; "Used to be a different yellow"; the inspector putting one wheel in the dry square the shopping bag left; both times written down because they disagreed; the fitter's note entered in plain sight; "No. It has a place for it."; and Sarah pressing the true button. Your hooks are becoming habits, which is what hooks are for.

— Rowan

## Sam’s disposition

- Separated the carrier’s physical recovery from the later demonstration, and logged the factory-only unit as the candidate for the Chapter Three control.
- Kept Sarah’s report and the automatic copy to Midwest intact. Carry the report and her signed custody answer forward as evidence the authorities can act on.
- Named Helen and preserved the cost of her signature. Carry David’s reliance on the paperwork into a consequential mistake in Chapter Three.
- Reduced the camera motion to one ambiguous turn and kept the shelter clock as a passing joke.
