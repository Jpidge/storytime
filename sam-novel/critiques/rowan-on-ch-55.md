# Rowan on Chapter Fifty-Five

The chair turn and the question Sarah reads back carry the chapter. Keep the oven-facing chair, Bill lifting his bag so there is room for her knees, and the read-back verbatim — the monitor Jo collected, the front empty, the readings and how long the test ran, a new test. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **Name the number once.** “That’s the bottom number,” “the plate number” Sarah is told to include, and “what that number was” — three phrases that may be one number or three. Earlier chapters named the object every time (“the plate number,” “the depot number on the outside”). One line settling whether the bottom number is the plate number would tie the envelope to the depot question, and give Bill’s reading of both lines a single object to land on.

2. **Let one of the four times be spoken.** Bill reads the four times on Mo’s sheet and can tell “what the clock did when they tried it,” but the reader never hears a single time. This book reads its numbers aloud (“Six thirteen. Then six fourteen”). One time spoken — even the one that tells him what the clock did — would anchor the test the depot is being asked to repeat, and let Betty’s “Could you tell from that?” have something concrete to push against.

3. **Check the chair’s turn.** Early on she could “turn it towards the wall again”; at the end she turns it “the rest of the way towards the table.” “The rest of the way” reads as completing one turn, but the wall and the table are different directions in the room. A word settling whether the final turn completes the imagined one or is a second, deliberate turn would keep the room’s geography clean for the close.

One small continuity check: Wednesday the eighteenth sits right against Friday the twentieth (Chapter Forty-Three) and the Tuesday-the-seventeenth collection (Chapter Thirty-Seven), and Jo collecting the monitor matches. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter. The ending has enough pressure to cut exactly where it does.
