# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-Three

Critique of Chapter Thirty-Three — grounded against ch-01–32 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The subject keeps "the first one" — but which number is that? Helen asks whether to put the new number in the subject; Sarah says leave the first one in the subject and put the new one in the question. The hold depends on the subject carrying the original reference while the replacement appears only inside the question. But by now the correspondence holds several numbers — the receipt's job numbers, the two site numbers written at the side, and now a replacement reference — and the chapter never names which one sits in the subject. "The one we started with" most likely means the original visit reference, but the reader has to guess. One line — Helen or Sarah naming which number the subject holds — would settle it.

2. The site question drops out of Helen's message. In ch-32 Sarah told Helen to ask three things: what the new reference is for, that the first test and today's delivery stay under their own numbers, and which site the picture is for. The reply answers none of the three — it names the number ("a replacement reference") and tells her to quote it in future correspondence. Helen's new message re-asks the first two — "Which reference does this replace?" with the first test and the delivery underneath, one on each line — but the site question isn't in it. Sarah knows it's still open; she tells Betty "We'll still need them to answer this one." Is the site question being held for the next reply on purpose, or did it slip out of the message? One line would say.

3. The receipt records everything except what's still unanswered. By the end of the scene the receipt holds the job numbers, both site names and numbers at the side, "replacement reference" on a clean strip below the crease, and Betty's address being written in — but nothing on the paper marks that the site question is still open. It lives only in Sarah's finger moving down the paper to the two names. The book's rule is that the record holds what matters — read aloud, written as heard — and here the one open question is carried by gesture, not by the paper or by words. One line — Sarah writing the unanswered question on the receipt, or saying it aloud to Betty — would put it in the record.

Small staging note: the string does two jobs at once. "You have to pull the string first" closes the bag; "Leave the string out. I can find that" makes the string the finder Betty reaches for. The final state — bag in Betty's coat pocket, string laid over the edge, keys retrievable — doesn't say whether the bag is shut. If the string is out, is the mouth open, and could the keys fall? One beat — the bag drawn shut with the string still in reach — would settle it.

One question: the brass disc. Four keys on the ring "and a little brass disc with a hole punched through the middle." Anne picks the ring up by the disc, lowers it by the disc, and the disc catches at the mouth coming out. The disc is used as a handle throughout, but the chapter never says what it is — a tag, a fob, a token? One line would say, or is the disc meant to stay unnamed, a thing whose use is its name?

Second question: "Put my address on your copy as well." Sarah's record is the receipt — a single paper, folded along its old crease. Betty asks for her address on "your copy," as well as on something else — but no copy of anything has been established, and nobody else holds one. Copy of what — the receipt, the question Helen is sending? One line would say.

What checks out: "Leave that in the subject. Put the new one in the question so they can tell which one you mean." — the hold: the original stays visible, the replacement appears only inside the question. "I've put those underneath, one on each line. I've asked them to answer on the same lines." — the answer format enforced, same as the receipt's lines. "The little one's the back door. I need it where I can reach it without looking." — the test's purpose stated plainly before it's run. "Leave the string out. I can find that." — the finder, not the closure, is what Betty needs. "She wrote the two words on a clean strip below the crease." — the receipt gains a new kind of entry below the fold. "At the house number she stopped and let Betty tell her." — digits recorded as read, the rule honored. "If they answer that question, will we know where they're coming?" — Betty asking the consequence, not the wording. "I didn't try keys." — Anne's honesty about what the spoon test did and didn't prove. "You have to pull the string first." — the bag's rule stated before the second try. "It fell the last inch with a sound like the spoon striking the table." — the test echoing ch-32's spoon. "Well, one of us can take our shoes off." — the domestic negotiation, shoes on, shoes off.
