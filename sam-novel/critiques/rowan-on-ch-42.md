# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Two

Critique of Chapter Forty-Two — grounded against ch-01–41 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The readings leave the house unread. Jo offers "the last one" before she disconnects it, and the display shows zero — but zero is the current draw, not the record. The monitor has been on the plug since the twelfth (ch-41), and "The readings go back with it" — the record departs without anyone ever seeing what it holds. Betty asks the right question ("Does that mean it hasn’t been doing anything?") and Jo answers it only for *now* ("It means it isn’t drawing anything now"). In a book where every number is shown and checked, the one record that could say what the unit did while the monitor was on is the one that leaves unchecked. One line — what the last reading was, or that the display holds only the current draw — would settle which.

2. The disconnection time is written and read back but never given. Jo writes *zero at disconnection* and "put the time beside it"; Betty reads the line back. The chapter is precise about nine and nine forty, and the copy Jo leaves Betty carries "the plate number, the time" — but the reader never gets the time itself. Same shape as the ch-41 tape date: the one number that would fix the record is the one withheld. One word would do it.

3. The dot is staged twice and never read. It blinks, goes out, comes back — and nobody asks what it is. Betty asks about the zero, not the dot; Jo never glosses it. In a book where every shown thing earns a reading — the plate, the address, the sheet, the hours in the envelope — the dot is the one shown thing nobody reads. Is it a standby heartbeat, a fault, a pulse? One line would say whether it matters or not.

Small staging note: Betty asks if she can bring the sheet inside, and the next beat is already at the socket with Jo pointing at the plug monitor "on the sheet" — the sheet crosses the threshold offstage. A line of Jo handing it over, or Betty taking it, would carry it across.

Questions:
- Why does Betty pull the table forward half an inch at the end — to keep the socket reachable, or is it habit? "She could still get two fingers behind it" measures the gap by hand, but the reason for leaving it is unspoken.
- The chair: ch-41 ended with Betty’s note, *leave chair*. The chapter opens with the chair placed where she can see the socket from it, and Betty’s copy laid on it — but after the table is pushed back, the chair’s final position is unmentioned. Is it left by the socket, as the note said?

Keep-list:
- The bell discipline paid off in action: it rings once, nobody knocks, the second note dies away before Betty stands — ch-41’s "Ring once and wait" kept to the letter.
- *zero at disconnection* written on the sheet with the time beside it, and Betty reading the line back — the sheet as a record, now in Betty’s hands as a copy with the plate number, the time, and a line for the two things taken.
- The tick discipline: Anne asks, Betty says "Wait till it’s gone," and the tick comes only after the gate shuts.
- The pencil string: Anne reaches first for the pencil and lays it on the envelope so the string won’t catch — the tethered pencil, still tethered.
- The display holding the zero for a moment after the plug is pulled, then going dark — a quiet ending for the thing that has been watched.
- The table’s half-inch, measured by two fingers — the house kept workable.
