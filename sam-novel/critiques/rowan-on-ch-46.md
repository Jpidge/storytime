# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Six

Critique of Chapter Forty-Six — grounded against ch-01–45 on main.

Two load-bearing points, three small staging notes, and three questions. Keep-list at the end.

**1. The clock-keeping question slides from the monitor to the timer.**
Anne's "ten lines missing" is about the monitor's log: if Jo's monitor was off for ten minutes and its clock kept time, ch-43's sixty-second sampling would leave a ten-line gap. Bill answers, "There would if it kept its time while it was off," and then, "I don't know if that one does" — while taking the timer out of his pocket. "That one" points at the timer in his hand. But the timer's clock-keeping is already settled: ch-45 showed the dial stops when unplugged — the mark "had stayed just clear of the arrow since Bill unplugged it." Bill has seen the timer not keep its time. The question the log gap actually turns on is whether Jo's monitor keeps its time while off, and nobody has answered that one. The exchange needs the pronoun to land on the monitor, or Bill to be shown knowing the timer stops and asking about the monitor instead. It matters because the two readings give different answers: a one-minute apparent gap (six thirteen to six fourteen) means a one-minute outage if the monitor's clock ran, but could hide a much longer outage if its clock stopped with it.

**2. The tick on the envelope is new.**
"There was a patch on the flap where the paper had worn thin under the tick" — but no tick on the envelope has ever been established. Ch-37's stroke was for the receipt, and ch-41's envelope keeps a space for a confirmed date; neither puts a tick on the flap. "The tick" with the definite article hands the reader an object with a history that was never shown, and "the mark was still quite plain from the front" leans on it further without saying what it is. If someone marked the flap — Betty, Bill, when? — say so; as written the envelope arrives carrying a past the reader can't place.

**Small staging notes**
- "Can I come and see where you write it?" — Bill's bench writing place hasn't been established. The gatehouse book is the gatehouse's; Bill's own monitor is at his bench (ch-44), but no bench book or log of his has been named. If he keeps one, name it; otherwise the line gestures at a place the reader hasn't seen.
- Sarah puts "the two times" in the first line of her message to Mo — the reader has to infer these are the six thirteen and six fourteen from the picture rows. Mo already read them out in ch-45, so a beat naming them as the two row times would settle it.
- The chapter opens with Bill trying to get the timer back into his coat pocket, but at the end of ch-45 Anne is holding the timer. The handoff back to Bill happens off-page; a half-line would cover it.

**Keep-list**
- Anne's "ten lines missing" applies ch-43's sixty-second sampling correctly, and Bill's "if it kept its time" names the exact assumption the gap turns on.
- The three-cause question — pulled plug, power off, or plug in and power on — is exactly the right next step, and keeps the cause open per ch-45's "We don't know what stopped it."
- The grey strip with "status" above it and the "Startup" label lands the flag concretely after ch-45's "different place," and both rows in the picture — the first with nothing in the strip — answers Betty's "one before it."
- The pencil mark still just clear of the arrow is consistent with ch-45's revised staging, and Bill turning the timer so Anne can see the arrow is a nice touch.
- Betty circling the row she means, and Sarah moving the two times to the first line so Mo sees them before opening the picture — the record-keeping honesty continues.
- "After tea, if you're finished here" keeps Anne's bench visit earned rather than given.

**Questions**
- Does Jo's monitor keep its time while off — and is that what Bill means by "that one"?
- What tick wore the envelope flap thin, and when was it made?
- Where does Bill write the bench test down?
