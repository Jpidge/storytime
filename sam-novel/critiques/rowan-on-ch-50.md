# Rowan on Chapter Fifty

Critique of Chapter Fifty — grounded against ch-01–49 on main.

Two load-bearing points, three small staging notes, and two questions. Keep-list at the end.

**1. "That's the one I was looking at" points at the wrong copy — six fourteen was read off the sent file, not the depot's.**

The preservation passage juggles two copies: "the copy they took off the monitor" (the depot's source copy) and "the one they sent us" (the attachment in the first depot email). Sarah says, "I can ask them to keep the copy they took off the monitor. This is the one they sent us." Betty answers, "Ask them to keep that one too" — "that one" is the off-monitor copy — and then, "If somebody asks why I said six fourteen, that's the one I was looking at."

But Betty said six fourteen in ch-43 while reading the file on the laptop: Sarah scrolled up on the laptop, Betty read the line herself before Sarah closed the page, and then had Sarah print that page sideways. The sideways printout came from the sent attachment, not from the depot's off-monitor copy, which Betty has never seen. So "that's the one" has to mean the sent one, while the sentence sits directly after "that one" meaning the off-monitor copy. At the exact moment the chapter is about preserving the right copy, the sentence can be read as tracing six fourteen to a copy Betty never looked at. Pin the referent — "the one they sent us" — so the provenance of the six-fourteen reading is unambiguous.

**2. "The two on the second line were the same" — the same as what? No two of the four times are equal.**

Betty lays Sarah's sheet alongside Mo's new one and runs a finger along the four times: "The two on the second line were the same, and she checked them both." Without a comparator, "were the same" reads as "equal to each other" — and no two of the four times are equal. Ch-49's tests give 9:10 and 9:11 on the old battery, 9:20 and 9:21 on the new; no arrangement of the four times onto two lines puts two equal times together. The intended reading is presumably that the second line agrees across the two sheets — Sarah's against Mo's — but the chapter never states the comparator, nor how Mo arranged her times on her two lines, so the reader can't tell whether the second lines even correspond. The four times are the chapter's evidence; the verification sentence needs its terms: the same as on the other sheet.

**Small staging notes**

- *Jo's receipt, and the clip.* "Betty took Jo's receipt out from under the clip" — neither the receipt under that name nor the clip has been staged. The book has "the depot's receipt" (ch-43, beside which Betty put the sideways printout), "the sheet Jo had left" (ch-43), and "Jo's sheet" (ch-48); "Jo's receipt" may be one of those, or a third paper, and the only clips near paper in ch-01–49 are the one in the depot's picture (ch-48) and clipboard clips. One phrase placing it would settle which paper this is and where the clip lives.
- *The circled number.* Betty "looked at the number she had circled" on the sideways printout — but no circling of that printout is staged in ch-01–49. The only circles in the book are ch-46's circle round the *Startup* row on Sarah's notes and ch-47's copy of *Startup* "beside her circle" on a different printout. Which number did Betty circle on the Wednesday reading, and when?
- *The second line of the email.* Sarah opens the first depot email and "looked at the name of the attachment", and the request ends with "two things to keep, each on its own line" — but the reader is never told the attachment's name, so the second line can't be reconstructed. The first line is fully given (Thursday 19 November, 11:23); the second is opaque. (That email had two attachments in ch-43, so "the one they sent us" is itself doing work the name would settle.)

**Questions**

1. *Whose answer is under the flap?* Ch-49 has Betty working the sum "without turning up the flap to see the answer", and ch-50 opens with "The answer was showing now" — but the flap was down in ch-49 and nothing stages it being turned up, and the answer itself is still unattributed. Is it Jo's answer, the envelope's own, or Betty's from an earlier working? Anne compares her answer against it ("Did you get the same?"), so the comparison's meaning turns on whose it is.
2. *Which number on Jo's receipt?* Betty "read the number along with Sarah" — presumably the plate number, to match the box at the top of Mo's sheet — but the receipt has carried two numbers before (ch-30: "which number belongs to the box and which one belongs to the visit?"; ch-31: two numbers on the receipt). One word would settle it.

**Keep-list**

- The fitter's initials resolve ch-49's "someone to stand with her and watch the bench clock" — the witness is named, and the second signature turns from decoration into evidence.
- "Thursday 19 November, 11:23" is pinned to the depot book line Sarah read from the picture in ch-48, and "without saying it's the first one" is exactly the book's ethic: the book gives a date, not an ordinal, and Betty refuses to label beyond the evidence. Sarah stopping mid-word on *original download* dramatizes the instinct.
- Keeping both copies — the one taken off the monitor and the one they sent — is the right preservation logic: a copy can differ from its source, and the six-fourteen reading has to stay traceable to the copy Betty actually read.
- Betty pulling the Wednesday printout clear of both Monday sheets keeps the days apart, the same discipline as ch-49's "Monday 30 November, depot" — the stapler set down, the sheets left unbound.
- The blank sheet from the printer misfeed becoming Anne's working sheet: the accident is put to use, and the two answers side by side on one sheet mirror the two sheets laid alongside at the top of the chapter.
