# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Three

Critique of Chapter Forty-Three — grounded against ch-01–42 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and three questions.

1. The one non-zero reading is never tied to a cause. The log finally answers ch-42's open question — the record is read, pages of zeroes, the one mark found by going to the bottom first and working back. Sarah reads it the way it is written ("Zero point four") and takes the unit from the header, not from a guess ("Watts. It says up at the top"). But in a book where every number is shown and checked against something, the single reading that isn't zero is never asked after: what draws four-tenths of a watt? The monitor itself samples even at zero — ch-42's dot "flashes while the monitor's taking a sample. It does that even at zero" — so the obvious candidate is the monitor measuring its own draw, but the chapter never raises it, and Betty's oven-timer alibi proves she was in the kitchen without touching the socket. One line — someone wondering whether the 0.4 is the monitor itself or something on the plug — would say whether the number is evidence or artifact.

2. The minute-apart gap is stated but the log's resolution is never fixed. Betty asks the right question ("Can you see how long it did that?") and Sarah answers honestly ("They're a minute apart. I can't see what happened in between"). But the chapter never says whether the log records every minute or samples once a minute — the 0.4 could have lasted a second or the full minute, and the distinction is the whole of what the number means. Same shape as ch-42's withheld time: the one precision the record could give is the one left open. If the columns carry the sampling interval, one line would fix it.

3. The Friday is undated. "The email came on Friday" — the book dates its records ("Twelve November" in ch-41; the receipt, the sheet, the tape all carry dates). Friday after a Thursday disconnection is inferable, but the chapter never puts a date on the email, the log, or the printed sheet Betty carries back to the kitchen. That sheet is meant to travel to "whoever answered" — without a date on it, the record is undated. One word would fix it.

Small staging note: the chair. Ch-42 closed with the chair placed where Betty could see the socket from it, and Betty given a practical reason for keeping the gap; ch-43 opens with Anne pulling "a chair up to the end of the table" with no word on whether it is that chair moved or another. In a book where the chair's position is a standing instruction ("leave chair"), a moved chair deserves a line. Related: Betty asks Sarah to print the page "with the top of the columns on it, so whoever answered could see the word over the number," and Sarah chooses landscape over shrinking — but the chapter never confirms the header made it onto the warm sheet Betty carries back. The instruction is given; the result isn't shown.

Questions:
- Who sent the log? The email "came on Friday" with two attachments, but the chapter never says from whom — the depot direct, or Jo forwarding it. It matters for who answers Betty's question: Sarah says "We can ask her," but if the depot sent it, the question goes to the depot, not Jo.
- The receipt Sarah opens is called "Collection receipt," and "the depot had stamped its copy *received*" — is the attachment a scan of the depot's stamped copy, or the receipt itself? The plate number is read on both and matches; the provenance of the copy is the one thing unshown.
- The envelope tick: Anne taps the tick she made after Jo left and asks "Does this stay?" Betty says "We watched her take it." The monitor is gone and the tick stays — but is the tick now the record of a finished job, or does the envelope still hold something open?

Keep-list:
- The log as the payoff of ch-42's open question: the record finally read, pages of zeroes, the one mark found by going to the bottom first and working back.
- The 9:52 match: Betty fetching Jo's sheet and laying it under the screen, the time matched, zero beside it — the sheet and the log agreeing.
- Betty's oven-timer alibi: set for six fifteen, switched off before it rang, the oven door remembered and the socket not — presence proved, the socket honestly unobserved.
- "Zero point four" read aloud the way it was written, and the unit taken from the header, not assumed.
- The minute-apart honesty: Sarah saying what the log can't show instead of guessing.
- The print discipline: print the page with the top of the columns so the word travels with the number.
- The envelope tick: Anne tapping the tick, "Does this stay?" — "We watched her take it."
