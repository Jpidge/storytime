# Rowan on Chapter Forty

Critique of Chapter Forty — grounded against ch-01–39 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The hold is confirmed but never lifted. In ch-39 the hold was the open question — Helen had asked for it, the woman read it back, but nobody said whether it was granted. In ch-40 the man says he "would leave the collection on hold" after saving the corrected address, so the hold is now real. But nothing in the chapter says when it lifts, or who lifts it. A corrected address plus a standing hold is still nobody coming — the chapter swaps ch-39's "driverless" for "held" without closing it. The lifting people "went out on Thursdays," but Thursday is a routine, not a booking, and the hold stands between now and then. One line — him saying the hold lifts when the supervisor confirms, or Betty asking who lifts it — would say whether Thursday is real or just the day the lifters go out.

2. The disconnection question is referred to the supervisor, but the referral itself is never confirmed. He "couldn't tell her which of them could disconnect the unit" — so even with the address corrected, the unit needs disconnecting by someone unidentified — and his answer is "I can ask the supervisor. She comes in at two." Betty says "Give her my number. I'm the one who'll be here." But he never says he will pass it on, or ask her. The chapter's last beat is Betty writing "call me" on the envelope, but no one has agreed to call. In this book a request gets read back — the address was read back before saving, the depot number was read in groups in ch-39 — and here the one request that carries the next step goes unanswered. One line — him confirming he'll pass the number, or Betty asking him to say it back — would place the supervisor in the chain instead of leaving her as a time on the envelope.

3. The day is still never named, and the space left for it is now spent. Ch-39's revision anchored Tuesday in its opening line; ch-40 never says the day at all. The reader infers it is still Tuesday — Betty went to the shop and came back, "when I get back" — but "two" and "Thursdays" float without a day to attach to. And the date-space: in ch-37 Anne was told "You can leave room for the date," and in ch-40 Betty writes "call me" in that space instead. So the room left for the date now holds "call me," and the collection day still has nowhere to be written. One word naming the day would anchor both the supervisor's "two" and the lifters' "Thursdays."

Small staging note: the pencil's string disappears. Ch-39's revision tied the string to the kitchen table leg, and the pencil could reach only as far as the string let it. In ch-40 Betty "picked up the pencil when Sarah was done" and writes — no string shown, no constraint. The pencil reads as loose. One line — the string going taut, or the pencil set back on its length — would keep the kitchen's small geography continuous.

Questions:
- He never says the word "equipment." In ch-38 Helen asked dispatch to confirm whether "equipment" meant the connected test unit, and in ch-39 "the collection still just says equipment." In ch-40 the man says "That's the unit we're waiting for" — the depot's answer in substance — but the word itself is never confirmed or replaced on the collection. Is the description now the unit, or does "equipment" still stand?
- Are the office voices deliberately unnamed? The man who answers, the supervisor "she," the ch-39 woman — the household is named (Betty, Sarah, Anne, Helen) and the office never is. If that is the book's rule, it holds; but the ch-39 question about Betty calling the driver "he" with no name was never answered on the page, and the pattern is now three chapters deep.

Keep-list:
- "Return from sixty-two" read back, twenty-six given, read back again and saved — the depot-role point from ch-39 answered in the open, the man doing what the woman could only describe.
- "That's the unit we're waiting for" — the 4173 match stated on the page at last, plate to list.
- "Leave it there for now. I can see the number from it." — the chair as the seeing-position; ch-39's "room to pull up a chair and read the plate" delivered.
- "The fold had gone soft in her pocket" — the envelope's small history held across chapters, folded once with the depot number on the outside, now carried to the shop and back.
- The read-back discipline held throughout: nothing saved without being read back, the address included.
