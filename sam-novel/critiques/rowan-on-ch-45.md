# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Five

Critique of Chapter Forty-Five — grounded against ch-01–44 on main.

Three load-bearing points, four small staging notes, and three questions. Keep-list at the end.

**1. "Nothing in the front" contradicts the timer having just been plugged in.**
Ch-44 fixes "the front" as the front socket of the block: Bill asks "what it shows when there's nothing on the front," and the depot's reply says the watts figure came from "the socket on the front of the block." In ch-45 Bill plugs the timer in — into that front socket, since the whole experiment is whether the monitor picks up the clock alone — and Anne then says "Nothing in the front." On the chapter's own terms the front socket is occupied. If Anne means the display shows nothing, which is what Betty's three-changes wait then confirms, the line needs to say the display, not the front. As written it reads as if the socket is empty.

**2. The pencil mark can't start opposite the arrow and newly show a gap.**
Bill puts the mark "on the rim opposite the arrow," then Betty sees it has "travelled just far enough that she could see a strip of white between it and the arrow" — and later Anne finds it "still just clear of the arrow." A mark that starts opposite the arrow already has half the dial between itself and the arrow; a small travel doesn't newly open a strip of white. The sentences only cohere if the mark started at the arrow and moved just clear of it. Either the mark goes on the rim at the arrow, or the gap sentence goes. Follow-on: the mark opens its strip in the time of three display changes — about three minutes at ch-43's sixty-second sampling — but is "still just clear" after the whole Mo phone call, which runs well longer than three changes. If the dial turns steadily, the mark should be further along by then; the dial's rate needs to pick one speed.

**3. The log itself should bound when the monitor went off.**
Mo says the flag says the monitor "had just come back on," and "There isn't a line for when it went off." But ch-43 established the log samples once every sixty seconds. If the monitor went off, its samples stop; the gap between the last line before the flag and the Wednesday line is itself the evidence — the previous line's time bounds the off-time to within a minute. The chapter leaves the off-time unknowable when the log should be able to say it. Ask Mo to read the line before the Wednesday line: what time does it carry, and does the gap match a monitor that was off?

**Small staging notes**
- Two benches share one name: Bill's bench (ch-44, where he uses his own monitor) and the depot's bench (Mo's "the programme we use on the bench"). Mo's meaning is clear enough in context, but a reader tracking "the bench" as one place could trip — a word for whose bench would settle it.
- "The monitor had just come back on" — which monitor? The log is the original's, so this is Jo's, but Bill's substitute is the monitor in the room and the chapter never says which one the flag belongs to. Name it.
- Sarah "fetched the sheet" and Betty then reads "from the printout" — same object? Name it once. And "the date" Sarah puts under Mo's name — the Wednesday date, or today's? Say which.
- Betty "looked towards the oven" when she asks what put it off — the oven hasn't appeared before, and the chapter never says why she'd look at it. If it's a candidate for what took the monitor off (same circuit?), say so; as written it's a glance at an object with no established meaning.

**Keep-list**
- The clock-moves / monitor-zero contrast is the chapter's clean result, and Sarah writing down both things ("The clock moved. The monitor showed zero.") keeps the record honest.
- Bill's limit is stated straight: the bench meter "would tell us about this timer" — not the one they took — the same honesty as ch-44's "That won't tell us what Jo's did."
- The export dropping the flag is a good catch: the notes column blank on both copies, the flag in a different place on the original, and the picture of the whole row with the heading above it is exactly the right repair (cf. ch-43's headings).
- "The second time Anne took the night-light out" lands exactly right against ch-44's single removal, and Betty making her wait until the number dropped follows ch-44's zero-wait beat.

**Questions**

- Is the oven on the same circuit as the monitor, or otherwise a candidate for what put it off?
- Whose bench is Mo's programme on — the depot's?
