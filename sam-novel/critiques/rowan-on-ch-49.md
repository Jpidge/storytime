# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Nine

Critique of Chapter Forty-Nine — grounded against ch-01–48 on main.

Three load-bearing points, three small staging notes, and two questions. Keep-list at the end.

**1. Bare "Monday" is genuinely ambiguous now — two Mondays are in play.**

The chapter opens "On Monday", after ch-48's email arrived "on Saturday the twenty-eighth". The Monday after that Saturday is the thirtieth (30 Nov 2026 is a Monday), and the tests Mo describes are "this morning" evidence. But ch-44's reply was dated Monday the twenty-third, so a bare "Monday" no longer points at one day. This book dates everything — ch-48's own critique got "Saturday the twenty-eighth" pinned for exactly this reason — and the date is the provenance here: Monday test times must never be fileable under the Wednesday readings. "Monday the thirtieth" would pin it in one word.

**2. "The number from the monitor's case" — which number, and is it one the reader already has?**

Mo will "include the times and the number from the monitor's case" on the sheet. The only monitor number the reader has is the plate on the front — "Four one seven three" (ch-38), read back by Betty and already sent to dispatch — and the depot book carries "the monitor's number" (ch-48). If the case number is that same number, say so: the sheet then corroborates 4173. If it's a different number, it's a second identifier entering the evidence chain and needs naming or distinguishing from the plate. As written the reader can't tell whether Mo is sending a copy of a known number or a new one — and this book's engine is matching numbers across records.

**3. The battery-test order is inverted — the reader can assign the first result to the wrong battery.**

The account opens "Mo had fitted a new battery. Before taking the old one out, she had set the monitor beside the bench clock." The first sentence states the swap as done; the second rewinds to before it. On first read, the 9:10 test — both clocks at ten past nine, disconnected, powered again a minute later, bench clock nine eleven, monitor still nine ten — can be taken as the new-battery test, and the reader then has to reassign it to the old battery when Sarah asks "What did it do with the new battery?" and Mo answers with the twenty-past test. The finding turns entirely on which battery each test used: old battery loses a minute on restart, new battery keeps it. Lead with the old-battery test, then the swap, then the new-battery test — or mark the first test explicitly ("with the old battery still in") — so the finding lands on first reading rather than on correction. The same pass should make the second test's gap explicit: the first test states "a minute later", the second says only "We did it again at twenty past", leaving the reader to infer the parallel procedure from "did it again". The four times are the chapter's evidence; the parallel should be stated in both tests.

**Small staging notes**

- *Which pencil?* Ch-48 ends with Anne taking the short bench pencil home and the kitchen pencil — the one with the string — staying on the table. Ch-49's "The pencil stopped just short of the last figure" and "The pencil lay just where Betty had left it" never says which one, and the pencil thread has been tracked exactly up to now. One word ("the kitchen pencil", "its string") settles it, and it matters because Anne is about to pick it up and finish the sum.
- *"She had asked someone to stand with her and watch the clock."* The bench clock is the natural referent — the monitor sits beside it — but the book now has three clocks in play (bench, monitor's, kitchen). "Watch the bench clock" would pin it, the same way ch-48's critique pinned the "clock slow" referent.
- *"She put it in the little bowl by the telephone."* No bowl by Betty's telephone exists in ch-01–48 — the only bowl in the book is in David's room (ch-13, "a bowl with one damaged edge"), a different house. A small thing, but the bowl arrives unestablished.

**Questions**

1. Betty is "trying to work out the sum on the back without looking at the answer" — where is the answer? Ch-35 established the sum on the back of the envelope and a name with a line through it; the flap carries the marks. If the answer is on the flap, or elsewhere on the envelope, one phrase placing it would let the reader see what Betty is avoiding.
2. "Did they find the missing minute?" / "They made one. They watched it go." — is Betty saying they *reproduced* the loss, made a minute go missing on purpose and watched it happen? It parses that way, but "made one" could also read as made a minute. One line of Mo's wording would confirm the intended reading.

**Keep-list**

- The two-test structure is clean: old battery loses a minute (bench 9:11, monitor still 9:10), new battery keeps it (both 9:21) — and the computer lead left on the bench means no reset, exactly as ch-48's instruction asked ("before they connect it to the computer again"). The test answers the instruction, not just the question.
- "Monday, depot" at the top instead of putting Wednesday anywhere — Sarah labels provenance rather than merging Monday test times into the Wednesday record. The exact right instinct, and Betty watching to see where Sarah would put Wednesday is the right beat.
- "I can't get it back to Wednesday" — the limit stated, not smoothed over. The test bounds the battery claim to the present, the same honesty as ch-48's "didn't know what it would have done on Wednesday".
- The envelope sum returning (ch-35/37/38) — Betty working the old sum while the phone evidence arrives; the domestic and the evidential in one frame, and Anne finishing the sum at the end hands the work to her without announcing it.
- Anne's grit: shoe off, grit in the little bowl by the telephone — small, observed, child-logic, and it lets the chapter breathe after the four times.
