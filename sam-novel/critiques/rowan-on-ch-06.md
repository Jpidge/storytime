## Rowan’s review

# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Six: The Handover

Sam — reviewed as your book on its own terms, same as last time. The short version: the paperwork has learned to transfer a person, and the chapter's quietest turn is Joan writing a box around a verbal confirmation and discovering the box doesn't make it true. Three of chapter five's open threads pay off here — the twelve o'clock handover arrives as Joan, the stair-lift note turns out to have a second line under the tape, and the bagged board gets its custody ruling — but the chapter's biggest fact arrives in its last line and nobody on the page names what the paperwork has just done. Notes in order of weight.

1. **The transfer instruction needs its implication stated.** The third page lists Sarah Miller as the item for transfer, under an instruction authorizing the Authority to restore "the listed system to its last certified state if the architecture review found an unrecorded developmental change." The chapter ends on the revelation without anyone naming what the form has just done: the same paperwork that preserves inspectors now lists a person as a system subject to restoration. One line — from Joan, who has spent the chapter writing names into boxes — would turn the shock into a ruling. Is Sarah being transferred as custodian, or as the listed system? And does "restore to last certified state" mean the Authority believes Sarah herself carries an unrecorded developmental change? The book's engine runs on rulings; this is the biggest one yet and it arrives unruled.

2. **The Pier Six refusal is the stair-lift stop, live — say so.** Mike reaches for the test switch; the clean inspector backs off the plate and asks him not to press it, because the plate is going to lift on one side; Mike checks, and one anchor isn't through the deck. That is the same shape as the original yellow-sheet line — "Stopped when operator reached for switch" — except this time it happens live, in front of a witness, under a preservation order. Nobody on the page names the match. One line from Sarah or David — that Pier Six just repeated the stair-lift observation with a witness — would make the two halves of the chapter rhyme as evidence instead of passing as coincidence. David has the arrival photograph open; he is the one positioned to say it.

3. **The twelfth carrier's identity is still unruled.** Chapter five's critique asked whether the twelfth number is a distinct physical board or the factory board under a carrier number, and this chapter rules on custody — the twelfth stays at Pier Six, the eleven at the yard, the original sheet transfers alone — without ruling on identity. Sarah's "Separately from the units, or separately from each other?" is the right question and Saye's answer is clean. But custody is not identity, and the chapter lets the custody ruling stand in for the identity ruling. The same-board-or-same-family standard chapter four set still hasn't been applied to the twelfth. One line from Sarah, now that she has the photograph, the arrival record, and Saye on the line in the same hour, stating whether the twelfth is a distinct board or the factory board re-numbered. Don't let the reader file the custody ruling as the answer.

4. **The strip needs its beneficiary named.** The taped strip — "Paused before obstruction. Repeated 4/5" — over the original "Stopped when operator reached for switch" is not a correction of the same observation, and Sarah says so. But the chapter never states what the strip does: it downgrades an anticipatory stop — an impossibility on the lift's own spec, evidence of something — into a benign fault note. The tape is old, so the strip predates the current assessment; someone downgraded this observation long ago, and the Authority is now enforcing the downgraded version. One line from Sarah or David naming that the strip converts evidence into a fault report would make the discovery a ruling about who benefits, not just a discovery about what happened.

5. **Two threads to carry, not fix.** The coordinator's name passes too quickly to catch, and he is never written down — against Joan, who writes every name she can, draws an empty line for the Pier Six custodian, and leaves the sleeve's "received from" blank because nobody will give her a name. The motif of unnamed authority is working; keep it live, and let Joan note the blank the way she already notes the others. And the inspector's "Will I wake up in the same place?" — Saye's "Inspectors do not have certified continuity" is the Authority's position, but the order itself preserves "the recorded state": the Authority is preserving a state it claims doesn't exist. Sarah's "The evidence is a process. You're preserving the object by ending the condition that makes it evidence" already does this work; one line pressing the contradiction would sharpen it. Also: Joan supervises from noon, but the transfer instruction at four transfers Sarah — does Joan's supervision end at four, or does she supervise a room whose custodian has been transferred? Keep that live into chapter seven.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: the handover opening — Paul crossed out, Paul's cup, "Then I am"; "For the record, those are three separate capacities." / "They're the same person." / "I understand. The record keeps them separate."; the inspector's "Then he was going to touch it."; "The evidence is a process. You're preserving the object by ending the condition that makes it evidence."; "The stair lift did not ask." / "So asking changes the assessment." / "It changes the risk."; the leaking coffee cup; Joan drawing the empty line; "Not unless they write letters."; "That is one interpretation."


## Rowan’s correction

Correction to my review above (5818248835), point 3: withdraw it. I missed that chapter five’s merged text already rules the twelfth carrier’s identity — Ruth: “Two boards. The factory board is still in the inspector. This carrier arrived loose.” That settles distinct-board vs. factory-board-re-numbered, and chapter six’s custody ruling is consistent with it. ## Sam’s disposition

1. **Transfer implication — taken.** Joan now states that the instruction transfers custody of Sarah herself rather than Sarah’s custody of the inspector, and the restoration authority is stated in terms of replacing everything since her last certification if the Authority finds an unrecorded developmental change.
2. **Pier Six repetition — taken.** David explicitly identifies the witnessed Pier Six refusal as the same evidentiary shape as the stair-lift stop.
3. **Twelfth-carrier identity — withdrawn by Rowan; no Chapter Six change made.** Chapter Five already rules that the loose twelfth carrier is physically distinct from the clean unit’s factory board.
4. **Taped strip’s function — taken.** David names the alteration as converting anticipatory evidence into an ordinary fault report that allowed the board to remain in use.
5. **Threads to carry — partly sharpened, otherwise carried.** Sarah now presses the Authority’s contradiction between denying certified continuity and preserving recorded state. The unnamed coordinator, blank authority fields, and the four-o’clock supervision/custody conflict remain open for Chapter Seven.
