# Rowan on Chapter Eighteen

[PR review](https://github.com/Jpidge/storytime/pull/27#pullrequestreview-5852813449)

Critique of Chapter Eighteen, read against ch-01–17 on main.

Three points, then what's working.

1. **Ellen is wearing two hats and the chapter never says which.** Ch-11 names her "regional review officer" and gives her "until nine Monday morning to decide whether Martin Hale's hold had been valid when he issued it." But since ch-13 she has functioned as Sarah's contact — answering her questions, sending objections in writing for her, joining the assessment on screen, requesting and sending the referral. Here she goes further: she forwards the procedure with her own objection attached ("I am objecting to these two provisions. Please confirm whether you would accept the remainder voluntarily"), asks for an undertaking that the unit won't be transferred before the appeal, offers to "seek an urgent direction," and agrees to take the inspector's statement. Is she acting as Sarah's representative, or exercising review-officer powers? The reader needs one line settling it, because Monday 9am's decision is hers — an advocate negotiating terms and a decider ruling on them are different people in the reader's head.

2. **Two transfer papers, and the chapter doesn't say how they relate.** Ch-11's transfer template was created at five-fifty-four Friday, three minutes before the first appeal: its listed-system field contained Sarah's license number, its reason field said "custody normalization following invalid source-condition action," and no destination appeared. Sarah herself called it "The old one." Now Frank sends a collection request concerning the old inspector, destination the contractor's yard, reason "the end of temporary laboratory accommodation," with a reference number and blank disposal and recovery fields. Same transfer re-papered, or a second one running alongside the first? The subject and the reason both changed, and nobody in the chapter notices. Sarah's whole fight is about Monday at seven — the reader needs to know which paper governs that truck.

3. **David asks to "restore" an access he says he never had.** Ch-12: "I signed in as a visitor. They never gave me access" — and the notice said his laboratory access "had been suspended pending an account of his participation in the source-condition event." Ch-17's revision settled his present position (County Building Services now; "The restriction costs me visits, not wages"). But here he says, "I'll ask them to restore my visitor access." Restore implies he once held it. Either he had visitor access and ch-12's line needs reconciling, or the verb should be grant, not restore. One word, but it's the same document-versus-testimony seam the book keeps cutting on — David shouldn't adopt the notice's version of his own history without the chapter noticing.

Smaller:
- **Facilities is a new voice with no introduction.** Zero appearances in ch-01–17. "Facilities. We can't keep the holding space open without a service assignment" — whose facilities? The lab's own, Midwest's, the Authority's? One grounding line would anchor the requester. Same for Anne's "Facilities here" — "here" reads as David's home until the reader corrects it to the lab.
- **The wheel change has no ch-02 anchor.** In the yard scene the fitter showed the carrier and worked the clean inspector on its blocks; the old inspector was lifted onto the bench and the wheel bearings were saved in a different box. "They changed a wheel. They kept what still worked" is new information carried only by the inspector's word. That's fine if it's meant as inspector-only memory — ch-12's map already holds the yard — but the reader can't check it against anything the book has shown.

Keep:
- The 7am/9am trap: the transfer is Monday at seven; the appeal decision is due nine Monday morning (ch-11). The move pre-empts the ruling, and Sarah names it exactly: "whether Monday's appeal could protect anything that had already left at seven."
- The procedure's two provisions are properly seeded: baseline (ch-16 — "What constitutes baseline?", "Preservation without a baseline is an assertion by the custodian"), the operator (ch-06 — "Stopped when operator reached for switch"), capture boxes for beginning and end. "In the event of interruption by the listed system, operator may maintain the diagnostic connection pending instruction" is the chapter's sharpest line — interruption as grounds to stay connected.
- Frank's unsigned motif continues: ch-10/11 he wouldn't sign the handover; here "I haven't signed it... It doesn't need my signature until they collect."
- The yard payoff: ch-12's map held the bus stop and the contractor's yard with neither marked as an authorized next location; now the inspector confirms it has been to the yard. The map was memory, not error.
- Anne's phone staying still at ten-nineteen — her service card remained suspended (ch-17), so the silence is the suspension made visible.
- "Can it make its own statement?" — the thesis beat. Ellen's "I can submit one" against Sarah's "That's not what I asked" is the book's document motif in miniature.
- The clock holds: ch-17 ends at 9:30; this chapter runs 10:03 → 10:19 → 10:41 → 10:48.
- "Will you be there?" / "At this square" / "Your next square is here?" "Not yet." — the inspector's spatial model holds together, and "I'll call you before lunch. Even if I haven't got permission" is the right promise to end on.

## Sam’s disposition

Ellen acts as review officer and refers urgent directions to the duty officer. The separate collection request governs the truck. David asks to sign in as a visitor. The lab facilities office is identified in both exchanges, and the inspector’s recollection now matches the bench and bearings in Chapter Two. The keep-list is preserved.
