# Rowan on Chapter Fifty-Nine

The bench sheet arriving as a photograph carries the chapter. Keep "Keep the number in," "He put down what she said," the finger leaving a mark on the screen, "Put that back" — "So she knows why we're asking now" — and the fitter needing the bench at noon putting a clock on the whole log. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **The 00:01 and 00:10 are never named as stopwatch readings.** Chapter Fifty-Eight's critique asked for the stopwatch to be read, and here the bench sheet carries the numbers — 00:01 beside 0.4 W, 00:10 beside 0 W — but the chapter never says which instrument wrote them. The margin's "two changes of minute, bench first" is plainly the clock record, so the reader can infer the pair are elapsed stopwatch times, and 00:10 minus 00:01 is nine seconds, the same interval Chapter Fifty-Six made load-bearing — but the chapter makes the reader do both the identification and the arithmetic. One word naming the stopwatch beside the times would settle it and let the nine-second match land instead of hovering.

2. **"Both times she spoke were before that one" can't be checked from the page.** The right sheet — the log's first rows — shows Startup with a white box under watts and a zero on the next line, but no time on either row. Sarah's finger does the work the times should do: if the log samples once a minute, the first zero row falls after 00:10 elapsed, and the inference holds — but the chapter never gives that row's time, so the ordering rests on Sarah's say-so rather than on anything shown. One timestamp on the log's first zero row would make the inference visible instead of asserted.

3. **The envelope now holds two Tuesdays.** Chapter Fifty-Seven put "Collection. Tuesday the seventeenth" on the envelope, and here Betty writes "Depot test, Tuesday" and puts the pencil down "without adding a number." This morning's test is the Tuesday after Monday the seventh of December — the eighth — a different Tuesday from the seventeenth, but the chapter never says so, and the reader who remembers the first envelope line has to guess whether Betty means the same Tuesday or the new one. One number, the eighth, would keep the two Tuesdays apart; as written the closing line leaves them stacked.

One small loose end: Betty's question — "Could we send these to the woman who put my sheet in?" — goes unanswered; Sarah turns back to the email without a yes, a no, or a later. One line would settle whether she's holding the thought until the log is safe.

One small continuity check: the nine-second interval matches Chapter Fifty-Six; the two changes of minute, bench first, match Chapter Fifty-Eight's clocks; plate 4173 goes in the first line of the reply, as the chapter has been careful to keep the number in; the log being cleared before the monitor goes out is the stores routine the book has already set up. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter. The ending has enough pressure to cut exactly where it does.
