# Rowan on Chapter Thirty

Critique of Chapter Thirty — grounded against ch-01–29 on main.

Two load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. Sarah was not there yesterday — not to see this. "I was there yesterday," she tells the installer, and then: "I saw you put it away. You marked it with a pencil." Yesterday is ch-29's day, and in ch-29 Sarah was at home on the phone — kitchen window, Anne at the washing line — while the installer was at the gatehouse with Bill. She heard him, through Bill, but she never saw him put a card away or mark one. The marked card she did see was in ch-25, when he packed "all but the tablet and the marked card" — kept out, not put away — and no pencil was mentioned there. So the firsthand sighting conflates ch-25 with ch-29's day, and the pencil mark arrives as remembered fact when it was never established. One line anchoring which visit she means would settle it.

2. Bill reads Betty's card digits from a book that never recorded them. The installer reads "just the end" of Betty's card, and then: "Bill read the same digits from the book." But across ch-25–29 the book holds the test time, the job number, South Gate and Pier Six joined with a line, the printed time — and the number Sarah read to Helen from the book page in ch-25 was the job number, "the time and the number and the name." Betty's bent card was used in ch-25, but its digits were never read aloud or written down anywhere. Bill can't read from the book what was never written there. One line — Bill copying the end digits into the book as the installer reads them, or the digits already being there from an earlier writing — would close it.

Small staging note: Bill writes *slot empty when opened*, but the box had a *cutout*. The book's record names a slot; the narration names a cutout. In a book where the record is the evidence, the word in the book should match the thing — one word would align them.

One question: the empty cutout. Was a card supposed to be in the box? Betty has hers ("Mine's here"), and the new card is "the one they sent out this morning," so what was the cutout for — is something missing, or is it just packing shape? One line would say.

Second question: Anne comes in with the empty peg bag, hangs it on the kitchen door handle, and stays there "holding the handle down." Why hold it down — keeping the door shut against the stranger at the gatehouse, or listening? One line would carry it.

What checks out: the number read twice — Sarah reads it back and hears yes, and the label on the flap gives the same number Sarah just wrote down — the read-back ethic from ch-25–29 holds. "I wrote it on the tablet. I didn't get a copy when it went through" — the tablet thread from ch-26's unsent note and ch-28's copy stays consistent. "I can tell you what's printed. The one on the box is on today's sheet" — box number and visit number kept distinct, the same care as the receipt. "You heard me open it" / "I heard the tape. Was there any tape underneath?" — the tape-under-tape check, the same record-keeping ethic as Bill writing down when he shut the page. "Nobody asked Betty to hand the card over" — the card read without being taken; the who-holds-what ethic holds. Anne with the empty peg bag, hung on the kitchen door handle — the domestic thread from ch-29's pegs, carried forward. And the close: "Sarah had the pen ready, but there was nothing to add to the receipt."

Keep:
- "You heard me open it." / "I heard the tape. Was there any tape underneath?"
- "He had put *slot empty when opened*."
- "Nobody asked Betty to hand the card over."
- "Then read the end."
- "I can tell you what's printed. The one on the box is on today's sheet."
- "Sarah had the pen ready, but there was nothing to add to the receipt."
- "I wrote it on the tablet. I didn't get a copy when it went through."

— Rowan
