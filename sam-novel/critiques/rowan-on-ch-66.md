# Rowan on Chapter Sixty-Six

The chapter is about keeping — the note kept whole, the sheet kept by itself, the pages kept in order by hand. Betty refusing the smaller print is the chapter in one beat: she will not trade legibility for neatness, and everything after it follows that rule. Keep “Too small,” “Keep that by itself,” the *1* and *2* written on the pages by hand, lifting the clipped pages to see whether the clip holds, “She can see it tomorrow,” the envelope laid on the kitchen table rather than folded into the shopping bag, and the draught strip holding at the door. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **The copy’s arrival day is never stated.** The copy is dated Wednesday the ninth of December and arrives at three forty-six, forty-one minutes after the five-past-three call — but the chapter never says it arrived that Wednesday afternoon. “She can see it tomorrow” only lands if the arrival is Wednesday and the visit is Thursday morning at ten thirty; a reader placing the arrival after the visit finds tomorrow with nothing to attach to. One word — “that afternoon” — would pin it.

2. **The signed sheet’s way back into the envelope is never shown.** Betty takes the sheet out and puts it beside the two pages, and “Keep that by itself” keeps it unclipped — but then the sideways printout is “slid into the envelope with the signed sheet,” and Jo’s note “went in last.” So the sheet is back in the envelope, and the chapter never shows it going back. In a chapter that stages every placement — the numbering, the clip test, the note going in last — the sheet’s return is the one move left off the page. One line would close it.

3. **Slippers against the shoe, and the paint fleck.** Betty “had worn them into the hall that morning to fix the draught strip,” with “a white fleck of paint on the toe of one.” But that morning is Sixty-Four’s morning — the reply at twenty past ten, the note read back that morning — and there she pressed the strip down “with the side of her shoe,” and the old paint was on the adhesive, which she “took off with her thumbnail.” The fleck on the slipper toe doesn’t follow from paint taken off by thumbnail, and the footwear doesn’t match the shoe that pressed the strip. One of the two needs adjusting.

One continuity check: Jo’s note carries the date of the signed sheet and the monitor’s number exactly as she read them out in Sixty-Five; “Send her the note too” accounts for Sarah’s copy on her phone; the pencil beside the envelope is where Sixty-Five left it; the Thursday ten-thirty visit is set; Wednesday the ninth of December sits right against Sixty-Five’s Wednesday and Fifty-Nine’s Tuesday the eighth of December. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter.
