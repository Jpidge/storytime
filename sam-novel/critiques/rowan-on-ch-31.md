# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-One

Critique of Chapter Thirty-One — grounded against ch-01–30 on main.

Two load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. "Yesterday's visit" is unanchored — and the chapter's own timeline makes it worse. The chapter opens "After Bill hung up," continuous with ch-30's call: same pen, same receipt, no day break. But dispatch wants the new number put against "yesterday's visit." In ch-30, "yesterday" was already used for the day before the box visit — the tablet page the installer wrote and never got a copy of ("I didn't get this page yesterday"). So is yesterday's visit that tablet-page visit, or the box visit — which would mean a day has passed with no marker? And Sarah's "both visits" — which two? The new number is for the box he took away in the box visit, so filing it against yesterday's visit either corrects the record or corrupts it, and the chapter doesn't say which. One line — a time marker at the open, or Helen naming the visit — would settle it.

2. The new number's kind is unclear, and the record it touches is never named. Sarah says the number is "for a box he took away," but dispatch wants it put "against yesterday's visit" — a box number against a visit record, when ch-30 kept the two distinct ("The one on the box is on today's sheet"). Is the new number a replacement for the box's number, the visit's number, or the missing card's number? And it goes on Helen's screen — "It goes away on my screen. I don't know where it goes after that" — while Bill's book, the record the whole book treats as evidence, is never mentioned. Does the replacement reach the book, or only the screen? Sarah asks for "both visits to stay where we can find them," but where is "where we can find them" — the book, the screen, or both? One line would say.

Small staging note: Helen asks "if the installer had left yet" and Sarah never answers — she moves straight to the dispatch message. Sarah was on the phone through the end of ch-30 and heard him take the box; a beat of an answer, or Helen not needing one, would close it.

One question: who is dispatch — and is it the same office that got the gate wrong in ch-29 ("they got it wrong")? The message asks Helen to replace a reference so the visit "would then appear under the correct record" — the same office that put Pier Six where South Gate belonged is now asking to rewrite a visit. Does Sarah trust the source, or is the distrust the point? One line would say.

Second question: the *accept* button. Helen doesn't press it — she answers by email instead — and the chapter ends with the email sent, the replacement pending, nothing pressed. Is the hold the point — the overwrite stopped, for now — or is dispatch meant to answer? The chapter doesn't say what happens next.

What checks out: "Neither of these" — the receipt has no place for Betty's number; the visit number and the box number are both accounted for, Betty's is not. "He wrote it down before the man took anything out" — matches ch-30's order: Bill wrote *cutout empty when opened* while the box was still on the desk, before the reader came out. "Can you ask without pressing it?" — the accept-button caution, and Helen answering by email instead of pressing. "Ask for both visits to stay where we can find them. They can tell us how the numbers are related." — the preservation instinct, the same as Bill keeping copies. "It goes away on my screen. I don't know where it goes after that." — the replacement's cost stated plainly. The peg bag thread: ch-29's pegs at the washing line, ch-30's empty bag on the door handle, now the torn seam and the blue thread — the domestic thread carried forward, and the bag as a carrier for Betty's card. "There was a last stitch with a long tail, held down by dirt" — the seam was never finished; the bag was never whole.

Keep:
- "Neither of these."
- "Ask for both visits to stay where we can find them. They can tell us how the numbers are related."
- "It goes away on my screen. I don't know where it goes after that."
- "Can you ask without pressing it?"
- "He wrote it down before the man took anything out."
- "There was a last stitch with a long tail, held down by dirt."
- "Sarah took the receipt off the table so Anne would have room to lay the whole seam flat."
- "The corner opened under its own weight."

— Rowan
