# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Four

Critique of Chapter Forty-Four — grounded against ch-01–43 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and three questions.

1. The substitute monitor is a different model, and the chapter records the difference without saying what it costs. Bill's monitor is grey, "with a dial where Jo's had had a light." Sarah reads the model number from the picture in the depot's reply and the last two letters are different from Bill's. Betty says "We can still see what yours does" — which is honest — but nobody says aloud what a reading on Bill's monitor proves about Jo's. The whole experiment exists to answer what Jo's monitor counted when it showed 0.4; a 1.8 on a different model shows what a monitor of this kind does, not what Jo's did. The book's rule is that a number is evidence only when it is shown against the same instrument. One line — someone naming that the models differ, so this test is a rehearsal rather than a reading of Jo's record — would carry the cost. Or show the two agreeing on something first.

2. The lag is caught but not yet assigned. The light's little window comes on so faintly Anne puts her palm above it to see, and the display stays at zero until Bill has got all the way up from his knees — then it shows one point eight. Anne names it: "That was on before the number." That is the chapter's best beat, and Betty's instinct is right: "Do that one again first. I want to see if it's late every time." But the chapter closes before the repeat, and it never asks whether the lateness belongs to the monitor (sampling, like the log's sixty-second interval in ch-43) or to the light (warming up to full draw). The repeat Betty orders is the test that would tell; the chapter ends with the test still to run.

3. The timer is introduced, explained, and never used — and its purpose is never stated. Bill brings a night-light and a timer; the light "gives it something to count," but nobody says what the timer is for in the test. Its pins are "pushed down all the way round" — a precise physical state that goes uninterpreted. Is it set to always-on, and if so why bring a timer at all? Is it meant to switch the light by itself? Betty defers it ("Do that one again first"), which is fair staging, but the chapter never says what the timer would prove when its turn comes. One line of purpose — what the timer is meant to show that the light alone can't — would close it.

Small staging note: the chair again. Bill puts the bag "on the chair by the socket" — Betty's chair, left beside the socket in ch-43 — then takes it off when Betty comes in. Later Betty "pulled the chair closer," and Anne stands "with one hand on the back of the chair." Three mentions, and it is never quite said whether it is the same chair throughout or where it ends up relative to the socket. In a book where the chair's position is a standing instruction ("leave chair"), a moved chair deserves a line — same note as ch-43, still open.

Questions:
- Whose monitor is Bill's, and where did it come from? The book shows provenance for its objects (the receipt, the sheet, the picture in the depot's reply); Bill's monitor arrives in his coat pocket with no history. If it is his own, one word would say so; if it is borrowed, from whom matters for whether its reading can be trusted.
- The picture at the bottom of the depot's reply: is it a photograph of the collected unit, or a picture of the model? Sarah reads the model number from it and the last two letters differ from Bill's — if the picture is the collected unit, its number is Jo's monitor's number, and the difference is a fact about the two units; if it is a catalogue picture, it is a fact about two models. The chapter doesn't say which.
- The depot's reply "came that morning" — but like the other records in this book it should carry a date. Does the reply itself date the answer, and does Sarah file it with the printout from ch-43? The printout went beside the envelope; where does the reply go?

Keep-list:
- The depot's answer as payoff: the figure in the watts column came from the socket on the front of the block, and the block's own supply was taken before that socket — the 0.4 can't be the block's draw, and the question Sarah promised to put in the email gets its answer.
- The empty-front control: Bill plugs his monitor in with nothing on the front, and they sit through not one zero but two — the control shown twice before the light goes in.
- Anne's eye: "That was on before the number" — the lag observed by the person watching the light, not the display.
- Betty running the test: "Wait. I want to see it again" and "Do that one again first. I want to see if it's late every time" — the repeat ordered before any conclusion.
- The model-number discipline: Bill turning the monitor so Sarah can read the last two letters herself; Sarah starting a new line for the number.
- The dial versus the light: Bill's monitor grey, with a dial where Jo's had a light — the difference shown in one physical detail.
- "It shouldn't show the block's draw" — Bill's careful "shouldn't," not "can't," keeping the depot's word as a claim rather than a fact.

## Disposition

Sam limited the substitute test to Bill’s model and added a repeat with a different delay. The text leaves its cause open and explains the clock test. The revision places the chair, identifies both instruments’ provenance, and dates and files the depot reply.
