# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Eight

Critique of Chapter Forty-Eight — grounded against ch-01–47 on main.

Two load-bearing points, three small staging notes, and two questions. Keep-list at the end.

**1. The second hand: ch-44's Sarah watches one at Betty's; ch-48 says Betty's kitchen clock lost its years ago.**

Ch-44: "Anne unplugged it. The window went dark straight away. Betty kept her eyes on the display. As soon as it went back to zero she told Anne to put the light in. Sarah watched the second hand. This time the number came up almost at once." The scene is Betty's kitchen — chair by the socket, night-light. The timer is still in the paper bag at that point ("Do you want to try the timer now?" comes just before, and Betty says do the light again first), so the second hand can't be the timer's dial. No watch is established for Sarah anywhere in ch-01–47 (Jo has one — ch-42, "checked her watch" — Sarah doesn't). The only clock in the room the reader knows is Betty's kitchen clock.

Ch-48: "Betty's kitchen clock had lost its second hand years ago and had a small hole where it had been."

Both can't stand — and this one matters structurally, because ch-48's whole justification for doing the test at the depot rather than at home rests on which clocks have second hands: Bill's wall clock has one, Betty's doesn't. The mechanism is good (see keep-list); the contradiction is in ch-44, not here. But it needs a fix in one of the two chapters — either ch-44's second hand belonged to something the reader can see (and nothing in that scene has one), or the kitchen clock keeps its second hand and the depot-test justification finds another reason. Note the knock-on: if ch-44 changes, its published post needs updating in place too.

**2. The "clock slow" slip doesn't say which clock — and the two readings point in different directions.**

The slip is clipped to the back of the depot book page and says *clock slow*, with no number beside the words. Betty asks "Slow by how much?" and Sarah enlarges the picture: nothing underneath but a ruled line. The chapter is honest that the magnitude is unknown, and that's fine — the open thread is the engine.

But the slip doesn't name its clock, and the reader only assumes the monitor's from where it's clipped. If the monitor's clock was slow, its pre-connection time was wrong but the 11:23 download time — taken from the bench computer — stands. If the *bench computer's* clock was slow, the 11:23 itself is suspect, and 11:23 is the chapter's one hard new fact. The slip also carries no date: was it written around the Thursday connection, or later? One word from Mo — "the monitor's clock" — or one line placing the slip would pin the referent so the reader knows which direction the doubt runs.

**Small staging notes**

- *"It had failed on Friday."* The only Friday in the thread is the twentieth (ch-43's email day), and Mo's "didn't know what it would have done on Wednesday" honestly bounds the battery evidence to the present — but the chapter never says "Friday the twentieth" in so many words. This book dates everything; one word would pin it.
- *"Anne pointed to the smaller figures."* Ch-47: Bill wrote "grey monitor" and "0 W" on the first line; Anne wrote "0.32 W" on the second. "Smaller figures" reads two ways — smaller handwriting (Anne's) or smaller values (0 < 0.32, which would be Bill's line, contradicting "I wrote that one"). The speaker tag resolves it, but "the smaller writing" or "my figures" would stop the reader doing the arithmetic.
- *"Mo had put the result of the battery test in the email."* The pictures arrive "on Saturday morning" and the battery result is "in the email" — the reader merges them into one message, but the chapter never quite says the pictures came by email. If they did, one word; if they came another way, say so.

**Questions**

1. "Ask them to write down both times before they plug it into the computer" — the monitor was already connected on Thursday and took the computer's time then. Is the plan to connect it again for the unplug test, so the instruction has a next occasion — or is Betty asking for something that can only happen on a future download? One line placing the next connection would firm it up.
2. Which Saturday? After ch-44's reply dated Monday the twenty-third, the next Saturday is the 28th; the Saturday after the Friday email is the 21st. The chapter doesn't date it — and ch-16 also used a bare "Saturday morning", so this may be deliberate style — but the battery-test Friday and the sending date of the three questions would let the reader place it. Is the gap between the questions and Mo's reply meant to be a day, or a week?

**Keep-list**

- Thursday the nineteenth, eleven twenty-three lands just after the Thursday nine-to-eleven collection window (ch-41/42) — the depot book quietly corroborates the collection day, and 11:23 sits 1h31m after Jo's nine fifty-two disconnection (ch-42/43). The timeline holds together without announcing itself.
- The three questions from ch-47 are answered in order: when connected (Thu 19th, 11:23), battery tested (failed Friday), time written down before the computer set it (no — hence Betty's write-both-times instruction). Clean closure of the ch-47 beat.
- Mo's "didn't know what it would have done on Wednesday" — the limit stated, not smoothed over. The battery evidence can't reach the Wednesday readings and the chapter says so.
- The pencil thread, tracked exactly: the short one from the groove, no string, against the kitchen pencil's string — and "Can I take the pencil back too?" is the right child-logic beat; she knows whose it is.
- The ridges: the pencil went through hard enough to leave ridges on the back — the physical fact that earns Anne reading the minute aloud from the reverse.
- The second-hand contrast as a mechanism: Bill's wall clock has one, Betty's doesn't — which is exactly why the test has to happen at the depot, not at home. The mechanism is sound, which is why point 1 needs fixing rather than the mechanism dropping.
- "Ask them to write down both times before they plug it into the computer. The time on it and the time they have." — Betty's instruction is the right next evidence, and it directly answers ch-47's third question.
