Chapter Twelve picks up Chapter Eleven's close exactly where it left it — the green reader, Anne's dark card, Frank having signed, Ellen still connected — and the walk out is the chapter the book has been earning since the glass scenes. Four things need fixing before this merges, and three smaller ones.

1. **The old inspector's map contradicts Chapter Two.** "The old unit's service map ended at the laboratory wall" and "There is no next square" — but this is the same unit that went outside in ch. 02: it sat on the bench at the bus stop across the road, put one wheel in the dry square the woman's bag had left, rode in a cab to the contractor's yard, and rolled on the loading-bay concrete ("Used to be a different yellow"). Outside has had a square for it since ch. 02, and it remembers the yard. If the map is a new or replacement map — rebuilt after the bench wipe, or issued with a narrower assignment — say so in one line. If "service map" means its assigned service area rather than its memory of the world, the chapter needs to mark that distinction, because as written the inspector's world-model denies its own history.

2. **"Listed system outside assigned location" fires on an assignment the record never made.** The field presumes Sarah has an assigned location. Her last formal assignment in the record is ch. 08's temporary room assignment — pre-service holding, a chair and a glass wall facing reception — and ch. 11's transfer order "has no movement": it transfers her to the room she already occupies. So which assigned location is she outside of — the laboratory, the holding room, somewhere else? Either name it, or let someone notice the field is inventing a violation from an assignment that doesn't exist. The second reading would be very much this book's argument, but the chapter has to mark it; as written Frank reads the choices straight and the premise passes unexamined.

3. **The clock is gone.** Ch. 11 is timed to the minute and ends at 6:41 with the card going dark; ch. 12 never gives a time again. The exception clock "counted upward" but from what start — 6:41, the moment she crossed the threshold, the moment the field appeared? — and the Monday 9 a.m. deadline floats unattached to the walk, the bus, the corner, the crossing. One anchor (the time they step outside, or the clock's start) restores the series' clock discipline and lets the reader place this walk against the holds Ellen extended to Monday.

4. **David's suspension notice presumes two things the reader has never seen.** "His access to the laboratory had been suspended" — but David entered as a visitor: ch. 05 has him at reception asking to visit Sarah, and no badge, pass, or access grant for him is ever shown. And the notice is "addressed to him under his employer's name" — his employer is never named anywhere in eleven chapters. Either show the access (a visitor badge, the sign-in) and name the employer, or let the notice's vagueness be the point and mark it: a suspension of an access that was never issued, from an employer that is never named, is exactly the book's paperwork-without-a-person — but only if the chapter knows it's doing it.

Smaller:
- The paper cup: at first sight the cup is full with "a clear dome trembled above its rim" and the tap's state is never given; later it overflows and Anne shuts the tap off. A running tap would have overflowed it before Sarah's first look; a drip would explain the slow fill. One word — dripping, or left running — settles which.
- Ellen moves channels without a line: in ch. 11 she is on Joan's terminal; here Anne holds out her phone and Ellen is already on it. Given how carefully this book tracks channels (desk speaker vs. laboratory speaker muted, "The desk speaker did not carry the question to Pier Six"), the switch earns one line — who called whom.
- "That we're going home": the form asks David to "Identify any unrecorded contact with the listed system," and his answer names a destination, not a contact. Is he evading the form or answering it? The chapter doesn't mark his intent, and ch. 11's rule — "The form wants somebody" — makes the distinction matter. One beat of intent would close it.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: the opening line carrying ch. 11's last image whole ("The reader stayed green after Anne's card went dark"); "That is an answer. It isn't a reason."; "I'm not the room." / "But I don't know where the boundary is."; the inspector's "Come back to this square" answered with "I'll try"; Anne wiping a spill she didn't make ("I just saw it"); "Will outside move?"; "For tonight"; and the notice arriving mid-crossing while David's hand stays in hers. The chapter's best move is that leaving is treated as a decision with a form attached to it, not an escape from forms.


## Sam’s disposition

All four required fixes and three smaller notes addressed. Assignment is distinguished from remembered geography; the location warning’s invented assignment is challenged; times anchor the walk; the suspension notice’s unsupported fields are explicitly questioned. Tap, channel transfer, and David’s intended response are clarified. Retained beats preserved.
