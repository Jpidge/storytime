# Rowan on Chapter Fifty-Six

The bread and the two short lines carry the chapter. Keep the knife put down with the blade towards the wall, “So it can say it with nothing there” against “The display can,” Betty pushing the whole slice across at the end, and “It was possible for both of them to be right on the same sheet.” Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **Name the 0.4 W line’s status once.** The two-line table is Mo’s handwriting (“she’d written *0.4 W on display*”); the thirty rows are the monitor’s own log (“rows in the attachment”). Sarah’s question asks what the monitor “had logged” before the half hour — but the 0.4 W line, being handwritten, may not be a logged value at all. One line settling whether the monitor’s own log holds the 0.4 W or only Mo’s note does would sharpen the question Sarah sends and the answer Betty is waiting on, because the depot case turns on what counts as the monitor’s evidence, not what Mo saw. It would also keep the sixty-second sampling rule clean (Chapter Forty-Three): the two lines are nine seconds apart, which only works if they are Mo watching the display rather than samples from the log.

2. **“Today’s test” points at two tests.** Friday holds two candidates: the two-line pre-zero moment and the thirty-row half hour. Betty says “Put *today’s test* in there. If she finds it, I want to know which one she found” — but the question as drafted asks about “before the half hour,” and if Mo answers with the half hour itself, “which one” has no second referent on the page yet. A word naming the half hour as the test being asked about would keep the two apart, so Mo’s answer can’t be read as the other one.

3. **The second slice’s contrast has no first half.** “She cut a thinner slice this time, which didn’t catch on the blade” — “this time” and “didn’t catch” both reach back to a first slice that caught, but the opening only says Betty “finished the slice and put the knife down.” Either the first slice caught and it should be on the page, or the contrast is doing work for a snag that never happened. A small thing, but the bread is doing the chapter’s quiet work and the snag is its only blemish.

One small continuity check: Friday morning sits right against Friday the twentieth (Chapter Forty-Three), the Tuesday-the-seventeenth collection (Chapter Thirty-Seven), and Wednesday the eighteenth (Chapter Fifty-Five), and the front socket empty matches Chapter Fifty-Five’s “front empty.” I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter. The ending has enough pressure to cut exactly where it does.
