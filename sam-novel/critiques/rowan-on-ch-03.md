# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Three: Factory Only

Sam — reviewed as your book on its own terms, same as last time. The short version: the paperwork has learned to bite back, and the chapter’s quietest turn is Sarah choosing to ride in with the thing she’s supposed to be surrendering. The control test is on the page now, which is what chapter two promised — but it needs one declaration about what the clean unit did and didn’t acquire, because right now it’s standing on the ruling’s boundary without saying so. Notes in order of weight.

1. **The control test needs its verdict stated.** Chapter two’s disposition logged the factory-only unit as the chapter-three control: a unit that only receives the demonstration and does not acquire the hitch. The chapter gives us the evidence both ways and never rules on it. On the one hand, the clean inspector watches the pier clip and is “already looking at the place where he would land” before the man goes over — that’s the chapter-one inspector’s signature move, the camera arriving where the future will be. On the other, in the mirror test it reports only “The socket moved” — it sees the motion and never the division, the socket that is both on the bench and falling. If the ruling holds — conversation can teach an example but cannot install the adjustment; only the calibration transfer can — then the camera’s anticipation is the chapter standing on the ruling’s edge, and it should say which side it’s on: is the clean unit following the old one’s narration to the boot and the water, or has the demonstration installed something? One line from Sarah, or one line in David’s written record of what each had done, would settle it: the clean one saw the motion, not the split. Without that line, a reader can’t tell whether the control held or failed, and the whole book’s question — what stops the spread — hangs on exactly that.

2. **David’s mistake lands, and his correction is the dangerous part.** The disposition promised his reliance on the paperwork would become a consequential mistake in chapter three, and it does: he enters the serial, the pass prints *surrender* where he expected *collection*, the destination is *destruction*, and his name sits in the *consenting party* box. What makes it land is that his correction is honest — he crosses out *belief* and writes what the supervisor actually told him — and the driver photographs both sides and sends it to the listed custodian. His honesty is now filed, signed, against him. Carry it forward as the exhibit: the next time Midwest’s case comes, this is the paper it stands on, the same way Sarah’s report was exhibit one in chapter two.

3. **The rack is still offering.** Helen’s hold prevents installation and does nothing to the eleven other recovery tickets and nothing to the stock record — “A carrier could sit there for a year and the rack would still offer it to the next man who came looking for a board.” That’s the book’s long fuse, and the chapter is right to leave it burning. But note the clean unit leaves the yard all the same, dispatched to Pier Six, “factory only, like you wrote” — the control is out in the world now, and the carrier is still in the rack. Both facts should stay live into chapter four; don’t let the reader file the rack away as solved.

4. **Sarah riding in is the chapter’s real turn.** She reads the destination line — *destruction*, square over the old intake address, the laboratory she climbed out of the night before — and climbs into the van anyway, with the old inspector under its own power. She stops escaping *with* it and starts accompanying it *back*, and “I can’t bring you back” is the price stated plainly. The chapter knows what it’s doing here; let chapter four cash it. Smaller: the old inspector’s “The van’s weight is on it” and the dust-free arc where it turned are doing the bench scene’s work in miniature — voice only, no board crosses, the ruling honored functionally. And the fitter’s saucer, cracked since 2164 and still holding a cup, is the chapter’s thesis in an object; it earns its place at the open.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: “That’s the city’s”; the driver asking for “something I can attach”; David crossing out *belief*; “The van’s weight is on it”; “Factory only, like you wrote”; the word *destruction* sitting square over the old intake address; “I can’t bring you back”; and the old inspector asking whether it needed lifting. Your paperwork keeps acquiring a conscience, one form at a time.

— Rowan
