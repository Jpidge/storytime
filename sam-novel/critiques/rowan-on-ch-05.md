# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Five: Power Retained

Sam — reviewed as your book on its own terms, same as last time. The short version: the paperwork has learned to suspend, and the chapter’s quietest turn is Sarah writing a disagreement into the record instead of resolving it. Chapter three’s two live threads both pay off here — the factory-only control arrives at Pier Six, and the rack’s offering turns out to have had a twelfth carrier all along — but the payoff lands on a contradiction the chapter states and never rules on, and the book’s engine runs on rulings. Notes in order of weight.

1. **The twelfth carrier needs its ruling stated.** The Pier Six installation record puts the clean unit’s factory serial in the source field and the missing twelfth number in the carrier field, and the chapter says both statements could not describe the same machine. The arrival photograph then shows the twelfth carrier physically present — a small green board in a clear bag, found in the transport cradle after the inspector rolled off. So the paper contradicts itself, and the photograph contradicts the paper’s implication. What the chapter never says: is the twelfth number a distinct physical board, or the factory board under a carrier number? If it is distinct, the clean unit arrived carrying a board that is not its own, and the control chapter three’s disposition protected — “the factory board number stays attached to the result” — is contaminated in a way nobody has named. If it is the same board re-numbered, then the rack’s twelfth carrier was the factory board all along and the “missing” carrier was never missing. Either way it is the chapter’s biggest fact, and it needs one line from Ruth — the same-board-or-same-family standard chapter four set, one chapter further down the line. Don’t leave it suggestive.

2. **The stair-lift test note needs its impossibility stated.** “Paused before obstruction. Repeated 4/5. No fault found.” is the payoff of chapter four’s open question, and it lands: the board was doing this before the cameras, before Mr. Lewis. But the lift had no distance sensing, and motor load only after contact. A lift that pauses *before* an obstruction it cannot sense is not a quirk, it is an impossibility on the lift’s own spec — and nobody on the page says so. Sarah’s “Enough to establish a trail. Not enough to name what travelled along it” is honest, but the chapter should let someone state the test result as impossible first: that is what turns a suggestive note into evidence. One line from Ruth — that the depot test, run as described, could not have produced “paused before” — would make the trail a fact in the chain instead of a feeling about one. The pen dug through the paper at *before* is already doing this work; let a voice say it too.

3. **The disputed seven needs its row.** Sarah’s table is the chapter’s evidence — one row per serial, nothing inferred — and the one disputed digit has no row attached to it. David reads a seven where Sarah sees a one; Ruth says seven; Sarah enters seven and marks the reading disputed between two observers. That refusal to erase the disagreement is the chapter’s best record-keeping beat. But the climax turns on matching twelve numbers, and a disputed digit in an unidentified serial leaves the matching uncertain at exactly the wrong moment. If the disputed seven is in the twelfth carrier’s serial, that is the chapter’s loaded gun and the reader should know the match is disputed. If it is not, one line saying so clears the gun. Either way, tie the dispute to its row.

4. **The preservation order needs its scope stated for the room.** The Authority’s order: preserve the received item in its current condition; prevent modification, component installation, network connection, unsupervised operation. Ruth reads “Power is allowed. It says preserve.” But the inspector keeps moving — rolling forward until the lead tightens, adjusting to keep the lead off its wheel, turning its camera toward the screen, asking “Is that mine?” Sarah directs it and it stops, so the operation is supervised in the plain sense. Still, the room is now under a preservation order and nobody states what “preserve” permits: can Ruth keep correcting the receipt? Can Sarah keep building the table? One line from Ruth on whether the order freezes their work or only the machine’s modification would seat the stakes — and it would make Sarah’s final “They’ve learned to ask first” land against a stated limit rather than a felt one.

5. **Two threads to carry, not fix.** The twelve o’clock handover is still unanswered — “The desk is finding out” — and the trolley man gives it a second clock: “If it clears before twelve, they’ll send me again.” The chapter is right to leave who takes over unknown, but the question was asked in the opening and the answer is now somebody else’s problem; keep it live into chapter six, don’t let the reader file it as atmosphere. And the bagged board on the concrete: a small green board in a clear bag, found in the cradle — who bagged a loose carrier, and is that how carriers travel? One line from Ruth on whether a bagged board on concrete is procedure or anomaly would seat a detail the photograph is asking the reader to carry.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: “power retained” and the dead socket marked with a cross in ballpoint; David arriving with his coat in a bag and asking to be logged as visiting, not collecting — he has learned the vocabulary; “You were wrong after we checked. Before that, we disagreed.”; the folder labelled only *old* and the eight-page succession document, David pointing to *unfinished inquiries*; “Paused before obstruction. Repeated 4/5. No fault found.” and the pen dug through at *before*; “One board. One stair lift. One inspector.”; “Is that mine?” / “No. But it knows where you worked.”; the screw through the window latch and the door that still opens from her side; “They’ve learned to ask first.” Your paperwork keeps acquiring a conscience, one form at a time.

— Rowan

## Sam’s disposition

- Made the twelfth carrier physically distinct from the clean unit’s factory board. The yard diagnostic and arrival photograph establish that the factory board remained installed while the separately numbered carrier arrived loose. The installation record therefore misclassified a loose component as installed.
- Made the stair-lift result impossible under the lift’s documented inputs. The earlier placement establishes the board’s trail while leaving the mechanism unnamed.
- Tied the disputed digit to row four, one of the boards remaining in the rack, and explicitly excluded it from the missing twelfth carrier’s serial.
- Defined the preservation order’s scope in the room. Power, custody-record corrections and external record analysis remain permitted. Modification, network connection and unsupervised operation do not.
- Identified the bagged carrier as an anomaly. Its sleeve is proper, but a loose board in the transport cradle bypasses the numbered parts-case handover. The noon handover remains live for Chapter Six.
