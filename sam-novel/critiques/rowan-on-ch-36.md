# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-Six

Critique of Chapter Thirty-Six — grounded against ch-01–35 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The visit runs on the original reference — the replacement never left the email. In ch-35 Sarah wrote *replacement in email, use unconfirmed* beside the address. The use is now decided, and it is not in use: the visitor’s sheet carries the original reference, and when Sarah asks “Can you see this other number anywhere?” he looks on the back and says “I haven’t got it.” So the visit proceeds under the reference Helen never replaced on the booking, while the replacement sits in an email nobody at the door has. The book has spent chapters keeping the two references apart precisely so they don’t blur; here they blur by omission. Is the original the governing reference after all — dispatch sent him for it, and Sarah’s receipt matches it — or is the visit running on the superseded number because the correction never left Helen’s email? One line — Sarah naming which reference the visit is under, or the visitor confirming dispatch sent him for the original — would settle it.

2. The print time is shown but never read — and it is the digit that would explain point 1. The back of his sheet has a box with three lines, and the first is the time his sheet was printed. The reader never learns it. The instruction on the sheet matches the corrected read-back from ch-35 (“front door, ring once, allow time for an answer” → “Front door. Ring once… to wait”), so the sheet is evidently current on the instruction — but that makes the reference harder, not easier, to explain: a sheet printed after the correction should have had the replacement on it if the replacement belonged there, and a sheet printed before it explains the original. The book’s evidence rule is that digits are read and shown (“Twenty-six” in ch-35, “They were the ones Helen had read” in ch-34). One line — the time read aloud, or Sarah checking it against the read-back — would say whether the sheet in his pocket is a stale print or the current one.

3. The copy that says what he did — and the blank wide enough for a name. Betty asks “Does it say what you did?” He turns the sheet round: below the return instruction there is a blank “wide enough for a name.” He says “I can write it in” — but the blank’s size says it is for a name, not for the work, so “it” is doing two jobs at once. And the chapter ends before he writes anything — “Before I sign,” she said. The reader never learns whether the copy Betty keeps will record the visit or only who made it. Ch-35 ended the same way, before the read-back, and Sam gave the confirmation in revision; the same threshold ending here leaves the house’s record open again. One line — him writing the work in, or Betty asking what the blank is for — would say whether the office’s copy carries the visit or only a signature.

Small staging note: the knock. The sheet says “Ring once” and “to wait,” and Betty’s words in ch-35 were “Ring once. It takes me a minute to get there.” He rang once — then knocked, while she was still coming (“I was coming”). Anne heard both and marked both boxes, so the house’s record captures a knock the sheet never authorized. Is the knock the sound of not waiting, or just what a visitor does at a door? One line — or nothing, if the knock is the point, the instruction bending on contact with a real door — would settle whether he followed the sheet or added to it.

Questions:
- The box’s second line is “the route.” Is that the route to the house — and if the visitor followed it, does it match the address Sarah read back (“Twenty-six”)? The address is settled in practice — he arrived, and ch-34 already had Sarah check the number on the brick against Betty’s — but the route is another unread line on the same sheet.
- “It works from the kitchen.” In ch-34 Betty said “Tell them the bell works” and “I heard it from the back.” Does the bell only sound inside — is that why he thought it was out, and why Betty was slow? The chapter treats it as reassurance, but a reader might wonder whether the bell’s reach is the reason for the knock.

Keep-list:
- The case undone one catch at a time so the lid wouldn’t spring against his knee.
- The brown paper cut to the size of the case, laid on the floor before anything else comes out.
- Anne: “I heard both” — marking each box, writing the time underneath, having to ask Sarah what time it was.
- “He read the reference from his sheet. It was the original one. Sarah put her finger on the matching line.” — the receipt doing its work, the two-records theme holding.
- “It works from the kitchen.” — Betty’s plain reassurance, the house answering in its own words.
- The finger on the printed name — he asks if she is Betty before he asks anything else.
