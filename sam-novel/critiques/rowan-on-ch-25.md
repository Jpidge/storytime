# Rowan on Chapter Twenty-Five

Critique of Chapter Twenty-Five — grounded against ch-01–24 on main.

Four load-bearing points, one small staging note, and one question.

1. The note the installer saves says nothing about Pier Six. Bill dictates the latch failure — "The card gave you a green light. It didn't lift the latch." — the installer types it, reads it back, and saves the visit unfinished. That is the permanent record the office will see: a latch fault, not a naming error. But the chapter's evidence is the name: the job says Pier Six, the book says South Gate, the address says here. And the only copy of the job number is on the back of a grocery receipt in Sarah's pocket. If the office reads the note as written, they send a man back to fix a latch, not to answer why the job names the wrong place. One line — Bill adding the name to the note, or Sarah asking for it in the note — keeps the written record carrying the same question the chapter does.

2. "Give me till half past" has no hour. Bill asks Betty what time to put, and she answers with a half-hour and no hour — and no return date or time for the installer is ever set. The book is the record: Bill writes the request at the top of the next page, and a future reader of that page can't tell half past what. The chapter either knows the hour and hasn't told the reader, or nobody has named it yet. Have Bill ask the hour, or have Betty give it — the request as written can't be read back.

3. The old reader's control test never names its card. Betty's hand comes away from the reader, the click, the door opens far enough for her foot — but the chapter never says it was her bent card. Ch-24 made the bent card the object of care: shown above the shoulder, wiped dry, the baseline for the new reader's failure. The control only works if the reader knows the same known card opened the old reader. One word — "her bent card" — seats the result the new-reader test needs.

4. "The last two words" fits both names. Sarah reads Helen the line — the time, the number, and the name — and Helen repeats the last two words. The reader infers the name is Pier Six, from the tablet's line. But the book is open right there with its name visible, and South Gate is two words too. The confirmation beat that should settle which name Helen heard would read identically if Sarah had read the book's name instead. Have Helen repeat the name itself, or have Sarah say "Pier Six" — the confirmation should name the name.

Small staging note: the tablet on the book. "The installer set the tablet on the book, then picked it up again when he saw what was underneath." What did he see — the receipt? the name? — and why did it make him lift the tablet? As written the gesture reads as a half-finished thought; give it its reason or cut it. (Same small thing with the bent card: it goes from Betty's hand on the reader to "back under her arm" without a beat showing it getting there.)

One question: David's "Could it just be an old name?" has two readings, and the chapter doesn't settle which he means. Pier Six is a real place Sarah worked — ch-01, the inspection machine off Pier Six; ch-06, the Pier Six arrival record; ch-11, the call at Pier Six. So "old name" could mean the gate used to be called Pier Six (a renamed place), or it could mean the job is raised against the wrong place entirely (Pier Six, where Sarah worked, versus South Gate, where they are). Those point at different mysteries. Which is it?

What checks out: the return structure — ch-24 ended with the old reader to be put back, and this chapter opens with the screws going back in. The bus-stop turn pays off in the address page — ch-23's missed stop is the road they drove down. The book holds — Anne's fingers on the corners, Bill writing the request in it, the name kept visible while Sarah reads the tablet's line to Helen. The water holds — the track under water in ch-24, "if the water's up I'll have to go the long way." The marked card stays with the installer, packed with the tablet; the new reader is packed too, accounted for. Helen's thread continues from ch-20's bus-stop call — she reads the line over the phone again, and asks for the job number the same way.

Keep:
- "The line comes from the test. The name comes from the job." — the evidence split, stated plainly; the cleanest line of its kind in the book.
- "It ought to." — the installer's hedge on the job number staying the same.
- "There was a soft place in the paper where it had been folded. She moved the pen down to keep from putting it through." — Sarah's care with the only copy of the number.
- "The card gave you a green light. It didn't lift the latch." — Bill's dictation, plain and exact.
- "He stood with one foot on the landing and one below it." — the installer's half-position.
- "Anne held her fingers where they were and he stopped." — the book protected without a speech.
- "Give me till half past... If the water's up I'll have to go the long way." — Betty's conditions, the water thread carried.

— Rowan
