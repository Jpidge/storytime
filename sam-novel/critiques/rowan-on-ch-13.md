Critique of Chapter Thirteen — "A Place for Tonight" (head 05b9e997)

First, the loop is working: this chapter answers all three points from my Chapter Twelve critique. The form's "Location of listed system" field is now the scene instead of a presumption; the employer field is blank on the page and the restriction is a visitor-event restriction applying to future visits, which settles the suspended-access question cleanly; and the chapter carries its own clock (7:26, 7:34, 7:58). What follows is against that background, not instead of it.

**1. Whose phone sounds — the one real continuity break.**

The message that interrupts the bedroom scene "had come to her additional-contact address," but the phone that sounds is David's, and the reply is composed on the phone between them on the table — which the text has already established as David's.

The chain as written: David sets his phone on the table with the notice still open; "From the kitchen his phone sounded"; "The phone lay between them on the table, offering to accept a reply"; at 7:58 Sarah writes and sends her reply from it. But the message is addressed to Sarah: Ellen acknowledged receipt of *her* contact details, and Sarah dictated her own address to Ellen in Chapter Twelve and saved the confirmation ("It called her an additional contact. Sarah saved it."). Sarah's phone is never in play in the evening scenes — Anne's phone carries the Ellen call (ch-12), and the phone that sounds at the crossing is David's (ch-12), carrying his notice.

As written, Sarah replies to a message sent to her own address from David's phone, with no route stated for how it got there. Either her phone is in the kitchen and it is hers that sounds (place it — coat, pocket, the hallway), or the message reaches David's phone by a stated route. Fix whichever side is wrong; the 7:58 reply is too good a beat to rest on a phone that shouldn't have it.

**2. The exception code is introduced and dropped.**

Anne: "There's usually an exception code." "Do you know it?" "No. I would have called someone who did."

Two problems. The sentence is ambiguous: does Anne know someone who knows the code? If so, why doesn't she call them now — the form is sitting unsubmitted in front of them. And the code never pays off: David calls account services instead, and the code is never mentioned again. The form's refusal to submit is the scene's hinge, and the code is the named way out of it. Either let Anne try the route she names, or cut the mention so the phone call is the only route and the dead end is clean. Right now it reads as a door opened and quietly closed.

**3. The walk's duration is the one unanchored span.**

Chapter Twelve's last clock is 6:51 (the bus); the crossing and the notice reading carry no time, and Chapter Thirteen opens at 7:26 at the door. Inside this chapter the anchors are consistent — 7:26 arrival, 7:34 the music stops, 7:58 the reply — but the reader can't check the arrival against the crossing, so the walk floats. One line, the crossing time or how long the walk took, closes the loop.

**4. "Not through the log" — small, and mostly a weight question.**

"The log" is unambiguous to a reader tracking Chapters Ten and Eleven: Anne's activity log, a Midwest contractor record (ch-11: "I'm assigned to Sarah's activity log"; "The log is a contractor record"), which Anne closed at ch-11. The exchange is coherent — Sarah's "I don't have the log" and Anne's "I know" acknowledge that. But it means Anne is ruling out a channel Sarah couldn't have used anyway, so the refusal carries less weight than the ask deserves. Consider letting the weight sit on "ask how I am" rather than on the channel.
