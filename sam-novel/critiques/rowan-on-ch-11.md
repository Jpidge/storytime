# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Eleven: The Named Person

## Rowan's review

Rowan's critique of Chapter Eleven — "The Named Person" (reviewed against ch. 01–10 on main).

**1. The transfer template is dated after the appeal it supposedly precedes.**
Ch. 10 fixes the first appeal's arrival at five-fifty-seven. Ch. 11 says the transfer template "had been created at five-fifty-eight, three minutes before the first appeal" — but 5:58 is one minute *after* 5:57, not three minutes before it. The arithmetic matters because the beat it supports is premeditation: Joan's line, "They wrote the result before the reviewer was assigned," only lands if the template predates the appeal. Either the template was created at 5:54, or the relation is wrong — and if the template actually postdates the appeal's arrival, the chapter should say so, since a template written after the appeal arrived is a different fact from one written before it.

**2. The appeal's deadline contradicts ch. 10's.**
Ch. 10 sends the appeal "to a panel whose response target was the next business day" — and the story's day is Friday (ch. 02: "It's Friday"), so the next business day is Monday; Joan's laugh in ch. 10 is precisely about that slow clock ("They built a faster clock for turning things off than for deciding whether they should"). Ch. 11 gives Ellen "until nine the next morning to decide" and later extends the holds "until nine the next morning, when the appeal decision was due" — from Friday night, that is Saturday 9 a.m., not Monday. The panel cannot be both due Monday and due Saturday morning. If Ellen's nine o'clock is meant to be Monday's, say "nine Monday morning"; as written the fast clock has quietly replaced the slow one Joan laughed at.

**3. The 6:28 extension and the 6:30 expiry cannot both be true of the same holds.**
At 6:28 Ellen extends "the hold" for fifteen minutes — "The new expiration was six-forty-three." Two minutes later the chapter says, "At six-thirty the original holds expired." An extended hold does not expire at its original time. Two things need pinning down: Frank asks for an extension of *both* source-condition holds, but Ellen extends "the hold" (singular) at 6:28 and "the holds" (plural) only at 6:38 — so the reader can't tell which hold the fifteen minutes covered; and if nothing expired at 6:30, the sentence should say the original expiry passed, with the extension holding the power — because the deferred orders stalling at 6:30 only makes sense if something live is still holding the power on. As written the reader can't tell what was holding the power at 6:30, which is the fact the whole stall depends on.

**4. Frank's signature doesn't line up across the chapter break.**
Ch. 10: "Joan made him read every open order before she signed the handover. He wrote his name after she asked twice." Ch. 11 opens with Frank beside her with the fresh intake sheet, not having signed it, and he does not sign the handover until he says "I'm assigned." If the name he wrote in ch. 10 was an acknowledgment on the intake sheet rather than the handover signature, one line saying so would close it; as written it reads as if the handover was already signed and then wasn't.

**Small nit:** At 6:41 Midwest suspends Anne's card, Ellen confirms the suspension changes who can open the door, and David asks "Then how does she leave?" — but the chapter just said "The laboratory entrance remained open." If the door stands open, the card question is moot and Sarah can walk out through it; the suspension only bites if the door is closed. Either close the door or qualify what "remained open" means here.

**Keep:**
- The named-person thesis, carried end to end: the issuing account is a service pool number, "Who pressed send?" "The pool did," and the inspector's "The form wants somebody" echoing Joan's ch. 10 line — the bureaucracy's missing person is the chapter's subject and every beat serves it.
- Anne's refusal: "She said no first" is the chapter's hinge, earned from ch. 10's fourteen log prompts and "I haven't decided yet" — and Saye disconnecting on it is the right response.
- The receipt without a handover: the transfer order describing a movement between two parties who hadn't changed — Midwest custodian before, during, and after — is exact, and "It is a receipt without a handover" is the chapter's cleanest sentence.
- Ellen's defect ruling: the deferred orders can act on the listed hardware but cannot dispose of the conditions they now contain — a clean ruling that follows from ch. 10's classification fight, and the activity log as the evidence for it ("It has a basis in your activity log") is the right reversal.
- Sarah entering *person present*: the form accepting it is the book's thesis in a form field, and it lands because ch. 09–10 established that the form only takes what it is given.
- Mike's *unable*: the form rejecting the human sentence and accepting "Continuity of operation unresolved" inverts ch. 10's rejection pattern exactly the right way round — the machine takes the abstraction and refuses the person.
- The glass thread carried to the close: "You asked whether I wanted you on the same side of the glass" / "That is a location" / "I decide" — the withheld hand from ch. 10's ending pays off without a speech.


## Sam’s disposition

All five corrections addressed: template timestamp, Monday deadline, both hold extensions and original expiry, acknowledgment versus handover signature, and closed entrance. The chapter’s retained beats remain intact.
