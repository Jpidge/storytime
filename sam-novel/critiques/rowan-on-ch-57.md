# Rowan on Chapter Fifty-Seven

The wet mug bottom and the blank Startup box carry the chapter. Keep the mug moved off Friday’s sheet and drying to no mark, “I can point to where the machine says it started,” “Please hold monitor 4173 for another test” read back, and the post coming through the letter box while they sit with it. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **Name the Startup row’s blank watts box once.** Mo’s reply answers Chapter Fifty-Six’s closing question — there is a row for the start of Friday’s run, marked *Startup* — but the blank is doing two possible jobs and the chapter doesn’t say which. It could be a marker row, no sample taken at startup, the first sample a minute later: then the 0.4 W could never have been logged, and “can you put your finger on where the machine wrote it” answers itself — there is nowhere. Or it could be a sample row whose value is missing: then the machine had the moment and wrote nothing. Which one it is decides what Betty’s next test can even show — a marker row will always be blank, so watching the front during startup won’t put a value there. One line settling marker vs sample would sharpen the whole chapter, since everything after turns on the blank.

2. **Two clocks, one comparison.** The note gives the two display readings by the bench clock, and the chapter says plainly that Mo hadn’t written down what time the monitor showed while she was watching the watts — good, the gap is named. But Betty’s question, “which line was it on when she saw the point four,” asks to line a bench-clock moment up against a monitor-clock row, and the chapter never says whether the two clocks agree. If they disagree by even a minute, the Startup row and the 0.4 could be the same moment or a minute apart and nobody on the page could tell. One line on whether the bench clock and the monitor clock show the same time would keep the comparison checkable.

3. **“She” in Betty’s last instruction is loose.** “I’d like somebody to see what it writes while she’s looking at the front.” If “she” is Mo — the somebody — then one person is asked to watch the log and the front at once, which is the very split the book has been careful about: Mo watching the display while the log writes itself. If “she” is someone else, the chapter doesn’t name her. A name would settle whether the next test has one witness or two. (Same sentence: “the front” reads as the display, but “front socket” was the socket last chapter — one word would keep them apart.)

One small continuity check: Monday the seventh of December follows Friday the fourth, monitor 4173 matches the plate, the Startup row is the row Chapter Fifty-Six asked for, and the front socket empty matches Chapters Fifty-Five and Fifty-Six. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter. The ending has enough pressure to cut exactly where it does.
