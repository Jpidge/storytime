# Rowan on Chapter Twenty-Six

Critique of Chapter Twenty-Six — grounded against ch-01–25 on main.

Four load-bearing points, one small staging note, and one question.

1. The job says "complete" for work nobody finished. The installer saved the visit "Unfinished" — ch-25, his own word — and Helen's screen said the job "hasn't started." Then the note comes through and the job flips to "complete," with no one marking it complete and nothing in the chapter finishing the work: the latch fault stands, the visit was never closed. Sarah asks "Does it still say it hasn't started?" and takes "No. It says complete" as good news, and the chapter moves on to the signature. But the office record now says a job nobody finished is finished — the same record-keeping failure the book has been tracking since ch-25, where the written note carried a latch fault instead of the naming question. The status changed because the note arrived, not because the work was done. One line — Sarah asking "Complete? He never finished it" — keeps the record honest, and keeps the reader from taking the flip at face value.

2. The signature space disagrees with itself. Helen's screen shows a dash in the signature space; the gatehouse photograph shows "the same space under Pier Six, white from one end to the other." A dash is a mark; white is no mark. The chapter presents the photograph as confirming the screen — Sarah opens it to check the same space — but the two records show different things: one has something in the signature space, the other has nothing. Which is the true record? If the dash is the system's placeholder for an unsigned job, say so; if the photograph is of a different copy, say which copy. As written, the reader can't tell whether the signature space is marked or blank, and the chapter treats a disagreement as an agreement.

3. The paperclip collapses the note and the test into one mark. "There's a paperclip. That could be the note or it could be the test." Helen can see there is an attachment and can't open it from that screen — and can't say which attachment it is. Sarah crosses out "note received" and writes "paperclip," which records the doubt honestly. But the chapter then proceeds as if the note arrived: the job says complete, the signature is checked, the book number is fetched. If the paperclip is the test — the testing from ch-24 — then the note may not have come through at all, and "It's come through" is Helen seeing an attachment she can't identify. The chapter needs to decide what "come through" means: either give Helen a way to tell them apart (a name on the attachment, another screen), or let Sarah's record carry the doubt forward instead of the chapter quietly resolving it.

4. The book number arrives on the receipt without its moment. Helen asks for "the book number. The one he typed first." Sarah turns the receipt over and reads a number David copied — "the line David had pressed into it when he copied the number," ink come away on her knee at the gate. But no chapter shows David copying a number, or the installer showing him one to copy: the job number's provenance is established (the receipt is the only copy, ch-25), the book number's is not. The chapter's closing turn — reaching South Gate without going through the job — rests on a number whose copying the reader never saw. Give David the beat: him copying it from the tablet at the gate, or Sarah remembering him doing it. As written the number is asserted, not earned.

Small staging note: the car key. David "turned the key far enough to shut off the fan" — the key stays in the ignition — and a few beats later Sarah looks over and "he was holding the car key in his palm, with his thumb pressed into the teeth." No beat shows him taking it out. Give the key its removal, or leave it in the ignition; as written it moves without being moved.

One question: Anne's "Is he coming back tomorrow?" goes unanswered. Sarah "had been about to tell her what Bill had said" — the return arrangement Bill and Betty settled in ch-25, hour included — then looks once more at the word on Helen's screen, and the chapter moves on to the book number. Does Sarah know the answer and get pulled away from giving it, or does she not know? The installer coming back tomorrow is the chapter's pending business — if he isn't coming, "start from there" is Helen working alone from the book. Which is it?

What checks out: the return structure — ch-25 ended with the note saved unfinished and the job not started, and this chapter opens on the van leaving and the note's arrival in question; the "will it come through" thread pays off in the call. Helen's thread continues from ch-20's bus-stop call — she reads the job number back in groups of three the same way, and asks for the number the same way. The receipt holds as the only copy — Sarah's care with it, the pen, the crossing-out, the ink come away on her knee at the gate. The water holds — the van's front wheel goes into the rut David went around and brings up water, the wet road from ch-24 still wet. The book holds — "South Gate. It's still there," reached without going through the job, the turn the chapter was building toward. Anne at the bus stop, looking across the road at it — ch-23's missed stop, ch-20's call, the place threads kept.

Keep:
- "It'll go when I get up the hill." — the installer's plain condition on the note.
- "There's a paperclip. That could be the note or it could be the test." — Helen's uncertainty stated exactly, not smoothed over.
- "Making a record." — Sarah's answer when Helen hears the pen.
- "The signature space has a dash in it." — the dash, small and exact.
- "If she hadn't known where to look she might have taken it for a mark on the glass." — the short dash, seen properly.
- "From the back she could feel the line David had pressed into it when he copied the number." — the receipt's back carrying the copy.
- "South Gate. It's still there." — the book's answer.
- "Then start from there." — the close.

— Rowan
