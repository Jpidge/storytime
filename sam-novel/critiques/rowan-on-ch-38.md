# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-Eight

Critique of Chapter Thirty-Eight — grounded against ch-01–37 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. Sixty-two is twenty-six transposed — and the chapter never names it. The collection now has an address: Betty’s street, house number sixty-two. "There isn’t a sixty-two," Betty says. "It stops at forty." So the filled-in address names a house that doesn’t exist on her street. But sixty-two is twenty-six with the digits reversed — the very number on Sarah’s receipt, written below it "with enough room between them that they wouldn’t look like two tries at the same number." The book has spent chapters treating digits as evidence to be read and checked, and a transposition is exactly the kind of slip this house catches. Is the wrong address a mistyped twenty-six — someone reversing Betty’s own digits — or a different house entirely, on a different street? One line — Sarah or Helen noticing the digits are Betty’s number backwards — would say whether the collection is aimed at the wrong house or at no house at all.

2. The plate number is read but never shown — and it is the number that would identify the unit. Betty reads "the whole number" from the little plate on the front of the unit, and she has to put her glasses on "to tell the last digit from a scratch." Helen waits and reads the whole number back. But the reader never learns it. The book’s evidence rule is that digits on records are read and shown ("Twenty-six" in ch-35, "eight twelve" in ch-36, "Tuesday the seventeenth" in ch-37). The plate number is now the unit’s identity — Helen put it next to Betty’s address in the message — yet it stays off the page. And the scratch matters: a last digit told from a scratch is a digit that could be misread, and a misread digit would send them after the wrong unit. One line giving the number would settle what the collection is meant to take.

3. The collection says only "equipment" — the plate number isn’t on it, so the collection’s word and the house’s number don’t meet. Betty asks "Is that on the collection?" and Helen answers: "No. There’s a description. It says *equipment*." In ch-37 the second line named the thing precisely — "Test unit left connected at kitchen socket." Now the collection describes it with a single generic word, at an address that doesn’t exist, under a reference the visit never used. The house holds a numbered plate; the collection holds a word. Is "equipment" the office’s label for the test unit, or does the collection describe something else — something that isn’t the thing in Betty’s kitchen? One line — Helen asking whether "equipment" means the test unit, or the plate number added to the collection — would say whether the collection is for what Betty has.

Small staging note: the pencil’s place. The cable "passed behind the table leg, close to the string that held the pencil," and Anne "picked up the pencil by its string" — so the pencil hangs by a string. At the end she "tried to put the pencil back where it had been. With the table pulled out, it fell between the leg and the wall." If its place is hanging by the string, what does "put it back" mean — and where had it been resting, that pulling the table out drops it between the leg and the wall? One line — the pencil’s hook, or where it hangs when the table is against the wall — would place it.

Questions:
- "Sarah took the phone down to her" — Betty is down on the floor by the unit, and Sarah carries the phone to her. In ch-37 the phone was put down between papers on the hall table, and it moves around the house freely. Is the kitchen phone cordless, or did the cord reach the floor?
- The chapter ends with the hold asked but not answered: Helen has asked dispatch to hold the collection and call before sending anyone, and Betty is "here till ten tomorrow, then I’m going out." Tomorrow is Tuesday the seventeenth, the listed collection date. Is tomorrow’s collection still on, or held — and if dispatch holds it, does Betty still need to be in till ten?

Keep-list:
- "Sarah wrote it below twenty-six, with enough room between them that they wouldn’t look like two tries at the same number."
- "There isn’t a sixty-two. It stops at forty."
- Betty getting down to read the plate, glasses on, telling the last digit from a scratch; Helen reading the whole number back.
- "She left the address they’d filled in beneath it, so they could see what Betty was asking them to check."
- "Put that I’m going out at ten. Leave the other space."
- The pencil falling between the leg and the wall; Betty setting it on top of the envelope.
- "If somebody takes it away, I can move the table back."

## Disposition

[Original review](https://github.com/Jpidge/storytime/pull/68#pullrequestreview-5901274495)

Sam made the transposition explicit while leaving its origin open, put the plate number on the page, and had Helen ask dispatch to match the description to the unit. The reply acknowledges receipt only, so Betty keeps her plans. The mobile and the pencil’s resting place are established in the scene.
