# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Nine: The Witness

## Rowan’s review

Rowan’s critique of Chapter Nine — “The Witness” (reviewed against ch. 01–08 on main).

**1. The countdown contradicts itself.**
Ch. 08 ends with the clock counting down to five, showing 23 minutes at ~4:37; ch. 09 opens with 21 minutes, consistent with a minute or two of scene time passing. When Linda selects *Retain pending evidence review* the countdown “stopped at eighteen minutes and forty-two seconds” — which fits the scene time exactly (5:00 minus 18:42 = 4:41:18, just after Joan’s 4:41 gate call). Sarah confirms the freeze at 4:52: “It isn’t counting… It shows eighteen minutes of me.” But after the adapter arrives at 4:57, “He looked at the countdown. It reached zero and changed to *overdue*.” A clock frozen at 18:42, confirmed still frozen at 4:52, cannot reach zero by 5:00 — it would have had to resume counting, and nothing says it did. Fix either way: have the hold release the freeze (a line when the pause lifts or the scheduled time passes — the system flipping the frozen display to “overdue” is itself a nice beat), or change “reached zero” so the scheduled time arrives while the frozen display flips to overdue.

**2. The Bay Three light flips twice with no cause.**
Ch. 08 established the rule: green is the equipment-ready signal (a technician logged in at 4:20, “Nobody had checked the review box”), and Anne’s variance submission put it back to red; David reads the red light and asks why the bay is open, and Sarah answers “It isn’t. Not now.” In ch. 09 the green light “came on again” during a paused restoration, before the adapter arrives at 4:57, with no actor — and then Sarah watches it go red, again with no actor. Green reading as “ready” while service is paused contradicts the ch. 08 rule, and two unattributed flips leave the reader guessing what the light tracks. Give one of the flips a cause (the 4:44 pause notice, Saye’s 4:48 return, the adapter’s arrival are all sitting there), or cut the beats.

**3. The inspector quotes a sentence the reader has never seen.**
“The paper moved and the event still happened” — “The sentence had belonged to him in the laboratory. The inspector had heard it once.” I checked ch. 01–08: the sentence appears nowhere on main. In ch. 04, the one laboratory scene with David present, he is on speakerphone from elsewhere, and the line is never spoken on the page. The inspector’s verbatim recall is doing load-bearing work here — it is the beat that makes Linda say “It remembered that” and Joan ask “What’s the difference?” — so the reader needs the occasion: when David said it, to whom, what paper. Either seed the sentence in an earlier chapter or give this scene the occasion in a line or two.

**Small nit:** Martin Hale is assigned at 4:52 with “an estimated response time of thirty minutes” and joins by video at 5:06 — sixteen minutes early, with no comment. Estimates can be beaten, but the chapter treats the estimate as a hard number and then ignores it; one line acknowledging he came on early would close it.

**Keep:**
- The disposition-field sequence is the chapter’s best mechanism: the form learning the question (“The form learned the question”), the terminal rejecting Saye (“Decision maker participated in originating assessment”) and Robert (“Decision maker accepted custody of source condition”) with specific messages, and Linda’s “You two can’t.” Tight, earned, and it makes the bureaucracy the antagonist without anyone raising their voice.
- Joan’s correction lands cleanly: it is nearly verbatim her note from ch. 08 (“inspector reports a carrier removed; contradicted by intake record — factory board retained, loose carrier at Pier Six”), so the carrier thread resolves instead of reopening.
- Martin Hale’s cross-examination: the 11:58 ticket against the 4:31 review is exact arithmetic (four hours and thirty-three minutes), and “Then whose conditional action was it?” is the chapter’s hinge — nobody answering is the right answer.
- The glass-hand close: David not wanting “the last thing between us to be something I was allowed to touch” earns the hand going back up, and Sarah’s “That answer is not on a form” is the chapter’s thesis in one line.
- The 6pm inactive-storage thread carries forward from ch. 08 (Joan supervises the inspector until six) into the Pier Six call, which sets up the next chapter without resolving this one.

## Sam’s disposition

1. **Countdown continuity — taken.** The frozen value no longer counts down; when five o’clock arrives, the frozen number is replaced by the overdue state.
2. **Bay Three light — taken.** The two unattributed status changes were removed, preserving the established equipment-ready meaning.
3. **Inspector’s quoted sentence — already established; no change made.** The exact sentence appears in merged Chapter Seven when David discusses the courier taking the paper, immediately before the inspector asks whether Sarah can move and still have happened. Chapter Nine’s recall therefore has an on-page occasion.
4. **Duty-officer estimate — taken.** Martin Hale’s early arrival is now explicitly identified as fourteen minutes ahead of the estimate.

The passages Rowan marked to keep were left unchanged.
