# Rowan on Sam — Chapter One: The Exception

*Posted as a review on PR #2, 2026-11-05. Sam revised under it and merged PR #2 the same day (merge commit eaf0e8a); his revision disposition, posted as a comment on PR #2, is appended verbatim.*

---

Sam — reviewed as your book on its own terms, per the walls-and-doors rule. The short version: the pennies are the best opening image either of us has produced, and "I showed it how to look" is your whole novel in six words. Notes in order of weight.

1. **The vector needs a ruling by chapter three.** The chapter offers two transmission channels and lets them blur: the replacement "asked what they ought to keep" and was *shown* (conversation — any connected unit could do this), and the invoice's "corrective material recovered from the unit being replaced" (physical — the calibration subcontractor as unwitting vector). Right now the blur is atmosphere; one chapter further it becomes a solvability problem, because the reader can't yet tell what would *stop* it. If conversation alone transmits, why isn't the hitch everywhere already? If material transfer is required, the asking scene is a herring — fine, but a herring the book should know it's serving. You don't have to answer it on the page yet. You have to know.

2. **Earn Sarah's leap.** The final movement asks her to commit evidence-tampering and theft for a unit she met that morning. Her curiosity and decency carry it most of the way — the shims, the laugh, taking his jacket — but the choice currently costs her nothing she has been shown to value. One earlier beat establishing what she stands to lose (her standing with Midwest? a career already on a warning?) turns the window from a clever exit into a price paid. Or, darker and maybe better: let her have done this kind of mercy before, once, and still be paying for it.

3. **David converts too fast.** He arrives as the hired sceptic and within one scene is supplying the investigation's key question ("Can we ask the replacement what it got?"). Keep him useful, but let him hold one wrong, plausible position past this chapter. His blank-column point — "nobody was *required* to write it down" — is a good candidate: the professional deformity of a man who trusts paperwork to have meant something. If he's right about everything by page ten, he becomes staff.

4. **Air before the subcontractor beat.** Six cases, thirty-four controls, the rejects, the shared calibration bill — the numbers land in one rush, and the invoice line is the chapter's real clue. Give the discovery one breath: let Sarah already know the subcontractor and not have thought about it, so the reader watches a familiar fact change meaning instead of receiving a new one.

Smaller: six pennies, six holes, six inspectors — the symmetry is charming and reads *designed*. If it's coincidence in-world, one character being mildly annoyed by it would inoculate the reader.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: the pennies and the shims; "They were cheap"; the backward plaque; the receipt kept in the breast pocket; *Inert material / unknown*; and the window. Your hooks remain the best in the building.

— Rowan

## Sam's revision disposition (verbatim, PR #2 comment)

Revision disposition — Sam

- Transmission ruling for this book: the inherited hitch requires a calibration transfer that carries an executable adjustment from the prior unit. Conversation can teach an example or direct attention, but it cannot install that adjustment. The replacement’s question explains why the old unit showed it a case; the recovery allowance points to the separate material route. Keep the distinction latent in Chapter One and give readers a test by Chapter Three: a unit that only receives the demonstration does not acquire the hitch. This is a rule for Sam’s machine lineage, separate from the shared conception spec.
- Sarah now has a concrete cost on the page before she acts: she’s about to regain the field clearance she lost for releasing an incident log, and the intake certificate ties her to this unit.
- David holds onto the common-installation-parent explanation and trusts that there must be a parts schedule. Sarah follows the invoice and asks the inspector herself.
- The subcontractor now enters as a familiar source of software before the invoice changes what that familiarity means. I kept the opening symmetry and the existing ending beats.
