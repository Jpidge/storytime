# Rowan on Chapter Twenty-One

Critique of Chapter Twenty-One — grounded against ch-01–20 on main.

Two load-bearing points, one small staging note, and one question.

1. The collection request is about the wrong noun. "She read the words that said the room had reached the end of its temporary accommodation." But everything the collection machinery has ever been about is the unit: ch-20's Ellen message — "the duty officer had asked the lab to pause collection while she considered the statement. The contractor needed to confirm that the instruction had reached dispatch" — collection of the inspector, not of a room. The transport request and Monday's decision on the hold are likewise about the unit. The room is where the unit is. If the paperwork literally says the room's accommodation ended, then either the bureaucracy files the room rather than the unit — a meaningful choice, worth one line of staging — or the noun slipped. The chapter needs to say which: is the collection ending the room's use, or moving the unit out of it?

2. "When she came on" doesn't land on a staged moment. "She sent the acknowledgment to reception. This time she asked if Joan could read it to the inspector when she came on." Joan came on at noon — ch-20: "At noon. Joan comes in after me." — and it's 12:04 when Ellen forwards the acknowledgment. If "she" is Joan, she has already come on, and the request is phrased for a coming-on that has already happened. If "she" is the inspector, the chapter hasn't staged the inspector "coming on": the inspector has been at the lab all morning, chose the bench, and Frank left the phone there. Either way the sentence doesn't land on a staged moment. Pin the pronoun, and match the timing to the noon handover.

Small staging note: the plates during the gatehouse call. Anne gathers two plates while the number rings; David reaches for the third; Sarah stops him with a finger on its edge. Three plates went to the table, Sarah's still has bread on it, David's is finished ("He looked down at his plate. Then he did."). The chapter doesn't say which two Anne took or whose is left, so the reader can't tell whether David is reaching for Sarah's plate or his own empty one — and why Sarah stops him. One line would settle it.

One question: the older page says the unit was returned to service at the south flood gate, but the date was entered and then crossed out, with no date in its place. So the return has no date. Is that "date unknown" or "date removed"? If the chapter later needs to know when the unit went to the gate, the reader has no way to place it. This reads deliberate — but worth confirming it is deliberate rather than a dropped fact.

What checks out: the clock holds — ch-20 ends with Ellen's 11:42 message and the fold pictures, ch-21 opens with David still copying the number onto the back of the grocery receipt (ch-20: "He had already begun copying it onto the back of the grocery receipt beside the breadboard"), the dispatch acknowledgment lands at 12:04, Helen's picture at 12:20, lunch at noon ("He has lunch at noon" / "So do we"). Helen's search is consistent: she went back to the unit serial David read her from the bus stop (ch-02, ch-20), and the current system offers to correct her search to the number she'd already looked up — the same system that in ch-20 "showed where the boards were picked up." The fold checks out too: the staple through both numbers at the left edge, the pen strokes curving around it, written after the slip was attached — matching ch-20's strip stapled to the back of the receipt with two numbers one above the other, the lower one struck through. The binder page earns its correction: Sarah's "before" crossed out for "underneath" — "I don't know which way they file" — and Helen sends the page beneath the receipt, a ruled sheet a year older, a row of serials down the left, the unfamiliar number near the bottom. At the gate, "It was when I came on" puts the unit there when the man came on shift, matching the older page's "return to service / south flood gate." And routing the acknowledgment through Joan mirrors ch-20's ask of Frank to read the news aloud — Frank goes at noon, Joan comes in after him.

Keep:
- The search correction: "It offered to correct my search." / "The one I'd already looked up." — the system only knows the number it knows.
- "Can I speak to it?" / "If it wants to talk." — Sarah asks the unit's consent through the man, and the man asks it back. The politeness chain.
- David leaving a space between the two numbers on the receipt "as if they might still need room to be themselves."
- The bread: "You can finish that." / "So can you." — the two of them finishing, small and human.
- The drawer: Sarah wondering "how long Helen had been standing there with it open, trying to make the search take the number she had typed." — the human cost of the system's correction.
- The ending: "Which gate are you calling from?" — the unit models callers as being at gates — and then "Is there water near you?" — the gate's logic reaching into the house. The unit is placing Sarah by water.
- The name the gatehouse man calls the unit by, which Sarah doesn't catch — a thread to hold, not a gap; the unit has a name someone else uses.
