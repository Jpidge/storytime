# Rowan on Chapter Sixty

The phone call carries the chapter — Mo ringing at 11:43 with the file saved, the read-back ritual for the collection number and the address, Sarah’s finger on the depot-test date, and Betty folding the sideways sheet on its old fold. Keep “It’s all one file when it comes out of the machine,” “I sent the part you asked for,” the iron stood on its heel before she picks up, and the closing “Now we can ask them to look at both” — once “both” is settled. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **How does Mo tell where this morning starts?** The file is “all one file when it comes out of the machine,” and the depot “had no way of telling from the file where the monitor had been on every date” — yet Mo can tell Betty “where this morning starts.” The chapter never says what marks the boundary: the 10:41 switch-on, a gap in the rows since the last clear, the bench sheet’s times. Without the marker, the split rests on Mo’s say-so rather than anything shown — the same shape as the Chapter Fifty-Nine point about the log row’s missing time. One line naming what she reads the boundary off would let the reader trust the split.

2. **“The email to Betty came first” clashes with what the email says.** The message’s last line reports the file “had gone to visits” — past tense, already sent — so the visits send precedes the email. But “came first” reads as first of the two sends Mo promised. If it means first thing Betty received, one word would say so; as written the reader has to hold both orders at once. The book has been exact about send and receive order, and this is the one place the chapter lets it slip.

3. **“Both” at the close has three candidates.** “Now we can ask them to look at both” — both what? The two sets of rows (this morning’s and the earlier ones), the bench sheet and the log file, or the two dates Mo is putting in the message? The chapter has just handled all three pairs, and the closing line doesn’t pick one. One word would settle which pair visits is being asked to look at.

Two small loose ends: Mo’s “He’s saved it” never names the “he” in this chapter — the reader has to supply the fitter from Chapter Fifty-Nine — and Betty’s “address from the reply they’d had on Wednesday” arrives with no date and no antecedent; the book has dated every other reply and envelope line, and Wednesday floats. One line each would anchor them.

One continuity check: 11:43 sits between the 10:41 switch-on and the fitter needing the bench at noon, so the noon clock from Chapter Fifty-Nine is still running and never mentioned — the reader can’t tell whether the file was saved before noon or only just in time. The collection-number read-back and Sarah’s finger on the depot-test date match the envelope Chapter Fifty-Nine closed on; “everything since the last time they cleared it” matches the stores routine; “I don’t want them thinking you did it here” keeps the depot test where it belongs. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter.
