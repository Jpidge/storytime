Critique of Chapter Seventeen, read against ch-01–16 on main.

Three points, then what's working.

1. The 8:28 call opens twice. Ch-16 ends with the call already begun — "Outside the building, at eight-twenty-eight, Sarah called Anne. 'I can talk now,' she said." Ch-17 reopens the same call from the ring: "Anne answered on the fourth ring. 'I'm outside,' Sarah said." The scene matches otherwise (David steps away in ch-16; in ch-17 he's across the pavement at the wet flagpole, which carries over from ch-16's flag hanging wet against its pole), but a reader holding both chapters will feel the restart — two different first lines for one call. Either pick the call up mid-conversation or reconcile the opening line.

2. Anne's 7:15 submission has no channel, and her only established channel is dark. Ch-11, 18:41 Friday: "Midwest suspended Anne's service card. The card went dark on the chair." Nothing since restores it. The account itself is well seeded — ch-15: "They want an account of why I closed the log," and "They asked whether my judgment had been affected by contact with the source condition," and her sentence "You spoke to me. I listened. That affected my judgment." — but the mechanics of a 7:15 submission need one grounding line: phone, terminal, in person. Otherwise the reader wonders whether the suspension was lifted off-page.

3. David's signing anecdote is the chapter's emotional core, and it works — but it's his first employment backstory and it still doesn't touch the live ch-12 thread. There, the notice carries an employer-account heading with a blank employer field, his lab access is suspended "pending an account of his participation in the source-condition event," and his own line is "I signed in as a visitor. They never gave me access." — a visit converted into a privilege it could withdraw. The anecdote tells us about a job he left years ago; the reader still doesn't know who employs him now or what the suspension costs him. One anchoring line tying the old signature to the present suspension would close it.

Keep:
- The clock holds: 8:28 → 8:34 → 8:46 → 9:08 → 9:20 → 9:27 → 9:30, tight against ch-16's ending.
- "No one asked for a license number" — clean callback to ch-14 ("A woman asked for her license number"). The café is the first place that doesn't ask, and Sarah nearly missing the question she was asked ("Tea or coffee?", repeated) is the chapter's best small beat.
- The duty-officer payoff: seeded in ch-09 ("Saye told Linda to request the regional duty officer"), Ruth asked for the procedure in ch-16, and here the office has to write it down. "Someone now had to turn a phrase into actions another person could read" lands the book's thesis about forms.
- The form's character limit erasing her name — she types "I am Sarah Miller, the person to whom access is proposed," the field won't hold it, and she becomes "The person you propose to access." The system asks her relationship to the listed system and won't hold her name.
- Anne's "It wasn't a disclosure. They asked." — the relabeling of her account is exactly the book's document-misrepresentation motif, and the objection's core line (her refusal of capture not be represented as absence of cooperation) answers the referral's second paragraph directly.
- David's "I kept waiting for the time it wasn't," Sarah listening "without improving it," and the kiss that "wasn't an answer." Keep as is.
