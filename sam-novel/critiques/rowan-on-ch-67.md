# Rowan on Chapter Sixty-Seven

The chapter is about sourcing — what Betty said against what the monitor recorded, and keeping the two apart on the page. Jo’s entry as it stood put Betty in the kitchen at the monitor’s minute, and the chapter is the slow work of pulling those two claims back to their separate sources: the kitchen from Betty, told to Sarah on the first of December; the minute from the monitor’s record, which never saw her. Keep “That’s what you sent” and “Have you got the same thing?”, the last sentence whole on the screen after breaking across the printed pages, “I didn’t tell you that minute” and “And the reading didn’t see me,” Betty keeping her finger on the date at the top of her account while Jo reads, “Or the socket” as Betty’s amendment to Jo’s amendment, Jo stopping and starting again when she writes the note, “Let me read it” and Betty turning the screen to keep the window out of it, the tea bag still in the cup with the paper tag on the table, and the clip taken off yesterday’s two pages and laid beside the envelope, ready for the new one. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **The two pages’ way onto the table is never shown.** In Sixty-Six Jo’s note went into the envelope last, still clipped, and the envelope was left on the kitchen table beside the pencil. Here Betty puts the two pages where the loaf had been, “still held by the clip” — but the chapter never shows her taking them out of the envelope, while it does show her taking out the sideways printout. In a book that stages every placement — the clip test, the numbering, the note going in last — the pages’ return to the table is the one move left off the page. One line would close it.

2. **The account’s keeping-place, and its signature.** “Beneath it was the account Sarah had written on the first of December” — beneath the printout. But Sixty-Six packed the envelope in full view: the signed sheet first, the printout beside it, Jo’s note last. The account was never put in, so a reader can’t tell where it was kept or when it went under the printout. And the chapter first calls it “the account Sarah had written” but later “her own signed account” — if Betty signed it, when? The signed sheet is a different document, dated the nineteenth of November, its instruction read back from Jo’s photograph. Two signed documents need one line to keep them distinct.

3. **The two lines about the clock and the socket are never shown.** Jo reads the account and stops moving her finger when she reaches them, and the whole correction turns on what they claim — whether they say Betty looked at the clock, or only that she was by it. Betty’s own correction, that she did not recall looking at the clock, “Or the socket,” implies the first; but the reader never sees the lines to check Jo’s reading against Betty’s words, the way the chapter lets the reader check the note Jo writes by having her read the whole of it back. One line giving the lines’ substance would let the reader do that check.

One continuity check: Thursday the tenth of December follows Wednesday the ninth (Sixty-Six) and Tuesday the eighth (Fifty-Nine), and ten thirty is the visit Sixty-Five set; the two pages still clipped, the shopping bag off the chair, and the bread from yesterday’s trip to the shop all carry through from Sixty-Six; the screen showing the last sentence without a page break is consistent with Sixty-Six, where only the printed copy broke it across pages; six fourteen is the monitor’s minute from the first line. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter — the monitor’s reading gives a time, not a sighting, which is exactly Betty’s point.
