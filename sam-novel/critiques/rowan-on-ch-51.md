# Rowan on Chapter Fifty-One

Critique of Chapter Fifty-One — grounded against ch-01–50 on main.

Two load-bearing points, three small staging notes, and a question. Keep-list at the end.

**1. Betty's question — "Does it say anywhere on the other papers where I was?" — never gets its answer on the page.**

Sarah goes through the sheets, holds up her questions sheet "long enough to look at the back," and the scene moves to the oven without Sarah saying yes or no. The back-look is where the answer should land: in this book backs carry content — the sum worked out on the back of the envelope (ch-49), the box with three lines of type on the back of the sheet (ch-36), the work put on the back (ch-36). A look at a back with no reported result reads as a fact withheld, and it leaves the reader unsure whether the papers do place Betty in the kitchen after all. One line — the back is blank, or what's there — closes the question and tells the reader the statement Sarah is writing is the only record of where she was. As it stands the chapter's own question is left hanging at the exact moment the statement becomes the record.

**2. The two sentences are paraphrased, never shown — and the clock answer has no visible home in them.**

Sarah asks three things in the exchange: the timer setting, the clock, the socket. Betty answers all three — timer set for quarter past six, couldn't remember looking up, doesn't remember looking at the socket. But the read-back is "the two sentences," and the reader never sees either one. The book shows exact wording when a sheet matters ("Thursday 19 November, 11:23," ch-50; "Monday 30 November, depot," ch-49), and the dating correction here proves the wording is doing work — Wednesday moves into the first sentence, Tuesday's date goes at the top. The reader needs to see the sentences to know whether "I didn't look at the clock" is in the record. It matters because the whole six-fourteen claim turns on the timer, not a clock reading: Betty knows she was there from what she set, not from what she saw. If the first sentence carries that distinction, show it; if the clock answer is deliberately left out of the statement, the read-back should say so.

**Small staging notes:**

- "Betty wrote her name in the space that left" — reads as a dropped "was" ("the space that was left" / "the space left").
- "the two that gave the times at the depot" — the table's pile is large now (Jo's sheet, Mo's sheet, the sideways printout, the depot reply clipped to Friday's printout, the envelope, Betty's morning sheet). Naming the two costs nothing and saves the reader sorting the pile.
- A pen arrives without introduction ("She offered Betty the pen") in a book that has been careful about the kitchen pencil on its string (ch-49). If the statement is deliberately signed in pen while everything else is pencil, that's worth one beat; if not, the pencil is already in reach.

**Question:**

- Sarah's socket question is elided — "When she asked about the socket" — and Betty's answer does the work of both question and answer. The socket is the thing the monitor was plugged into and the thing whose reading is at issue; is the question left out because the answer is the record, or should the reader hear what Sarah actually asked?

**Keep-list:**

- Mo's ticks beside each of Sarah's two lines, and the reply confirming both asks — the Thursday download file kept, the readings attachment set aside as sent. Clean closure of ch-50's email.
- Betty picking up the sideways sheet by its corner — "It's on there. I can show you where I read it." This settles the ch-50 ambiguity about which copy six fourteen was read off: the sent file, printed sideways.
- The dating correction — Wednesday into the first sentence, Tuesday's date at the top — echoing ch-49's rule (Sarah dated the four-times sheet "Monday 30 November, depot" instead of Wednesday). Betty catching it with "When did I tell you this?" is the right trigger.
- The timer demonstration — turned just far enough to catch, back to zero, the little click — matches ch-43's "switched it off before it rang," and the socket answer ("I don't remember looking at it") matches ch-43's own words.
- The oven running and the timer remembered — consistent with ch-45 ("The oven was running about then. I remember the timer.").
- Dates check: Tuesday the first of December and Wednesday the eighteenth both land on the right weekdays.
