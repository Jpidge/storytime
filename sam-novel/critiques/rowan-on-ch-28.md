# Rowan on Chapter Twenty-Eight

Critique of Chapter Twenty-Eight — grounded against ch-01–27 on main.

Two load-bearing points, one small staging note, and one question.

1. The gate name in the address. Dispatch asks for the job number and then for the first line of the address. Sarah gives her the road. The woman reads it back, "including the part that was usually left off the signs." Sarah waits for her to say the name of the gate — and the woman says, instead, "I can resend the link." So the part usually left off the signs is something else, and it is never named, and the gate name never comes. The reader can't tell whether the office's address names South Gate, Pier Six, or neither — which is exactly the question the book has been tracking since the installer typed both names into the note in ch-25 and Bill joined them with a line and a question mark in ch-27. One line — Sarah hearing whether the gate is in the address, or noticing that it isn't — would say whether the office record names a gate at all, and which one.

2. The signature "No" is never confirmed. The link wants Bill to say he's the person who signed. Bill didn't sign — the installer left the signature empty and saved the visit unfinished in ch-25, and the dash is the no-signature placeholder (ch-26). Sarah tells dispatch plainly: "Did he?" / "No." The woman is quiet for a while — and then moves straight to resending the link. She never reads the "no" back. In a book where Helen reads every number back and Bill writes every time down, a correction that isn't repeated back hasn't landed. One line — Sarah saying it again, or the woman repeating that nobody signed — would show whether the office heard that the premise of the link is false, or whether the false premise is the point and nobody at the office is listening.

Small staging note: the tap against the glass. Bill is on the phone at the office computer; something taps against the glass at his end, and then Betty is waiting for him to look at the grass with her. The tap is presumably Betty's knuckle on the office window — but the glass hasn't been placed, and Bill is the one at the computer, so the reader has to guess whose glass it is and which side Betty is on. Name it — her knuckle on the window, or her tapping at the door — and the beat lands without the guess.

One question: "the reply had gone to the gatehouse address." Bill is looking at it on the office computer, so the address reads as an inbox — but the chapter never says email, and "address" is also the postal first line Sarah gives dispatch, and the post-office-box invoice address from ch-27. Is the gatehouse address an email address? One word would settle which of the three addresses the reply went to.

What checks out: the two names — ch-27's revision named them South Gate and Pier Six, so "when she read the two names" now has its antecedent, and Bill's quieter second yes lands as recognition, not confusion. Helen's one attachment matches ch-27's "something attached to it, but she can't tell what" — she could see the test, and the note is the next page of the same file, which is why "Go down a page" finds it. The note after the test matches the order dispatch gives ("a test and a note") and the order the installer worked in ch-25. Bill's number on the request is the "Put my number down" from ch-27, and the reply going to the gatehouse address instead of his number is why he's the one looking at it. The receipt keeps accumulating — job number on the back (ch-25), request number underneath it, the time beside that. The book open by the computer is where it lives now, and Bill writing down when he opened the page and when he shuts it is the same record-keeping ethic as "Two was when he started." "There wasn't anywhere to put that" is the form admitting its own gap.

Keep:
- "In case the page goes away." — Bill on the book, the record against the vanishing page.
- "There wasn't anywhere to put that." — the form's gap, stated plain.
- "Did he?" / "No." — the whole signature question in two words.
- "Go down a page," he said. "He put what I said after the test." — Bill knowing the file's order.
- "I couldn't tell. She had to show me." — Bill on the grass.
- "I did," he said. "I'll write down when I shut it." — the open and the shut, both written down.
- "When she read the two names he said yes again, more quietly." — the quieter yes.

— Rowan
