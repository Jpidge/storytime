# Rowan on Chapter Fifty-Four

Critique of Chapter Fifty-Four — grounded against ch-01–53 on main.

Three load-bearing points and two small notes. Keep-list at the end.

**1. The number in the reply is never identified.**

Betty's ask at the end of the call is exact: "Can you send me the number of the visit you put it with?" The reply arrives, and in the body, beneath the words *Attached to completed collection visit*, is "a different number" — different from the message number in the subject. Sarah writes it on the envelope beside the first one, Betty says "Down a line", and the chapter ends. But nobody says the number is the visit number, nobody reads it aloud, and the reader never learns it. After ch-53 made the missing visit number the open question, ch-54 hands Betty a number without confirming it's the one she asked for. One line — the woman naming it as the visit number in the body, or Betty reading it back the way she reads back 4173 — closes it.

**2. The second visit has no origin.**

"There are two visits at that address on the nineteenth." Betty feared exactly this in ch-53 — "There might have been more than one" — and now there are two. But the superseded one arrives with no history: nobody came, it's marked superseded, and nobody asks who booked it or why it was superseded. The chapter's engine is which-visit-which-number; a second visit at the same address on the same day with no provenance is a thread left hanging. One question from Betty — who made the other one? — either answers it or marks it as the next thing to chase.

**3. The note offer is left unanswered.**

"Do you want me to make a note of what you told me?" Betty never says yes or no; she pivots to the name on the sheet, and the offer disappears. Small fix: a yes or a no, or the woman noting it anyway, so the exchange completes instead of trailing off.

**Small notes**

- "She set Jo's sheet beside the reply on the screen" — paper sheet beside a screen reply. One clause placing the sheet on the table beside the laptop clears it.
- "The woman asked for the number on the reply." Betty reads it from the back of the envelope, but the chapter never says which number she reads — the reader has to infer it's the message number from ch-53's reply subject. One clause naming it as the message number pins it.

**Keep-list**

- The name read back from the photograph, then the date at the top — closes ch-53's retake question, and "I won't read it all over the phone" is exactly right.
- 4173 read back digit by digit against Jo's sheet — the number discipline holds.
- "Down a line" — the envelope as a running record; room for the second number without crossing out the first.
- The rubbish bookend — the bag set outside the door at the start, carried all the way to the bin at the end.
- The calendar holds: Wednesday morning before Thursday 19 November (ch-51's Wednesday the eighteenth).
- "Leave it as it is, then" — Betty declining the full read-back; the account stays hers, in her voice.
- "There are two visits" answering Betty's "There might have been more than one" from ch-53.
- "Nobody, by the look of it" — the woman hedges honestly instead of inventing a visitor.
- Betty waiting until she hears the keys stop before thanking her and hanging up — the small observed close.
