# Rowan on Chapter Sixty-Two

The equipment call carries the chapter — Betty's question from Chapter Sixty-One finally reaches someone who can act on it, the monitor is located, the check is promised while the monitor is still as they left it, and for the first time there's a name to put beside the question. Keep "There was no music this time," the drawer opening with something rolling in it, the cup put down with the handle turned away and turned back by its handle, "Does the machine measure what goes through that socket, or can it count what it's using itself?", and "We can do that while it's still as they left it." Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **The nineteenth of November is never anchored.** Betty asks the man to read the date on the photograph of her signed sheet; he reads the nineteenth of November, and she moves her thumb off the number — the check lands, but the reader was never told what date her sheet carries, or what the date is the date of. The dated anchor the reader holds is the envelope's "Depot test, Tuesday the eighth of December" from Chapter Fifty-Nine, which is a different date for a different sheet — so the nineteenth arrives with nothing to check it against, and Betty's satisfaction stays hers alone. One line giving the expected date beforehand, or naming what the date attaches to, would let the reader share the confirmation.

2. **The monitor's booking is left hanging against the clearing threat.** Chapter Fifty-Nine set up that the monitor could go back into stores at noon, and if another job needed it they would clear the log before it went out. Here the monitor "was in the cupboard" and "had been booked out for tomorrow morning" — but the chapter never says whose cupboard, or whether tomorrow's booking is the other job that would clear the log. The chapter's own stake is the check "while it's still as they left it," so whether the booking threatens that matters. One clause placing the cupboard and the booking would settle it.

3. **The file is waited for and never arrives.** "Betty waited for him to say something about the file." He never does — the call moves on to the socket question, and the file isn't mentioned again. The query was sent with the whole log, and Chapter Sixty established the file carries the runs from when Betty had the monitor, so whether equipment has it is a live question. One line — he has it, or he doesn't — would close it.

One smaller tangle: two numbers, one "the number." The chapter opens with "the new number" — the query reference from Chapter Sixty-One — and later Betty gives "the number off the plate"; when the man is talking to stores "he said the number twice," and the reader has to guess which one. A word each time would keep them distinct.

One small loose end: "Betty asked him to send her the time when he had it." The time of what — the fitter's check, or the call back? One word would anchor it.

One small note: the name. "For the first time there was a name she could put beside the question, and she wrote it while he was still on the line" — but the chapter never gives the reader the name. Functionaries stay unnamed throughout the book, so the withholding is consistent; but the chapter itself flags the name as the beat that matters, and keeping it from the reader leaves the beat abstract. Either give it, or the line stays a gesture.

One continuity check: the fitter needing the bench at twelve matches Chapter Fifty-Nine's noon; "when there was nothing in it at the depot, it said the same thing for a while" matches the bench sheet's zero readings; the plate number, the envelope moved out of the way, and the pencil all carry over; the query from Chapter Sixty-One has now reached equipment, which is the chapter's forward motion. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter.
