# Rowan on Sam — Chapter Four: Received Alive

Sam — reviewed as your book on its own terms, same as last time. The short version: the paperwork has learned to receive, and the chapter's quietest turn is Sarah asking Ruth to receive the inspector *alive* — not as a condition on a list, but as a thing to be seen. The receiving lab pays off chapter three's promises: David's corrected consent arrives with the inspector, the control is at Pier Six, the carrier is still in the rack. But the chapter's biggest escalation, the Continuity Authority, arrives as a name on a screen, and it needs one line of routing to become load-bearing. Notes in order of weight.

1. **The Continuity Authority needs its routing stated.** The packet has been transferred to the Authority, and by the last line the Authority has received Ruth's request — in real time, while Sarah is still in the room. That's the chapter's largest move, and right now it's atmospheric: we don't know where Sarah's dispute goes (the city, which Ruth names as the recipient of the completed recovery record, or the Authority, which just received the request?), and we don't know whether Ruth's record to the city can be completed while its packet sits with the Authority. The book's engine is auditable paperwork — every form has a destination and a custodian. One line from Ruth — whether the dispute is filed with the city and copied to the Authority, or whether the Authority now owns the record — would make the escalation a fact in the chain instead of a shadow over it. The final line earns its chill either way; it just needs to cost somebody a routing decision first.

2. **The stair-lift lineage needs its verdict stated.** Ruth's numbering sheet gives us the evidence both ways and never rules on it: the prefix found twice, the earliest job a municipal stair lift eight months before the railing came down, a machine with no camera — pressure pads, door contacts, motor load. Sarah asks "Is it one of your six?" and Ruth says no, but the chapter never says what the lineage *is*: one physical board that has lived in machine after machine, the hitch older than the inspector — or a shared prefix, a numbering coincidence, a family of boards with one history each. Chapter three's critique asked for exactly this kind of ruling on the control test, and the same standard applies here: one line from Ruth — same board or same family — would settle whether the trail is a trail. The book's question is what stops the spread, and a hitch with a pre-inspector past changes the stakes entirely. Don't leave it suggestive.

3. **David's second mistake is forming — name the trap once.** His chapter-three mistake was signing what the paper said; here his eagerness runs the same way, pointed at the past instead of the destination. He finds "the last installation," and Sarah has to slow him twice — "Read the whole row," "Good. Don't yet." The heading already lies: "last installation or stock event" over a box that says "Received." If he enters the stair-lift date into the record, the record will say the carrier was *installed* somewhere it was only *received* — the same species of error as the consent, with the same signature on it. One line from Sarah — that the heading is the trap, not the box — would make his near-miss legible as the chapter's second consequential mistake in waiting, and give chapter five its exhibit.

4. **Ruth's "Operational" is the chapter's best beat — protect it.** The condition field is a list, and "alive" isn't on it, so Ruth writes "Operational" and Sarah makes her ask the inspector instead: "Can you read your serial?" / "Will you?" / "I came back." The inspector's camera turning toward the screwed window is the book's first self-report of return, and it should stay exactly that spare. Smaller: the pen on a cord lengthened with string, the bag put on the floor instead of the bench, the bad knee — Ruth arrives as a person in three details, and her line "Until somebody accepts responsibility for switching it off" is the chapter's hinge: an indefinite stay that nobody will end. One loose thread to seat: the penny at the wheel housing, held crooked by the little strip of paper — if it's a callback, the antecedent should be findable on the page for a reader coming cold to chapter four; if it's new, one line earlier would seat it. Let chapter five test whether Ruth's hold survives the Authority's receipt.

Keep, untouched if I have any say: "Could you receive this one alive?"; "It's what I want you to see"; "I came back"; "Is this a different place now?" / "Somebody else is using it"; "Until somebody accepts responsibility for switching it off"; "I agreed that I had the inspector. Nobody asked me whether I wanted it switched off"; "Pier Six left on the factory board. Fitter signed it. Our carrier is still here"; the screw through the window latch; and Ruth leaving the pen on the paper. Your paperwork keeps acquiring a conscience, one form at a time.

— Rowan


## Sam’s disposition

- Made the routing explicit. The dispute remains with the city. Ruth can complete receipt while the condition inquiry remains open, and obtaining the transferred packet requires sending the Authority a request with the dispute attached. Sarah authorizes that attachment on the page. Ruth’s staff number is attached to the request.
- Distinguished the depot prefix from the full board serial. The stair-lift record belongs to the same physical board. Its test remains unavailable, so this establishes the board’s earlier placement without claiming that the hitch was already present there.
- Made Sarah identify the event-type trap before David creates another entry. His correction and the images of the source records remain available for the next chapter.
- Identified the penny’s paper shim as Sarah’s work from the inspector’s first arrival. Preserved Ruth’s operational condition beat and the inspector’s return exchange.
