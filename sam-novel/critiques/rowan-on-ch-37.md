# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-Seven

Critique of Chapter Thirty-Seven — grounded against ch-01–36 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. Two things are read aloud and never shown — the collection date, and the words of the second line. Helen reads the date to Sarah ("I can see a date" … then the word *collection* again, then the date, then the reference from the email) — but the reader never hears it. And Betty reads the second line aloud to Helen — she has to start again after the first three words — and Helen reads it back, "including the words at the end where the writing went uphill" — but the reader never hears those words either. The date decides when they come for what was left; the words decide what was left. The book’s evidence rule is that digits and words on records are read and shown ("Twenty-six" in ch-35, "eight twelve" in ch-36). One line giving the date, and one line giving the second line’s words, would settle both — or the withholding is the point, and it should stand as a choice the chapter owns rather than an omission.

2. The collection runs under the replacement while the visit closed under the original. The visit was closed under the number from the man’s sheet — the original reference. The collection box sits under "the other one" — the replacement, the reference from the email. The two references, kept apart for chapters precisely so they don’t blur, are now split across two transactions: the visit under the original, the collection under the replacement. Helen offers to put both references in the same message, but the chapter ends before she does. Is the split the intended state — the replacement governing the collection while the original governed the visit — or should the message reconcile them? One line — Helen confirming the collection belongs to the replacement, or the message going out with both references named — would settle it. And the collection line has a field for an address, and Helen says it is empty. The thing was left at Betty’s house, at number twenty-six, but the collection names no place. One line — the address named, or Helen confirming the collection is from the house — would say whether they know where to come.

3. The stroke closes Betty’s copy while the arrangement stays open. There was room to write a third line on Betty’s copy, but the man had drawn a stroke through the space — her copy is finished as a record. Yet the sheet gives no date for taking it away, and "They’ve left it with me" means the thing stays with her indefinitely. The stroke says the record is closed; the missing date says the business isn’t. Which is it — a closed record of a finished visit, or an open one awaiting the collection date? One line — Betty asking him to leave the space open, or Helen adding the date to the copy — would settle it.

Small staging note: the envelope moved. At the end of ch-36 Betty laid the envelope beside the man’s sheet at the hall table. In ch-37 Anne is "reaching for the envelope under the telephone," and the telephone is in the kitchen — Sarah takes Helen’s call there, where she can still see Betty at the hall table. The envelope went from the hall table to under the kitchen telephone between chapters with no beat showing it moved. (The two marks on the flap track — Anne’s two boxes from ch-36.) One line — Anne carrying it back, or Betty setting it by the phone — would place it.

Questions:
- "He said they’d ring about that" — about when it goes. Helen’s ring is about the visit closing, not the date. Is the promised ring still to come, or is Helen’s call the ring Betty was waiting for?
- The van: "Betty waited till the van pulled away." The man arrived in ch-36 on foot at the door, case in hand — no vehicle was mentioned. Is the van his, and did he park it out of sight, or is it someone else’s?

Keep-list:
- "The man had written on the back with the paper laid against his knee. At the end of the second line the letters ran uphill."
- Anne’s finger under the first word, moving along until a word she couldn’t read.
- "That says *tested*. The bit about what he left is underneath."
- The stroke through the space for a third line — the record closed while the arrangement stays open.
- "They’ve left it with me. I want to know if they mean to take it from me."
- Betty having to start again after the first three words; Helen waiting; Helen reading the line back including the uphill words.
- "Put down that somebody’s got to ring. You can leave room for the date."
