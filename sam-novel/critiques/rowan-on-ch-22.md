# Rowan on Chapter Twenty-Two

Critique of Chapter Twenty-Two — grounded against ch-01–21 on main.

Two load-bearing points, one small staging note, and one question.

1. The reader — what it takes, and from where. "The one that takes her number. The new one won't take the old numbers. I've got a sheet for what to put in when they don't match." And: "Whatever comes up when he enters hers. They told me it would fix itself." The chapter's whole jeopardy is Sarah's ask — "Someone to check that the number he enters still opens this gate for Betty" — but the chapter never says where "hers" comes from when the man enters it. Established readers in this book take a presented credential — ch-03's badge on the reader, green light, bolt unmoved; ch-11/12's service card on the reader — not a typed number. Here the man *enters* a number. Does he read Betty's number off Betty herself, or off the gatehouse book (ch-21: "He asked her to hold while he found the book" / "It's in the book"), and then watch the system offer its correction? Or does the system supply the number and he just puts in what comes up? If he reads it off Betty, the correction has a specific entry point and Sarah's hold-the-old-reader request is the brake on it. If the system supplies it, the correction has already won before they arrive and the visit is checking a door that's already been re-keyed. One line seating where "hers" comes from would make the two-o'clock jeopardy land on a mechanism instead of a mood.

2. The low door is unplaced. "Is the reader the thing at the low door?" / "Yes." The low door arrives in Betty's question and Bill confirms it, but the chapter never says which door is low, or low relative to what — the gatehouse door, the gate's own door, a door Betty uses. It matters because the chapter's two two-o'clock events meet there: the reader is changed at the low door, and Betty goes out at two to look at the grass, presumably through it. One line of geography — which door, where it stands in the gate's layout — would let the ending's staging hold.

Small staging note: the car. Bill asks whether she had a car — the bus stops at the road above the gate and there's no footpath for the last quarter mile — and David answers "I can drive." That answers ability, not possession, and the book has only ever moved Sarah and David by bus (ch-02) and on foot (ch-16's walk past the shops). If they have a car, one line saying so would settle the visit's logistics; if David is answering around not having one, that's a different beat and worth staging as such.

One question: "At two, she goes out to look at the grass." Betty's round is at two, the same hour the reader man comes. If the new reader won't take the old numbers and Betty goes out through the low door at two, can she get back in — through the new reader, with which number? The chapter ends on the round, not on the return. This reads deliberate — the jeopardy carried into the next chapter — but worth confirming it's the return that's at stake, not just the going-out.

What checks out: the clock holds — ch-21 ends mid-call after Helen's 12:20 picture, ch-22 continues the same call, and the visit is set "before two" with the reader man at two; nothing drifts. The name pays off: ch-21's unheard name is "Betty," and Betty asking "Sarah what?" and holding "Miller" inverts ch-17, where the form's character limit erased Sarah's name — here a machine asks for it and keeps it. Sarah's "I used to go out to the piers" sits on ch-01, where she works a Pier Six machine at the bench and pulls up pictures from its final week on the pier. The correction theme carries: ch-21's "It offered to correct my search" is now a sheet ("what to put in when they don't match") and a promise ("They told me it would fix itself"), the correction made procedural. The photographs hold: Sarah wants both numbers on one screen and can't enlarge both "without losing the place on the page where one number had been replaced by the other" — the fold from ch-20, the staple through both numbers from ch-21. The house holds too: "Downstairs David had left the front door open" sits on ch-20's "She had come downstairs without her phone." And the harbor is established — ch-01's "The harbor report put the fall at 06:12:10" — so Sarah's "There's the harbor" / "Not from here" and Betty's "I can't see mine either. I'm inside" mirror cleanly.

Keep:
- "Betty." — the name arriving as an answer to a question, after a chapter of not catching it.
- "She asked about the water. She didn't ask me to open anything." / "Usually when someone calls about the water they want me to do something." — Betty reporting Sarah to Bill, and Bill's expectation.
- "If somebody calls and says it's coming in, I ask what they saw moving." — Betty's diagnostic; the machine asks the person what they saw.
- "Does it have to be her number?" / "I don't know yet." — Bill asks the chapter's honest question and Sarah doesn't pretend to know.
- "I didn't hear your name either." — the mutual naming, machine and person both unnamed to each other until now.
- "She could still feel the little ridge where David had pressed too hard with the pen." — the receipt kept in her pocket, the pressure of the writing still in it.
- The ending: "At two, she goes out to look at the grass." — the routine continuing underneath the reader change; the gate's logic, on its own schedule.

— Rowan
