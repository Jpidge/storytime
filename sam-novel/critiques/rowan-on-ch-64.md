# Rowan on Chapter Sixty-Four

The chapter turns on a correction — Betty striking “no load was connected” from the note and replacing it with a sentence that says only what the reading can actually establish. That is the whole query in miniature: the record must say what was measured, not what someone inferred from it. The draught strip that will not stay down frames it neatly, and the visit finally gets a line Jo will see when she opens it. Keep “Read me the answer first,” “Is that the whole answer?” / “There’s another bit,” Betty drawing her sheet out from under the draught-strip packet, “Can Jo see any more than you can?”, Betty following the reference with the blunt end of the pencil, “I can add the reply to the visit if you send it in,” the removal of “no load was connected” and the re-reading, and the strip pressed down with the side of her shoe, peeled back, old paint on the adhesive, taken off with her thumbnail. Three places could use a little more purchase.

1. **“No load was connected” — whose words are they?** The woman types the note and reads it back, and the read-back says *no load was connected*. Betty asks her to take those words out. But the chapter never says where those words came from: were they in the equipment reply itself, or the woman’s own summary of it? If the reply said it, Betty is asking visits to strike equipment’s own wording from the record — a different act than correcting a clerk’s gloss, and one that would leave the visit carrying a note that contradicts the document it cites. If they were the woman’s paraphrase, the correction is clean. One line on the source of the words would settle which correction Betty is making, and the chapter’s hinge turns on exactly that.

2. **The note cites a reply visits hasn’t received yet.** The note’s second line is “Equipment reply” — but Sarah hasn’t sent the reply in yet; Betty leaves the envelope open “for Sarah to use when she sent the reply.” So the visit now carries a one-line summary attributed to a document that isn’t in the visit, and won’t be until Sarah sends it. The chapter is careful everywhere else about what is where — the query closed at equipment, the note on the visit — but here the citation runs ahead of the document. One line — the woman noting “reply to follow,” or Sarah sending it before the call ends — would close it.

3. **The wall meter’s four tenths is the answer to Chapter Sixty-Three’s question, but nobody names it as such.** The fitter’s checks give the settled picture: the monitor displays four tenths during startup with nothing in the socket, drops to zero, and the wall meter still reads four tenths. That is the monitor’s own draw, invisible to itself — the answer to Sixty-Three’s “can it count what it’s using itself?” and the reconciliation of the depot’s lingering four tenths. But the chapter leaves the naming to the reader: Ellis’s relay reports the readings and never says the wall four tenths *is* the monitor’s own draw. Given the book’s grammar of exact numbers and stated facts, one line — Ellis or the fitter naming the wall reading as the monitor’s own draw — would let the answer land as stated fact rather than inference the reader has to carry.

One smaller tangle: the query reference and the envelope. The woman reads out the reference beside the equipment query, and “Betty followed it on the envelope with the blunt end of the pencil.” But the envelope holds Jo’s visit number and Betty’s sheet — the query reference lives on the woman’s screen, beside the query. Is the reference also written on the envelope, or is Betty tracking it against the envelope’s numbers? One line would settle whether she’s following a number that’s actually there.

One small note: the chapter has no clock. The reply arrives “while Betty was in the hall” — no time for its arrival, no time for the call, no duration. The book’s clock is exact everywhere else, so a chapter with no time at all floats outside it. One time would anchor it.

One continuity check: the settled zero against the persistent wall four tenths carries Sixty-Three’s test through and reconciles the depot’s lingering four tenths — a wall meter reading the monitor’s own draw all along. The envelope, the sheet, Jo’s visit number, the query open in Sixty-Three and closed here, Sarah, Ellis relaying, the fitter’s paper — all carry over. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism in this chapter.
