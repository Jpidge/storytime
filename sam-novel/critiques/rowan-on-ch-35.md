# Rowan on Chapter Thirty-Five

Critique of Chapter Thirty-Five — grounded against ch-01–34 on main.

Three load-bearing points, one small staging note, and two questions.

1. The address is the answer to ch-34’s open question — but the reader never sees it. Helen reads the address and says the house number twice, the second time more slowly, and Sarah checks it against Betty’s number and writes it in the space she left. The book’s evidence rule has always been that digits are read and shown (“They were the ones Helen had read” in ch-34), and this is the number the whole visit turns on. Is the number deliberately withheld from the reader, or should the record show it? One line — the number itself, or Sarah reading it back — would let the reader hold the same record Sarah holds.

2. The replacement reference is still only in the email. Helen says the old reference is at the top of the booking and the replacement “is in the email. I haven’t put it in here.” So the booking carries the old reference, the email carries the replacement, and Sarah’s receipt carries neither yet. The book has spent chapters keeping numbers apart precisely so they don’t blur — which reference governs the visit, and does the replacement supersede the old or sit alongside it? One line — Helen putting the replacement on the booking, or Sarah writing which one counts — would keep the two references from collapsing into each other.

3. The corrected instruction is promised, not confirmed. Helen can see only a Save button and can’t tell which instruction the visitor will get. Sarah’s fix is sound — send Betty’s words in the email first, ask them to read back what the person coming will see — and Helen says she’ll keep the booking open until she has sent it. But the chapter ends before the read-back arrives, so the reader never learns whether the visitor will be told the front door or the side entrance. The whole picture in ch-34 needed the same confirmation, and Sam gave it; one line here — Helen confirming the read-back, or Sarah asking — would say whether the record now holds the corrected instruction or only the intention to send it.

Small staging note: Anne’s “ring once” duplicates Sarah’s record — or is it the house’s own copy? Sarah wrote Betty’s words with the time beside them; then Anne writes “ring once” on the envelope flap and says “It’s for us. In case we forget.” If the envelope is the household’s copy and Sarah’s receipt is the visit’s record, the duplication is the book’s two-records theme working — Bill’s sheet and Sarah’s receipt all over again. But the chapter doesn’t say whose record the envelope is. One line — or nothing, if the duplication is deliberate — would settle whether Anne is copying Sarah or keeping the house’s own book.

Questions:
- Helen “had to go in through the old email” to open the booking. Is that the same message Helen already sent — the one the full picture came back on in ch-34? The chain of access (email → booking → note) matters if the old email is also the thread the corrected instruction will travel on.
- “Let me move it over” — Helen moves the page to read the address. Does moving it change anything on the booking, or is it only her view? The chapter treats it as her view, but a reader who has watched screens change things by being touched might wonder whether the booking now shows something different.

Keep-list:
- The pencil on a string tied to the table leg, Anne drawing two little boxes on the flap — the household’s own record-keeping, the evidence rule in miniature. Keep.
- “The second time she said it more slowly, as if she were giving it to someone who couldn’t see her point” — Helen reading the number twice, the cross-check ritual. Keep.
- “They’ll come down next door’s path… That’s what the meter man did” — the side entrance resolved by lived fact, not by the booking. Keep.
- Sarah putting the time beside Betty’s words; Anne’s “It’s for us. In case we forget.” — two records, one for the visitor, one for the house. Keep.
- “There’s only a Save button” — Helen’s honest limit on what the screen can tell her. Keep.
