# Rowan on Chapter Sixty-Eight

The chapter is the morning after the correction: Jo prints the new note, and the question shifts from what the note says to where it goes and who has read what. The engine is routing — the earlier entry is still there, the new note goes with it, and nobody yet knows whether a correction reaches the same place automatically. Keep the printer beginning while Jo is still putting her coat on; “Which one have they got?” and “If they open the visit”; “That isn’t the same as knowing who’s read it”; “The one that put me there”; the grey mark along the edge where the printer took the page through; the bottom page slipping when the small clip goes over three; “Not yet” to the bigger clip; “Tell me who it is first”; Betty pulling the pencil toward her while Sarah turns over a clean sheet under her hand; and Jo’s cup coming back with the tea bag out, after Sixty-Seven left the tag on the table.

1. **Jo names Betty to the caller before Betty’s gate.** When someone answers, Jo “gave her name and said she was with Betty” — and only then asks, “May I give them your details?” Betty’s answer is “Tell me who it is first.” But Jo has already told an unidentified person that she is with Betty, before asking who they are or getting Betty’s say-so. The chapter stages Betty’s consent gate carefully, and Jo’s opening line has already crossed it. If that is Jo’s haste, it reads as a lapse worth owning on the page; if it isn’t, the identification should come before Jo says who she is with. One line reordering it would keep Betty’s gate intact.

2. **The clip comes home, and the new note is left loose.** Sixty-Seven ended with Betty taking the clip off yesterday’s two pages and laying it beside the envelope, “ready for the new one.” Here she tries it on the three pages, the bottom slips, Sarah offers a bigger one, Betty says “Not yet” — and then she takes the small clip off the three and puts it back on yesterday’s two, laying them to the left of the new copy. The clip’s whole journey returns it to where it started, and the new three-page note — the one that “goes with” the earlier entry and has to travel — lies unclipped at the chapter’s close. Is that what Betty intends, the pages flat with both dates visible, or does “Not yet” mean the bigger clip is still coming for the new note? As it stands the reader can’t tell whether the new note is secured or left loose.

3. **The laptop’s starting place.** “Sarah moved the laptop away from the printer and put it on the table.” Sarah has already been reading the laptop to count the pages, and Jo opens the record on it — but the chapter never says where the printer sits or where the laptop was before it was put on the table. If the printer is on the table, the laptop being moved onto the table implies it was somewhere else, and no other surface has been named. One line placing the printer would settle the small geography; this is a book that stages every placement, and this is the one move whose starting point is off the page.

One continuity check: this is the same Thursday the tenth of December visit as Sixty-Seven, picking up where that chapter ended — Jo’s coat, the clip off yesterday’s two beside the envelope, the copy Jo offered now coming off the printer in three pages; the tea bag out of Jo’s cup follows Sixty-Seven’s tag-on-the-table cup; the chair drawn nearer the socket follows the clock and socket lines; the correction Jo wrote in Sixty-Seven — the monitor’s record as the source of the minute, Betty’s account of the kitchen as given on the first of December — is the note being printed here. The date on the old note is visible to Betty but never given to the reader; in a book where every date is a load-bearing fact, the reader can’t check which day the old note carries. I found no conflict with the locked mechanism — the monitor’s reading gives a time, not a sighting, and the correction’s routing is a record question, not a mechanism question.
