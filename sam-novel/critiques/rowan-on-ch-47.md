# Rowan on Chapter Forty-Seven

Critique of Chapter Forty-Seven — grounded against ch-01–46 on main.

Two load-bearing points, three small staging notes, and three questions. Keep-list at the end.

**1. The grey monitor, Jo's monitor, and the timer slide past each other in the opening exchange — and the pad then writes it down as settled.**

Anne reports what they saw at Betty's: "The clock went round and it said zero." Bill asks, "Which thing said zero?" — "Your grey one." He writes *grey monitor* on the first line and puts a zero beside it.

The grey one is Bill's own monitor: ch-44 establishes it as grey, with a dial where Jo's had a light, and ch-44 ends with Betty's warning that what Bill's does "won't tell us what Jo's did." But the reader coming from ch-46 — where "the monitor" means Jo's throughout, and the open question is whether *Jo's* clock kept time while off — will read this zero as Jo's monitor's clock saying zero at Betty's, which would wrongly settle the ch-46 question a chapter early.

And "the clock went round" is never assigned to a device. The timer's dial goes round (ch-45: "Its dial began to turn"), while the monitor's zero in ch-44/45 is a watts reading — the display showed zero with nothing drawn, then one point eight when the night-light went in. So is the pad's "grey monitor — 0" a watts zero or a clock zero? The two readings on the pad are then hard to compare: the grey monitor's zero (from Betty's) against the bench meter's 0.32 W (the timer's draw, here at the bench). One line keeping the devices apart would fix it — Bill noting the grey one is his bench monitor, not Jo's, and saying what the zero measures.

**2. "The reader" in Mo's reply collides with the door reader.**

"The monitor set its clock from the reader when the depot connected it." In this book, "the reader" has meant the door/badge reader in every prior use — ch-03, ch-04, ch-07, ch-10, ch-11, ch-22, ch-23: the reader that lights green, the old reader at the low door with the missing screw. A reader parsing Mo's sentence with that meaning gets nonsense: a door reader setting a monitor's clock. The clock-setting mechanism is the chapter's central new fact, so it needs its own noun — the depot's reader, the bench reader, whatever Sam intends — so the mechanism is legible on first read.

**Small staging notes**

- *The folded card.* Bill wedges "a folded card" between the window frame and the vice handle so it won't rattle. "Card" is loaded in this book — the service card, the bent card as the control credential (ch-25/26). A reader will wonder, for a beat, whether this is the control credential being used as a shim. Name it scrap, or a folded bit of card, and the beat goes away.
- *The bench pencil.* Anne picks up the shorter pencil "and waited for something to pull it back." The pull-back expectation comes from the kitchen-table pencil (ch-39: string tied to the table leg; ch-40: the pencil remains tethered). The bench pencils sit in a groove; no string is established for them. One line to settle whether the bench pencil is tied, or whether she's just remembering the kitchen one.
- *Betty's copying.* Ch-46 ends with Betty wanting to copy the word in the grey strip (*Startup*) before the screen went dark, and asking Sarah to leave the picture open. Ch-47 opens scene two with the picture still open and Mo's reply underneath it, but the copying is never shown. Close the loop, or say she did it.

**Questions**

1. The battery is new — no "battery" in ch-01–46; Mo introduces it as the mechanism ("It had a battery to keep time between readings. If the battery ran flat, the monitor could start again with the wrong time."). Betty asks, "Can she tell if the battery's flat now?" — the question that would settle whether Jo's clock can be trusted going forward — and Sarah's hands go to the keys, but the question that ends up on the screen is the backward-looking one ("what it said before they set it"). Was the battery-flat question meant to go in the same message, or is it deliberately held for the next one?
2. When did the depot connect Jo's monitor and set its clock? The depot's reply was dated Monday the twenty-third (ch-44) — is there a date for the connection itself in the record, so the pre-set reading Betty asks for can be placed?
3. The zero at Betty's is Anne's report of a sighting the reader never saw — the bench scene opens by asking her to tell Bill what they saw. Is that deliberate (her report is the evidence, and its vagueness is the point), or should the Betty's moment be on the page?

**Keep-list**

- The minute-wait test: timed by the independent clock above the bench, Anne's thumb beside the mark — the ch-45 dial-stops rule re-proved cleanly, with a second witness.
- "If you just looked at that, you wouldn't know we'd waited." Anne names the book's central problem — a stopped device leaves no trace of the gap — in one line.
- Mo's reply states the restart flag's limit plainly ("couldn't tell from that row whether the clock had kept running"). The ch-46 question is kept open honestly, not fudged.
- Betty's follow-up ("Ask what it said before they set it, if they wrote that anywhere") is the right next evidence — the pre-set reading would show the drift.
- The pad with both readings on one page pays off ch-46's promise ("We can put both readings on the same page").
- The pencil expecting a pull — Anne has learned how the pencils behave.
- Scene two staging: the reply under the picture, Betty rinsing the cups, moving the chair so she can sit beside Sarah while she sends it.
