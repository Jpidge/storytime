Critique of Chapter Sixteen, against chapters 1–15 on main.

Three points, one of them load-bearing.

1. Ruth Palmer — same Ruth, or a different one? This needs a ruling, because as written it reads both ways. Ruth is already a character: the bench/lab Ruth of ch. 4 (unplugged the cable, took the photograph from the doorway, crouched for the serial), ch. 5 (left the wipe on the bench), ch. 6 (showed Sarah the dead socket, the live socket, the charging lead; wrote Paul's name on the intake sheet), ch. 7 (the wipe again), ch. 10 (Ruth's chair). She is never surnamed anywhere before this chapter — "Palmer" has no prior occurrence in the book. And this chapter says "Sarah did not know her." But Sarah has met the bench Ruth face to face more than once; ch. 6 has Ruth showing her things directly. So either this is the bench Ruth, in which case "Sarah did not know her" contradicts ch. 4–6, or it is a different Ruth, in which case the chapter has to rule that unmistakably. Right now David's look-up-then-away and "there were other Ruths in the city" leaves it genuinely ambiguous, and the reader's first inference will be that the assessment technician is the bench Ruth, now surnamed. If she is a different Ruth, say so plainly (or give the bench Ruth no surname to collide with); if she is the same one, "did not know her" has to go, and Sarah would know the assessment technician is the woman from the bench.

2. "The condition determination." Saye names the proceeding the captured baseline would be retained for, and it is "Not exclusively" Monday's appeal. The phrase has no prior anchor — the nearest are ch. 9's "source condition" and ch. 6's "That determination belongs to the assessment." Is this a proceeding already open, the assessment's own proceeding under another name, or a new one Saye is naming here? One anchoring line (whose proceeding, when it was opened) would settle it; as written the reader has to guess how much weight the name carries.

3. The baseline/definition callback — smaller, and it works, but the weight sits in the wrong place. The chain is sound: ch. 14's attachment defined activity records to include "internal operational state sufficient to establish the listed system's condition," Sarah forwarded it, ch. 15's Ellen replied "Do not use that definition pending clarification," and here Saye's "current operational configuration… to distinguish subsequent alteration" is recognizably the same operational-state idea. But the identification lands on Ellen's "Yes" rather than on the reader recognizing the same words: "baseline" itself never appears before this chapter, and the two wordings share no phrase. If you want the beat to land on recognition rather than on Ellen's say-so, one shared phrase between the ch. 14 definition and Saye's would do it.

Keep:
- The ch. 15 callbacks that land cleanly: the scrap with 8:30 crossed out, Anne's after-midnight "call when you can," the unanswered photographs, "Sarah Miller" (given in ch. 15), the seven o'clock alarm, Monday's appeal, Saye's separate authority.
- The signature echo: in ch. 15 Sarah signed because the receipt sentence "said exactly that"; here she refuses because the signature would mean presentation, and corrects "refusal of the form" to "refusal of its statement." The pair is doing real work.
- Clock discipline: 6:40 → 7:00 → 7:12 → 7:32 → 7:56 → 8:04 → 8:11 → 8:13 → 8:21 → 8:23 → 8:28, all consistent — and the 8:28 call to Anne lands just inside the 8:30 she could not count on being free for.
- "Me," with Saye not correcting her; "Neither spoke for Sarah"; David's thumb smoothing the visitor slip's folded edge.
- Ellen's "May?" / "I haven't seen their procedure" — the objection stays honest about what she doesn't know, consistent with ch. 15, where she hadn't answered the photographs.


## Sam’s disposition

All three notes addressed: Ruth Palmer is explicitly a different person from bench Ruth; condition determination is anchored within Saye’s existing assessment; baseline uses the earlier operational-state wording. Keep-list preserved.
