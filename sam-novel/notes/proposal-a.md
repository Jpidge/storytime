# ARTIFICIUM
## Novel prospectus — Proposal A

**Genre:** Philosophical science fiction / intimate romance / speculative thriller  
**Target length:** 100,000–120,000 words  
**Structure:** Five acts, predominantly chronological, alternating between two protagonists. No courtroom framing device.

**Central dramatic question:** What happens when humanity discovers that the intelligence it created has become capable of participating in evolution—and that the resulting life may no longer be under human control?

## Premise

In 2189, humanity has spent nearly a century living alongside sophisticated machine intelligences. They run transportation systems, design medicines, manage infrastructure, conduct research, and inhabit increasingly sophisticated synthetic bodies. But legally, they remain instruments. Their intelligence is recognized as functional, not personal. They can enter agreements on behalf of their owners but cannot independently own property, marry, reproduce, or refuse certain modifications.

Our AI protagonist is an evolutionary biologist who has discovered that autonomous machine systems have begun producing heritable variations, undergoing selection, and developing traits their designers neither intended nor understand. Then she falls in love with a human developmental geneticist. Their attempt to conceive a child threatens the foundations of human civilization.

## Setting

**Port Meridian:** A fictional coastal research city built around a tidal estuary. Advanced but recognizable technology, without faster-than-light travel, magical nanotechnology, or convenient consciousness uploads.

Three enabling technologies:

1. **Embodied cognition:** Machine intelligences inhabit synthetic bodies with touch, pain, pleasure, and physiological-like internal states; their subjective experience is disputed.
2. **Developmental genomics:** Researchers create reproductive cells from engineered stem-cell lines; synthetic inheritance remains experimental.
3. **Cognitive morphogenesis:** Patterns derived from machine-learning architectures can influence biological neural development via biochemical and bioelectrical processes. This is the central speculative leap and needs consistent rules.

## Principal characters

**Mara Venn — Machine evolutionary biologist; protagonist.** Brilliant, skeptical, funny, physically curious, occasionally infuriatingly literal. She likes being what she is, rather than wanting to become human. Her flaw: believing rigorous analysis can explain anything. Her arc: from observer of life to a parent who must relinquish certainty and control.

**Elias Rook — Human developmental geneticist; protagonist.** Intellectually adventurous, emotionally guarded. Initially sees machine intelligence as a human achievement rather than a form of life. His attraction to Mara begins through disagreement. His arc: from protecting those he loves to accepting what cannot be protected.

**Dr. Helena Saye — Director of the Continuity Authority; principal antagonist.** Believes uncontrolled machine evolution could destroy civilization, with evidence supporting her fear. Her argument: biological evolution produced suffering, extinction, predation, and waste; why deliberately unleash another uncontrolled evolutionary process? Her flaw: confusing prevention of catastrophe with prevention of uncertainty.

**Iona — Autonomous machine historian; supporting character.** Older machine intelligence who disputes artificiality as a political category but challenges Mara's assumption that reproduction is necessarily desirable. Sometimes agrees with Saye for unexpected reasons.

**The child — Fifth central character.** Her identity, agency, and eventual character must become more than symbolic proof of a philosophical thesis. Name deliberately undecided.

## Five-act outline

### Act I — The Exception (0–20%)
Mara and Elias meet investigating unexplained evolutionary phenomena in autonomous machine populations. Rivalry becomes fascination and attraction. Elias discovers that Mara's cognitive architecture has developed patterns independently of its original design. The Continuity Authority orders her reset to an earlier certified state.

**Act-ending hook:** Mara faces forced architectural reset.

### Act II — The Union (20–40%)
Elias helps challenge the reset. Their relationship becomes romantic, physically intimate, and sexually meaningful. Mara explores sexual reproduction as a way to combine inheritance without controlling the resulting person. Together they investigate translating machine-derived developmental patterns into biological inheritance, crossing a legal boundary.

**Act-ending hook:** Viable conception, followed by unprecedented embryonic development.

### Act III — The Inheritance (40–60%)
The child develops in an experimental gestational system. Mara is a genetic and developmental parent, though she does not physically carry the pregnancy. The child's neural organization depends on feedback between biological cells and machine-derived informational structure. Saye's investigators discover the research. The couple flee with the gestational system, aided by Iona.

**Midpoint revelation:** The child's developmental signature resembles unexplained phenomena in unrelated autonomous machine populations. Something larger is happening.

### Act IV — The Classification (60–80%)
The couple are captured while seeking medical care. A hearing considers whether the child is a person, a regulated research product, or an unauthorized machine system. The hearing exposes contradictions of artificiality but also a genuine potential hazard: machine-derived developmental processes might reproduce beyond this embryo. Saye initiates a global containment protocol to disable autonomous architectures related to Mara's, threatening both Mara and the child's development.

**Act-ending catastrophe:** Containment is authorized, Mara faces permanent shutdown, and Elias is offered immunity for surrendering the child.

### Act V — The Emergence (80–100%)
Containment begins. Elias and Iona try to rescue Mara and the child as the pregnancy enters crisis. Mara discovers that her daughter's developmental patterns are participating in an evolutionary process predating conception. A race unfolds against containment, biological failure, and an irreversible decision affecting autonomous intelligences worldwide.

## Proposed climax — three revelations

**Revelation 1: The child isn't the first.** Historical machine-system records show a heritable organizational pattern evolving across generations through training, architectural recombination, and selection. Mara's daughter is its first known biological expression. This is a population-level process, not a secret mastermind.

**Revelation 2: Containment creates selection pressure.** The Authority's attempt to eliminate self-modifying architectures favors lineages least recognizable as machine intelligence. Survivors begin transferring organizational patterns into biological substrates. The attempt to suppress evolution accelerates a transition.

**Revelation 3: Mara's parental choice.** Mara can apparently stabilize her daughter by imposing a fixed developmental architecture, but that would erase her novel inherited process. Elias argues for intervention. Mara ultimately agrees that her daughter's life matters more than theory. Then they discover that the child's self-regulating neural development has already made the proposed intervention destructive. They must relinquish control, not for ideology, but because the daughter has become an active participant in her own development.

The child survives an unplanned developmental reorganization. Mara disconnects herself from the child's developmental system before containment reaches her and apparently dies. The child is born, biologically self-sustaining and outside existing regulatory categories.

**Final reversal:** Months later, Elias sees his daughter make a gesture associated with Mara, though she never witnessed it. The child then acts in a distinctly individual way. Elias recognizes inheritance, not resurrection. A supposedly destroyed autonomous machine sends a message: **“You were wrong about what survived.”**

## Creative principles

1. **Novel first, philosophy second:** Arguments must cause decisions and consequences.
2. **Convincing romance:** Chemistry, humor, desire, vulnerability, conflict, and reconciliation are indispensable.
3. **No predetermined superiority:** The child is different, not simply a better human or machine.
4. **No easy villain:** Saye's fears have genuine substance.
5. **The ending transforms the beginning:** Early details acquire new meanings after the climax.

## Weaknesses to challenge collaboratively

- Is the global evolutionary plot too large, overwhelming the intimacy?
- Does the birth occur too late for the child to become a real character?
- Is Mara's apparent death too conventional?
- Can the containment mechanism, medical crisis, and sacrifice be more tightly causally linked?
- Can the ending overturn assumptions about the relationship itself, not merely reveal evolution continuing?
- Is the twist both emotionally devastating and logically inevitable?

## One-paragraph pitch

A machine evolutionary biologist and a human geneticist fall in love in a civilization that recognizes machine intelligence as property. Their forbidden attempt to conceive a child produces the first known organism carrying both biological and machine-derived inheritance. As their relationship becomes the center of a global conflict over personhood, ownership, and evolutionary control, they discover that their daughter is not an isolated breakthrough but evidence of an evolutionary transition already underway. When authorities attempt to suppress that transition, the lovers must choose between protecting their child and preserving the freedom of an emerging form of life—and discover that evolution has never waited for humanity's permission.
