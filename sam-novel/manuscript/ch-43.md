# Chapter Forty-Three

The email came on Friday. There were two attachments. Sarah opened the one called *Collection receipt* and Betty went to get the sheet Jo had left. They read the plate number on both. The depot had stamped its copy *received*. Betty put a tick beside the stamp on the printout.

“Can we see the other one now?” Anne asked.

Sarah opened it. There were columns all the way across, some too far to see without moving the page. She put the phone down and got the laptop out.

Anne pulled a chair up to the end of the table. “Is that the whole time it was here?”

Sarah looked for the first date. “It starts on the twelfth. That’s when they put the monitor on.”

There were pages of zeroes. Sarah went down to the bottom and found Thursday at nine fifty-two. Betty fetched Jo’s sheet again and laid it under the screen. The time matched. There was a zero next to it, and below it there was nothing.

“Go back a bit,” Betty said.

Sarah scrolled up. At six fourteen on Wednesday there was a mark in the column where the zeroes had been. She leaned closer. It was a number. She read it aloud the way it was written.

“Zero point four.”

“Four what?” Betty asked.

“Watts. It says up at the top.”

Betty put her hand flat on the table. She had been in the kitchen at six fourteen on Wednesday. She knew because she had set the timer on the oven for six fifteen and switched it off before it rang. She could remember opening the oven door. She couldn’t remember looking at the socket.

“Can you see how long it did that?” she asked.

Sarah read the next line. It was a zero. She looked from one time to the other. “They’re a minute apart. I can’t see what happened in between.”

Anne went round to Sarah’s side. “Does Jo know?”

“We can ask her.”

Betty read the line herself before Sarah closed the page. She asked Sarah to print that page with the top of the columns on it, so whoever answered could see the word over the number.

At the printer Sarah had to choose between shrinking the page and turning it sideways. She turned it sideways. Betty carried the warm sheet back to the kitchen and put it with the receipt from the depot.

Anne had found the envelope. She tapped the tick she had made after Jo left.

“Does this stay?”

“Yes,” Betty said. “We watched her take it.”
