# Chapter Forty-Three

The depot’s email came on Friday the twentieth. There were two attachments. Sarah opened the one called *Collection receipt* and Betty went to get the sheet Jo had left. They read the plate number on both. The attachment was a scan of the depot’s copy, with *received* stamped across the bottom.

“Can we see the other one now?” Anne asked.

Sarah opened it. There were columns all the way across, some too far to see without moving the page. She put the phone down and got the laptop out.

Anne brought a chair from the hall. Betty’s chair was still beside the socket. “Is that the whole time it was here?”

Sarah looked for the first date. “It starts on the twelfth. That’s when they put the monitor on.”

There were pages of zeroes. Sarah went down to the bottom and found Thursday at nine fifty-two. Betty fetched Jo’s sheet again and laid it under the screen. The time matched. There was a zero next to it, and below it there was nothing.

“Go back a bit,” Betty said.

Sarah scrolled up. At six fourteen on Wednesday the eighteenth there was a mark in the column where the zeroes had been. She leaned closer. It was a number. She read it aloud the way it was written.

“Zero point four.”

“Four what?” Betty asked.

“Watts. It says up at the top.”

Betty put her hand flat on the table. She had been in the kitchen at six fourteen on Wednesday. She knew because she had set the timer on the oven for six fifteen and switched it off before it rang. She could remember opening the oven door. She couldn’t remember looking at the socket.

“Can you see how long it did that?” she asked.

Sarah read the next line. It was a zero. She looked from one time to the other. “It says one sample every sixty seconds. I can’t see what happened in between.”

Anne went round to Sarah’s side. “Does Jo know?”

“We can ask the depot. They can ask Jo if they need to.”

“Could it be the block itself?” Betty asked.

Sarah looked at the column again. “It doesn’t say what the monitor counts. I’ll put that in the email.”

Betty read the line herself before Sarah closed the page. She asked Sarah to print that page with the top of the columns on it, so whoever answered could see the word over the number.

At the printer Sarah had to choose between shrinking the page and turning it sideways. She turned it sideways. The date and the column headings were both on the warm sheet. Betty carried it back to the kitchen and put it beside the depot’s receipt. Sarah sent the question with the dated row attached.

Anne had found the envelope. She tapped the tick she had made after Jo left.

“Does this stay?”

“Yes,” Betty said. “We watched her take it.” Betty left the tick where it was and put the printout beside the envelope.
