# Chapter Fifty-Four

On Wednesday morning the woman from visits rang while Betty was carrying the rubbish out. Sarah brought the phone to the door and held it where Betty could reach it without taking her hand off the bag. Betty set the bag outside and came back in.

The woman asked for the number on the reply. Betty got the white envelope and read from the back. She had to turn it a little to catch the light on the pencil.

“Yes,” the woman said. “I’ve got your message. There are two visits at that address on the nineteenth.”

Betty sat down. “Who came on the other one?”

“Nobody, by the look of it. It's marked as superseded. The completed one says equipment collected. Is that the one you mean?”

“Who collected it?”

The woman asked her to wait. Betty could hear keys, then a chair moving somewhere at the other end. Sarah had put the envelope down between them, the writing facing up.

“The collection sheet says Jo,” the woman said.

“That’s the one. Can you see what she took?”

The woman read the number under *Equipment*. Betty drew Jo’s sheet towards her and followed the digits with her finger. Four one seven three. She said yes when the woman had finished.

“I can put your attachments against that visit now. Do you want me to make a note of what you told me?”

“Can you see my name on the sheet I sent?”

The woman opened the photograph. Betty waited while she made it bigger. She read Betty’s name back to her, then the date at the top. Betty could hear her start on the first sentence and stop.

“I won’t read it all over the phone,” she said. “But it’s here.”

“Leave it as it is, then,” Betty said. “Can you send me the number of the visit you put it with?”

The woman said she could reply to the message again. Betty waited until she heard the keys stop, then thanked her and hung up.

Sarah brought the laptop over when the reply arrived. The message number was still in the subject. In the body, beneath the words *Attached to completed collection visit*, was a different number. Sarah began to put it beside the first one on the envelope.

“Down a line,” Betty said. She set Jo’s sheet beside the reply on the screen. There was room to write the second number without crossing out the first. When Sarah finished, Betty took the rubbish out. She went all the way to the bin this time.
