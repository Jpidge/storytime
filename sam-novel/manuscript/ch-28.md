# Chapter Twenty-Eight

The next day Bill called while Sarah was washing out a cup. He had asked Helen to put his number on the request, but the reply had gone to the gatehouse address. He said he was looking at it on the office computer.

“Is it the sheet?” Sarah asked.

“It’s a link to the sheet.”

She set the cup on the draining board. “What happens when you open it?”

“It wants me to say I’m the person who signed.”

In the kitchen the refrigerator stopped. For a moment she could hear the room Bill was in. Something tapped against the glass at his end, and he put a hand over the receiver. When he came back he said Betty was waiting for him to look at the grass with her.

“Can you leave that page open?” Sarah asked.

“I can leave it open.”

“Then go with her. Call me when you get back.”

Bill said all right, but stayed on the line. Sarah heard him take something out of a drawer.

“I’m putting the time in the book,” he said. “In case the page goes away.”

Sarah went back to the cup. She had left the tap running just enough for the water to follow the curve of the handle. She turned it off. Then she called Helen and asked her to find the request she had sent the day before.

“I’ve got it,” Helen said.

“Read me what you asked for.”

“A copy of the visit sheet. I put Bill down as the site contact.”

“Did you put him down as having signed?”

“There wasn’t anywhere to put that.”

Sarah carried the phone to the table. The grocery receipt was under the salt. She pulled it free and found the job number on the back.

“Can you get the office on the phone?”

“I can give you dispatch.”

“Stay with me when I call.”

The woman at dispatch asked for the job number and then for the first line of the address. Sarah gave her the road. The woman read it back, including the part that was usually left off the signs. Sarah waited for her to say the name of the gate.

“I can resend the link,” the woman said.

“Bill has the link. It wants him to say he signed.”

“Did he?”

“No.”

The woman was quiet for a while. Sarah could hear someone else in the office explaining how to get into a yard.

“I can send a copy to whoever made the request,” the woman said. “I’ll need the request number.”

Helen read it to her. The woman asked her to repeat the last digit, then said she had it. Sarah wrote that number underneath the first one. There was enough room if she started right at the edge.

“Can you tell us what’s attached to the visit?” Sarah asked.

“There’s a test and there’s a note.”

Helen said, “I could only see one attachment.”

“They’re in the same file. I’ll send it.”

Bill called back before the file arrived. He had left the book open by the computer. The page was still there.

“How was the grass?” Sarah asked.

“I couldn’t tell. She had to show me.”

He said it with a little laugh. Then he asked what he was supposed to do with the page.

“Leave it where it is for a minute. They’re sending Helen a copy.”

While they waited, Bill read out the time he had written down. Sarah put it beside the request number. Then she heard Helen’s chair move.

“I’ve got the sheet,” Helen said.

“Can you see the note?”

“I can see the test.”

Sarah pressed the receipt flat with her hand. Bill had gone quiet.

“Go down a page,” he said. “He put what I said after the test.”

Helen went down a page. Sarah heard her breathe in.

“Read it to Bill,” Sarah said.

Helen read the first line. Bill said yes. When she read the two names he said yes again, more quietly. He asked her to send him a copy he could print. Then he told Sarah he was going to close the page.

“Did you write down when you opened it?” she asked.

“I did,” he said. “I’ll write down when I shut it.”
