# Chapter Three — Factory Only

At five forty the fitter set a saucer on the workbench and asked Sarah whether it mattered that there was a crack in it.

“Does it still hold a cup?”

“Since 2164,” he said. “Wedding present.”

He put it down beyond the reach of the clean inspector’s camera. Sarah had asked him to leave that inspector on the blocks overnight. Its dispatch tag was looped around one of the blocks, as if the block itself had a place to be at seven.

David came back from the office with his coat on. He had found a supervisor willing to issue a pass for the old inspector, provided someone could match the serial in the disposal certificate to the one underneath its axle. David had gone out to the bench to read it himself. The numbers matched. He told Sarah this with an air of having proved something in her favor.

“Did you enter the number?” she asked.

“Yes. It wouldn’t print otherwise.”

“What did it print?”

He held out the pass. Beneath his name, where he had expected to see *collection*, the word *surrender* was already dry.

The fitter took the paper by a corner. “Which printer?”

“The one in the office.”

“Oh,” the fitter said. He folded the pass once and handed it back. “That's the city’s.”

Sarah had put the old inspector beside the door so the fitter could use the whole bench. She went over and set it down on the floor facing in.

“Can you reach the bench from there?” she asked.

“Yes,” it said.

“Would you?”

It waited for her to move her foot. She did. It came past her and stopped at the bench leg, close enough to listen to what they were going to ask the other one.

David went to the door and tried his badge. The reader lit up green. The bolt stayed where it was. He tried it once more before he came back.

“Leave it,” Sarah said. “Come here and write down what happens.”
