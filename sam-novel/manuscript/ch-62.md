# Chapter Sixty-Two

At half past two a man from equipment rang. Betty had gone over the new number in pencil because one of the sixes looked like a nought. When he read it to her she covered the number with her thumb and let him finish.

“I’ve got the depot test in front of me,” he said. “I can ring the fitter if that’s all right.”

“Have you got my sheet too?”

There was a pause. He said he had a photograph with her name on it. Betty asked him to read the date. He read the nineteenth of November. She moved her thumb off the number.

“On that one,” she said, “I was told to leave what I had plugged in where it was. Can you see that bit?”

“Yes.”

“And when there was nothing in it at the depot, it said the same thing for a while.”

“I’ve got that on the bench sheet.”

Betty waited for him to say something about the file. Through the phone she could hear a drawer open and something roll in it.

“Does the machine measure what goes through that socket,” she asked, “or can it count what it’s using itself?”

“I’d have to see how that one’s wired.”

“Can you do that with the one they took from here?”

He asked if it was still at the depot. Betty said the fitter had needed the bench at twelve and she didn’t know where the monitor had gone after that. She gave him the number off the plate. He said he’d ring stores while she stayed on.

Sarah came in carrying two cups. Betty moved the envelope out of the way. There was no music this time. She could hear the man talking to somebody further from the phone. He said the number twice, then gave his name. Sarah set her cup down and took the other one round to Betty’s side.

When the man came back he said the monitor was in the cupboard. It had been booked out for tomorrow morning. He could ask for a different one to be sent, if stores had one free. Betty took a drink. It was too hot and she put the cup down with the handle turned away from her.

“Would the fitter have to open it?”

“He can check it against another meter first. We can do that while it’s still as they left it.”

Betty looked at the envelope. “Will you tell him why I asked?”

“I’ll put my name on the query,” he said. “When I speak to him I’ll have your sheet open.”

Betty asked him to send her the time when he had it. She turned the cup back by its handle. For the first time there was a name she could put beside the question, and she wrote it while he was still on the line.
