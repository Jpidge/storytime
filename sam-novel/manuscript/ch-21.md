# Chapter Twenty-One

Helen called while David was still copying the number. He picked up with the pen in his hand.

“Did you get both pictures?”

“Yes. Can I put you on the table?”

“Who’s there?”

He told her. Sarah waited for him to add why they were looking. He set the phone down instead.

“Do you want me to tell her?” he asked.

Sarah shook her head. “I can.”

She told Helen about the number on the manifest, then about the collection request. She read the words that said the room had reached the end of its temporary accommodation. At the other end of the call, Helen set something down hard enough to make the speaker crackle.

“Sorry,” she said. “I had a drawer open.”

David turned the grocery receipt over and back again. “Can you find the other serial in the current system?”

“I tried. It offered to correct my search.”

“To what?”

“The one I’d already looked up.”

“Did it say why?”

“No. I tried twice because I thought I’d put a digit in wrong. Then I asked the storekeeper to bring the binder back.”

Sarah looked at the picture. The staple had gone through both numbers at the left edge. The pen strokes curved around it. Whoever had written them had done so after the slip was attached.

“Can we see the page before this one?” she asked.

“Do you mean the other side of the receipt?”

“The page in the binder. The one before the fold.”

Helen was quiet for a moment.

“I can ask him. He has lunch at noon.”

“So do we,” David said.

Helen laughed. Sarah could hear a drawer slide shut.

“Write down what you want to see,” Helen said. “I don’t want to get him back from lunch and ask him for the wrong thing.”

Sarah pulled the grocery receipt toward her. David lifted his pen off it but kept his hand there until she had the corner.
