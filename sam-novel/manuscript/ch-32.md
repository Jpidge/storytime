# Chapter Thirty-Two

Anne had sewn the corner shut before Helen called back. She put a teaspoon in the bag and shook it over the table. The spoon struck the cloth twice and stayed where it was.

“Can I tell her it works?” she asked.

“You can tell her what you tried.”

Helen had called from outside. Sarah heard a door swing shut behind her and then the sound of traffic.

“They’ve sent me a picture,” Helen said. “I asked for the two references. They’ve sent me a picture of where to click.”

“Is there anything written on it?”

“There’s an arrow.”

Sarah moved the receipt away from the spoon. The ink on the bottom line had dried darker at the end, where she’d waited for the man to read the last digit.

“Can you send me the whole reply?”

Helen said yes. She was quiet a little longer, and when she spoke again the traffic had gone.

“There is a number in the corner of the picture,” she said. “It’s neither of ours.”

“Can you make it out?”

Helen read it to her. It was shorter than either of the numbers on the receipt. Sarah wrote it at the side, where the paper began to curl.

“Is there a word beside it?”

“Give me a second. I’m trying to get the whole thing on the screen.”

Anne tipped the spoon back into her hand. A little of the blue thread had caught in the bowl. She pulled it free and put the spoon in the sink.

“Here,” Helen said. “It says *site*.”

Sarah moved the salt and turned the receipt round. She could see where she had put the tick after Bill read the number from both pages. She had left room to write another job number. She hadn’t left room for a site.

“Can you look that up without opening the link?”

“I can try it in the directory.”

Sarah waited. The picture arrived on her phone while Helen was looking. The arrow covered part of the button. Whoever had drawn it had gone over the point twice.

“It’s Pier Six,” Helen said.

Sarah set the phone down on the table. “Can you look up South Gate the same way?”

Helen asked her to wait again. Anne picked up the bag and put it in her coat pocket, then took it out to see whether the string had gone in with it.

“I’ve got South Gate,” Helen said. “It has a different site number.”

“Send me both pages.”

“Just the pages?”

“The part that shows how you got there, too.”

Helen said she’d have to go back to the search and do it again. Sarah listened while she did. The first picture showed the number above the name. The next showed the search with South Gate still in the box. She kept them in the order they came.

“Should I write back?” Helen asked.

“Ask which site they want you to move the visit to. Give them the two names and the two site numbers.”

Helen read the message back. Sarah asked her to add the street address of the gate before she sent it. She heard Helen say the road aloud as she typed.

Anne stood by the kitchen door with her coat on. “Are we going now?”

Sarah folded the receipt along its old crease. “I’ll call first.”

Bill answered on the second ring. She asked if Betty was there. He started to say what time she would be back, then stopped. From farther away Sarah heard the door open and Bill ask if she wanted the phone.

Anne took the bag out of her pocket and set it beside Sarah’s hand.
