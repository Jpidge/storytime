# Chapter Twenty-Nine

The printer pulled the second page in at an angle. Bill took his hand off the phone and caught the paper before it dropped behind the desk.

“Is it all there?” Sarah asked.

He flattened it against the book. “I can see what I said.”

“Does it have the job number on it?”

There was a pause while he looked. He read the number from the first page and then from the second. Sarah put a tick beside the number on her receipt. She heard him take the pages apart again.

“Who do I give it to if they send somebody else?” he asked.

Through the kitchen window Sarah could see Anne at the washing line. She had taken the pegs out of the bag and was putting them back one at a time. The phone was warm against Sarah’s face.

“Keep that copy,” she said. “Can you print another?”

Bill said he could. Then Betty asked something from the other side of the office. Bill moved the receiver away from his mouth.

“She wants to know which name’s on the top,” he said.

“Pier Six,” Bill said. “It’s on both pages.”

Betty said something Sarah couldn’t make out.

“Hang on,” Bill told her. “I’ll put you where she can hear.”

There was a scrape and then Betty’s voice came up close. “Is that where you used to go?”

“Yes.”

“Is it a gate?”

“It’s a pier. You can walk right to the end of it without going through a door.”

Betty was quiet. Sarah could hear the printer start again. Out at the washing line Anne stretched the bag open to see if there were any pegs left in the bottom.

“Does it say our name anywhere?” Betty asked.

“Bill said it was in the note.”

“I can see that,” Betty said. “It’s where he says they got it wrong.”

Bill put the phone down again. The printer stopped and Sarah heard two sheets being lifted together.

“I’m putting one in the book,” he said.

“Could you put the time on it?”

He asked which time. She had meant the time he printed it, but there were already two times in the book and two on the sheet. She asked him to write *printed* beside the one he was about to add. He read it back as he wrote it. Betty was still close enough to the phone that Sarah could hear her breathing.

Someone knocked at the gatehouse door. Bill said he’d call back, but a moment later he was still on the line.

“It’s the same man,” he said.

“Did he call first?”

“No. He’s brought a box.”

Sarah pushed the kitchen window up. The frame stuck halfway. She called Anne in and held the phone away from her ear until she heard Bill speak again.

“He wants to know if we’ve got the new card.”

“He took it with him.”

“I know. I’m telling him that.”

Sarah heard the installer answer, too far from the receiver to make out the words. Then Bill said, “I’ve got the sheet here. Do you want to look?”

The man came nearer. Sarah heard him say that he had a sheet of his own. There was a sound like a stiff page being pulled out of a sleeve.

“What number have you got?” Bill asked.

The man read it aloud. Sarah followed the first group on the receipt and lost him in the second. Bill asked him to start again. She drew a line below the job number so she could write the other one without crossing anything out.
