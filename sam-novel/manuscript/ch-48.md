# Chapter Forty-Eight

Mo’s email came with two pictures on Saturday the twenty-eighth. Sarah opened the first while Betty was sorting the papers on the table. It showed a line in the depot book with the monitor’s number and a time beside it.

“Thursday the nineteenth, eleven twenty-three,” Sarah read.

Betty took Jo’s sheet from the pile. “That’s the day it went.”

“The time they connected it,” Sarah said. “It says *download* in the next column.”

The second picture showed a slip clipped to the back of the page. Someone had written *clock slow* on it. Mo said it was the fitter’s note about the monitor, made at the Thursday download before the computer set its time. There was no number beside the words.

“Slow by how much?” Betty asked.

Sarah enlarged the picture until they could see the edge of the clip. There was nothing underneath it but a ruled line.

Mo had put the result of the battery test in the email. It had failed on Friday the twentieth. She had written that she didn’t know what it would have done on Wednesday. Betty asked Sarah to print the email with the pictures.

Anne came in carrying Bill’s pad. He had let her take the sheet out, but she’d thought it would be easier to bring the whole pad and show Betty where to look. She opened it at the page with the two readings.

“Which one’s yours?” Betty asked.

Anne pointed to the second line, where her writing was smaller than Bill’s. “I wrote that one. I put the minute on the back.”

Betty turned the page over. The pencil had gone through hard enough to leave ridges. Anne read out what she’d written: *Unplugged for one minute by the wall clock. Mark stayed still.*

“Could we do that with the one from here?” she asked.

Sarah looked back at Mo’s email. “We can ask them to do it at the depot. They still have the one Jo took.”

Betty put the slip with the words *clock slow* beside Anne’s page. The wall clock at Bill’s had a second hand. Betty looked up at the second hand on the kitchen clock. “They’ll need a clock they can see while they’re doing it.”

“Ask them to write down both times after they’ve had it off for a minute and before they connect it to the computer again,” she said. “The time on it and the time they have.”

Sarah wrote the question. Anne waited until she had sent it, then closed the pad and put it under her arm.

“Can I take the pencil back too?”

Betty looked at the pencil on the table. It was the short one from the groove at Bill’s bench. She put a finger on the kitchen pencil’s string so Anne could lift the other one clear.
