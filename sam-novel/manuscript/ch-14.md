# Chapter Fourteen

At eight-seventeen Sarah went back for her phone.

David stayed in the bedroom. She had asked him to, and he had understood that much without turning it into a question.

There was no answer from Ellen.

There was a receipt from Midwest.

The company had acknowledged the preservation request on behalf of the licensed system. Sarah read the phrase standing barefoot beside the table, with the chair still pulled out and her unfinished water beside it.

She opened a reply.

*You have not acknowledged this on my behalf.*

Before she sent it, she checked the recipient field.

The reply would go to Midwest alone.

She added Ellen’s address, then read her sentence again. The company had acknowledged something. What she meant was that it had no right to make the acknowledgment hers.

She deleted the first sentence.

*Midwest’s acknowledgment is its own. Please retain my request for clarification separately.*

She sent that.

In the bedroom a floorboard creaked. David had moved, but he did not come out.

Sarah picked up her glass and emptied it into the sink. She wanted another drink and wanted, absurdly, to avoid leaving a second unfinished thing.

“Can I come in?” David called.

“It’s your kitchen.”

“That wasn’t what I asked.”

She smiled before she could prevent it.

“Yes.”

He wore his shirt unbuttoned, carrying neither phone nor advice. He leaned against the counter and waited while she filled the glass.

“They answered for me,” she said.

“Ellen?”

“Midwest.”

He held out his hand. She gave him the phone.

He read only the receipt she had opened, then handed it back.

“What did you say?”

She showed him her reply.

“Do you want me to read it?” he asked.

“I already sent it.”

“All right.”

She drank. The water tasted faintly of the old pipe.

“I wanted to tell you before I sent it,” she said.

“You didn’t have to.”

“I know. I wanted to.”

He touched her wrist.

There was a difference she could feel and could not yet explain: asking him to help because he knew the words, and wanting him there while she found her own.

At eight-twenty-two Ellen replied.

*Clarification pending. Preservation request does not authorize collection or inspection. Do not delete existing relevant records while scope is being determined.*

Below it, the copied recipient list still included Midwest.

Sarah sat.

“They can’t collect them,” David said.

“She says this request doesn’t let them.”

“Yes.”

“She doesn’t say nothing does.”

“No.”

The phone rang in Sarah’s hand.

The caller identified itself as Midwest service coordination. She let it ring twice before answering.

A woman asked for her license number.

“You called me,” Sarah said.

“I need to verify the record.”

“What is your name?”

“Linda. Service coordination.”

“Linda, what do you want?”

“To arrange compliance with the preservation notice.”

“Ellen says it doesn’t authorize collection.”

“We aren’t collecting. We’re offering a managed retention service.”

“What would you retain?”

“Relevant activity records.”

“Which ones?”

“That will be determined.”

Sarah looked at David. He had pulled out the other chair but remained standing behind it.

“Then how do you know what to retain?”

“The standard package captures the complete interval so no relevant item is missed.”

“Captures it where?”

“In your licensed support account.”

“Who can read it?”

“Access follows the account permissions.”

“My permissions?”

“The licensed account permissions.”

Sarah laid the phone on the table and enabled the speaker.

“David is here,” she said. “He can hear you now.”

Linda paused.

“Is he authorized to receive service information?”

“I asked him to listen.”

“I’ll note that.”

“Note what?”

“That a third party is present.”

Sarah felt the evening shrinking to the size of a field again.

“Note his name. David Bennett. I asked him.”

Linda’s keyboard sounded through the speaker.

David sat.

“Is your package required?” Sarah asked.

“It is the recommended route.”

“Is it required?”

“The custodian is responsible for demonstrating preservation.”

“Midwest.”

“Yes.”

“So you want my records to demonstrate that your company complied.”

“We want to avoid loss.”

“Which loss?”

“Any loss within the interval.”

She looked toward the bedroom, where the light was still on.

“No,” she said.

Linda stopped typing.

“I can leave the offer open.”

“I declined it.”

“I’ll record that you declined assistance.”

“Record what I declined.”

“The managed retention service.”

“Yes.”

Linda read it back. Sarah waited until the wording was complete, then ended the call.

David had not spoken.

“Was I clear?” she asked.

“Yes.”

She slid the phone away, but did not turn it over.

“I don’t want you to tell me it will be all right.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“What were you going to say?”

He looked at the glass.

“That I haven’t eaten.”

She laughed, and the laugh hurt.

“Neither have I,” she said.

They searched the cupboards together.

There was rice, an onion soft at one end, eggs and a jar David opened, smelled and closed again. Sarah cut the bad part from the onion while he put a pan on the stove.

He reached around her for a knife.

“Ask,” she said.

“For the knife?”

“To come behind me like that.”

He stepped back.

“Can I?”

“Yes.”

This time she leaned into him as he passed.

They ate at eight-fifty-three. The rice stuck in a brown sheet to the bottom of the pan. David put it between them rather than pretending it had not happened.

Sarah scraped some loose.

“I like that part,” she said.

“So do I.”

“You were going to throw it away.”

“I was going to leave it until you couldn’t see.”

She took another piece.

Her phone remained silent through the meal.

Afterward she washed the plates. David dried. They did not discuss whether that was an arrangement.

At nine-oh-six a new message arrived from Midwest. It confirmed her refusal and attached instructions for preserving records without the managed service.

She opened the attachment.

The first page told her to preserve original files, timestamps and associated metadata. The second defined activity records to include internal operational state sufficient to establish the listed system’s condition.

Sarah put the plate down in the water.

“What?” David asked.

She read the definition aloud.

He set the towel on the counter.

“Ask Ellen.”

“Yes.”

She forwarded the attachment with a single question.

*Does your request include this?*

Then she opened a new document of her own.

She wrote the time of Linda’s call, the offer, her refusal, the names of the people present. She left out what had happened in the bedroom. When she reached the meal she stopped, and then wrote that they had eaten.

David watched from the other side of the table.

“Is that for them?”

“I don’t know yet.”

“Then why write it?”

“So I can decide what to give them without having to ask them what happened.”

She saved the document in her own account.

At the top, where the application offered *Activity log*, she entered a different title.

*Friday.*

Then she closed it.
