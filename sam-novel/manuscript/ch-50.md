# Chapter Fifty

The printer took two sheets at once. Sarah heard them go through together and put her hand out to stop the second one falling. It was blank. The other came out with the bottom of Mo’s signature cut off.

“Leave a bit of room round it,” Betty said.

Sarah changed the setting and printed it again. The plate number was in a box at the top of the sheet. Betty took Jo’s receipt out from under the clip and read the number along with Sarah. At the bottom, beneath Mo’s signature, there was a second set of initials. Sarah enlarged the email to see if Mo had said whose they were.

“They’re the fitter’s. He read the bench clock.”

Betty took the sheet Sarah had written that morning and laid it alongside the new one. She ran a finger along the four times. The two on the second line were the same, and she checked them both.

Sarah picked up the stapler. “Where do you want these?”

Betty brought out the sideways printout with the Wednesday reading. She put it between the two sheets and looked at the number she had circled. Then she pulled it clear of both of them.

“I want this the way they sent it,” she said.

“It’s the same file on the laptop.”

“Can they send it again?”

Sarah set down the stapler. She opened the first depot email and looked at the name of the attachment. “I can ask them to keep the copy they took off the monitor. This is the one they sent us.”

“Ask them to keep that one too,” Betty said. “If somebody asks why I said six fourteen, that’s the one I was looking at.”

Sarah began the email with the plate number. She wrote *original download* and stopped.

“Can you ask for the one from Thursday without saying it’s the first one?” Betty asked. “We know when they wrote it in the book.”

Sarah typed *Thursday 19 November, 11:23*, straight from the depot book. She turned the screen so Betty could read the request. There were two things to keep, each on its own line. Betty asked her to send it.

Anne came to the table with the envelope. The answer was showing now. She’d written her own beside the sum, in the space Betty had left.

“Did you get the same?” Betty asked.

Anne held the flap up so she could see both at once. “Yes. Mine took more room.”

Betty took the blank sheet that had gone through the printer and slid it under the envelope. Anne drew a line down the middle. She put the two answers at the top, one on either side, and began to show Betty how she’d got hers.
