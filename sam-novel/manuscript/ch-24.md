# Chapter Twenty-Four

There was a dry place to put a foot for most of the way down. Betty knew which ones would still be dry on the way back. When Sarah stepped on a pale patch that looked like sand, Betty caught her by the cuff and waited while she pulled her shoe free.

“Did you see it move?” Betty asked.

“I saw where somebody had been.”

“That was me yesterday.”

Sarah turned to look. The gatehouse window reflected the sky. She could see Anne on the steps with her arms around the book. David had gone up to move the car. From here it was possible to see both ends of the road at once.

“Do you go all the way to the water every day?”

Betty shook her head. “Only if I can’t tell from the window.”

At the end of the flattened strip there was a plank with two bolts through it. One bolt held it to a post, and the other had nothing under it. Betty stepped past the loose end. Sarah put her foot where Betty had put hers. The grass went flat under her sole and then came up again behind her.

“Here,” Betty said.

Sarah could see the water coming over the bottom of the post. A little piece of foam went downstream and caught on a blade that was lying the other way. It hung there for a moment. Then it slid under.

Behind them the gatehouse door opened. The installer called that he had the blank ready. Betty turned and showed him her bent card, holding it above her shoulder so he could see that she still had it.
