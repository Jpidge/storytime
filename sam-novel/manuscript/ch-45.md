# Chapter Forty-Five

The second time Anne took the night-light out, Betty made her wait until the number dropped. Then Bill plugged the timer in. Its dial began to turn. He put a pencil mark on the rim opposite the arrow so Anne could see it move.

“Nothing in the front,” Anne said.

Betty waited through three changes of the display. On the third it still showed zero. She bent to look at the mark Bill had made. It had travelled just far enough that she could see a strip of white between it and the arrow.

“I can see that moving,” she said.

Sarah wrote down both things. The clock moved. The monitor showed zero.

Bill took the timer out. “We could try it at the bench. I’ve got a meter there that reads smaller loads.”

“Would that tell us about the one they took?” Anne asked.

“It would tell us about this timer.”

He reached for the paper bag and found it folded flat. Betty had used it under the monitor to keep the casing from scratching the table. She lifted the monitor while he pulled the bag free.

Sarah’s phone rang. Helen asked if she still had the printout with the Wednesday line. She had got a name at the depot. A woman called Mo had exported the log and was prepared to look at the original while they had their copy in front of them.

Sarah put the phone on the table and fetched the sheet. When Helen brought Mo onto the call there was a pause while everybody said hello. Then Betty read the Wednesday date and the time from the printout.

“I’ve got that line,” Mo said. “Is there anything to the right of watts on yours?”

Sarah flattened the paper. The next column was blank all the way down.

“Mine’s blank too,” Mo said. “Let me open it in the programme we use on the bench.”

Bill slipped the timer into the bag. The point of its plug went through the paper. He took it out and held it in his lap.

Mo came back and asked Betty to read the time again. Then she said there was a flag on the original. The export had a column for notes, but the flag wasn’t a note. It showed in a different place.

“What does it say?” Betty asked.

“It says the monitor had just come back on.”

Betty looked towards the oven. “Does it say what put it off?”

“I can see when it came back,” Mo said. “There isn’t a line for when it went off.”

Sarah wrote those words beside the printed row, then put Mo’s name and the date underneath. She asked for a copy with the flag on it. Mo said she could send a picture of the whole row with the heading above it.

Anne turned the timer over in Bill’s lap. The mark was still just clear of the arrow. She ran a finger down the rim until she found it.

“Can I leave that on?” she asked.

Bill gave her the timer to hold while he folded the torn part of the bag inwards. “Leave it,” he said. “That’s where we got to.”
