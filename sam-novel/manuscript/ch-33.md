# Chapter Thirty-Three

“I can come round,” Betty said. “I’ve got my shoes on.”

Sarah looked at Anne, who had both hands flat on the table now. Between them the bag lay as flat as a letter.

“We were going to come to you.”

“Well, one of us can take our shoes off.”

Anne pulled out a chair with her foot.

“Come here, then,” Sarah said. “Did Bill tell you about the visit?”

“He told me it was still down for here. Is it?”

“As far as we know. Helen’s asked them to say what the new number is for.”

Betty said she’d be five minutes. She was nearer twenty. Anne had taken her coat off by then and put it back on to answer the door.

“Is that for outside or inside?” Betty asked.

Anne stepped back to let her in. “I was going to come and get you.”

“I had to find my keys. They were in the door.”

Betty put them down beside the bag. There were four on the ring and a little brass tag with a hole punched through the middle. Anne picked the ring up by the disc.

“Those would fit,” she said.

“What would?”

“In here. I did the corner again.”

Betty took the bag and put her finger into the corner. She pressed until the cloth made a point.

“How did you find out it would hold?”

“I tried a spoon.” Anne looked at Sarah. “I didn’t try keys.”

“We could try mine.”

Betty held the mouth open while Anne lowered the ring by its disc. It fell the last inch with a sound like the spoon striking the table. Anne turned the bag over. Betty put her hand under it and caught the keys.

“You have to pull the string first,” Anne said.

“I thought we might.”

They did it again with the string pulled. This time Betty left her hand on the table.

Sarah’s phone rang. Helen didn’t say hello. “They’ve told me what the number is called.”

Sarah unfolded the receipt. “What do they call it?”

“A replacement reference.”

She wrote the two words on a clean strip below the crease.

“Did they say what it replaces?”

“I’ve read it three times. It says to quote it in future correspondence. Then there’s a thank-you.”

“Anything about the sites?”

“No.”

Sarah put the pen down. Betty had loosened the string and was working the ring back out. The brass disc caught at the mouth.

“Can you send the reply to Bill as well?” Sarah asked. “I want him to have the words they used.”

Helen said she would. “Do I put that number in the subject when I write back?”

“What’s there now?”

“The original visit reference.”

“Leave that in the subject. Put the new one in the question so they can tell which one you mean.”

Helen read the question back. It was short enough that Sarah could write it down while she listened. Which reference does this replace? Helen stopped there.

“And the first test and the delivery?” Sarah asked.

“I’ve put those underneath, one on each line. I’ve asked them to answer on the same lines.”

“Put the picture question underneath those,” Sarah said. “Pier Six or South Gate.”

Sarah could hear the tapping start again. She waited until Helen said it had gone.

Betty had got the ring out. “The little one’s the back door,” she told Anne. “I need it where I can reach it without looking.”

Anne tried putting the bag in Betty’s coat pocket. The string hung out. She tucked it in and asked Betty to get the keys.

Betty got as far as the knot before she took her hand out and looked down.

“Leave the string out,” she said. “I can find that.”

Anne put the keys back in, pulled the mouth shut and slipped the bag into the pocket with the string over the edge. Betty drew the bag out by the string and worked a thumb into the mouth. When she brought the keys up she was holding the little one between her finger and thumb.

Sarah slid the receipt towards Betty. “This is what I have so far.”

Betty read it with her keys still in her hand. At the two words below the crease she stopped.

“If they answer that question,” she said, “will we know where they’re coming?”

Sarah looked at the two names she’d written at the side. She moved her finger down the paper to give Betty room to see them both.

“We’ll still need them to answer this one.” Sarah wrote Which site is the picture for? beside the two names.

Betty set the keys down. “Put my address on the receipt too.”

Sarah started to write it from memory. At the house number she stopped and let Betty tell her.
