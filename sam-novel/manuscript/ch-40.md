# Chapter Forty

Betty came home with a loaf under her arm. She put it on the kitchen table and took the envelope from her coat. The fold had gone soft in her pocket. She opened it with the heel of her hand and set the loaf on one corner.

“I got as far as the shop before I thought of what to ask,” she said.

Sarah pulled a chair out for her. “What are you going to ask?”

“If I can take it back myself.”

Betty dialled the number from the envelope. She asked the man who answered to wait while she got her glasses. They were on the table beside the loaf. She had put them there before she went out.

He asked for the collection reference. Betty looked at Sarah, who slid the receipt across and pointed to the separate line. Betty read it to him without lifting the paper.

“I’ve got it,” he said. “Return from sixty-two.”

“Can you leave that on the screen a minute? I want to tell you what’s actually here.”

She took the chair round the end of the table and sat facing the socket. Sarah held the telephone while Betty settled herself. Then Betty read the number from the plate, four one seven three, and took the telephone back.

“That’s the unit we’re waiting for,” he said.

“Can I bring it?”

“Is it still connected?”

“Yes.”

“Then leave it as it is. I can put the right address on the return sheet. What’s the house number?”

“Twenty-six. Same street.”

She waited until he read it back. He said he had saved the address but would leave the collection on hold. The people who did the lifting went out on Thursdays. He couldn’t tell her which of them could disconnect the unit.

“Who can tell me?”

“I can ask the supervisor. She comes in at two.”

“Give her my number,” Betty said. “I’m the one who’ll be here.”

Sarah wrote down the time he had given for the supervisor and put *comes in* beside it. Betty watched her write. She picked up the pencil when Sarah was done and added *call me* to the envelope, in the space where Anne had left room for a date.

Anne came in when she heard the bread knife on the board. She touched the back of the chair beside the socket.

“Can I put this away?”

Betty cut another slice. “Leave it there for now. I can see the number from it.”
