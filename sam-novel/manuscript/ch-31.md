# Chapter Thirty-One

After Bill hung up, Sarah found she was still holding the pen above the receipt. Anne came to the table and looked down at the two numbers.

“Which one goes with the thing they took away?”

Sarah pointed to the lower line.

“And which one goes with Betty?”

Sarah moved the pen back to the first line. That number belonged to the visit. She could have told Anne what had happened on that visit, from the time on the photograph to the words Bill had put in the book. There was still no place on the receipt where she could point and say it was Betty’s.

“Neither of these,” she said.

Anne went to get the peg bag. She brought it back without the pegs and set it beside the receipt.

“Can we take that to her?”

“The receipt?”

“The bag. She could put her card in it when she goes out.”

Sarah picked up the bag by its string. One seam had come apart. She put a finger through the gap and showed Anne.

“We could sew it,” Anne said.

The phone rang before Sarah had let go of the string. It was Helen. She wanted to know if the installer had left yet. “He’s just leaving,” Sarah said. Helen said she’d been asked to put the number from today’s box in place of the job number for the first reader test.

“Who asked you?” Sarah said.

“The message came from Hale Marine dispatch. Same office that sent the sheet. They want me to put it in the box where yesterday’s number is.”

Anne had her finger in the seam now. She was trying to turn the bag inside out without letting go of the string.

“What happens to the number that’s there?” Sarah asked.

“It goes away on my screen. I don’t know where it goes after that.”

Sarah took the cap off the pen. “Will you read me the message?”

Helen read it slowly. The first sentence asked her to replace a reference. The second said the visit would then appear under the correct record. At the bottom there was a button marked *accept*. Sarah looked at the two lines on the receipt. The office still had Pier Six at the top of the sheet.

“Does it say which record?”

“No. I can ask.”

“Can you ask without pressing it?”

Helen said she could answer the email. Sarah heard her start to type, then stop.

“What do I tell them the new number is for?”

“A box he took away. The card that was supposed to be in it wasn’t there.”

“Did Bill see that?”

“He wrote it down before the man took anything out.”

Helen began typing again. This time she read the sentence back before she went on. Anne had got the bag turned and laid it flat beside the receipt. From the inside Sarah could see that the seam had never been sewn all the way to the corner. There was a last stitch with a long tail, held down by dirt.

“Do you want me to tell them about yesterday?” Helen asked.

“Ask them to leave the first test and today’s delivery under their own numbers on your screen. Bill has them separate in his book. They can tell us how the numbers are related.”

Helen read that back too. A little later she said she had sent it. She would leave the reference alone while they waited for an answer.

Sarah put the cap back on the pen. Anne went to the drawer and came back with a spool of blue thread and a needle in a piece of folded paper.

“Will she want it?” Anne asked.

“We can ask her.”

Anne held up the bag by the string. The corner opened under its own weight. She set it down again and pulled out a length of thread. Sarah took the receipt off the table so Anne would have room to lay the whole seam flat.
