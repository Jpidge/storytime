# Chapter Twenty-Six

By the time they got back to the road, the van was running. The installer had set his tablet on the passenger seat, face down. He lowered his window when Sarah stopped beside it.

“Did it keep the note?”

He turned the tablet over. She could see the little picture of a cloud in the corner. There was a line through it.

“It’ll go when I get up the hill,” he said.

She stood back. He pulled out slowly, his front wheel going into the rut David had gone around. When the van straightened she could see the patch of road behind it where the wheel had brought up water.

Anne was in the back seat with her coat undone. She asked Sarah if they were going home.

“In a minute.”

Sarah called Helen again. She gave her the job number this time. Helen read it back in groups of three, with a pause between each group. Sarah followed with her finger on the receipt.

“Have you found it?” she asked.

“I’ve got a job with that number,” Helen said. “What did he say he was saving it as?”

“Unfinished.”

“Then wait with me a second. Mine says it hasn’t started.”

Sarah watched the van climb. At the bend it slowed enough that she could see the lettering on its back door. Then it went behind the hedge.

“What are we waiting for?” Anne asked.

“To see if it changes.”

David turned the key far enough to shut off the fan. In the quiet Sarah could hear Helen’s keyboard. There was a pause, and then a smaller sound like a cup being moved.

“It’s come through,” Helen said.

“Can you read the note?”

“I can see there is one. I can’t open it from this screen.”

Sarah put the receipt against the dashboard and wrote *note received* beside the number. Helen heard the pen and asked what she was doing.

“Making a record.”

“Put down what I can see,” Helen said. “There’s a paperclip. That could be the note or it could be the test.”

Sarah crossed out what she’d written. Under it she put *paperclip*.

“Does it still say it hasn’t started?”

“No. It says complete.”

Sarah looked at David. He was holding the car key in his palm, with his thumb pressed into the teeth.

“Can you see who signed?”

Helen asked her to wait again. This time Sarah heard no keys. A lorry went by, pulling air through the open window, and she had to catch the receipt against the dash.

“There’s no name,” Helen said at last. “The signature space has a dash in it.”

“Can you send me a picture of that?”

Helen said yes. Sarah stayed on the line until the picture arrived. The dash was very short. If she hadn’t known where to look she might have taken it for a mark on the glass. She opened the photograph from the gatehouse and found the same space under Pier Six, white from one end to the other.

“Do you still have the address?” she asked.

“Same road. Same turn.”

Anne had wound her window down. She was looking across the road at the bus stop. “Is he coming back tomorrow?” she asked.

Sarah had been about to tell her what Bill had said. She looked once more at the word on Helen’s screen.

“Pass me the book number,” Helen said. “The one he typed first. I want to see if I can get there without going through the job.”

Sarah turned the receipt over. From the back she could feel the line David had pressed into it when he copied the number. On the front it was still legible, though a little ink had come away on her knee at the gate. She read it slowly. Helen read it back.

“Here it is,” Helen said.

“Where?”

“South Gate. It’s still there.”

Sarah put the phone down between the seats where they could all hear it. “Then start from there,” she said.
