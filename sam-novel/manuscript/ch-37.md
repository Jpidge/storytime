# Chapter Thirty-Seven

Betty waited till the van pulled away before she read her copy again. The man had written on the back with the paper laid against his knee. At the end of the second line the letters ran uphill.

“Can I have a look?” Anne asked.

Betty held the page by its corners and turned it round. Anne put her finger under the first word and moved it along until she came to a word she couldn’t read.

“Is that the thing he left?”

“That says *tested*,” Betty said. “The bit about what he left is underneath.”

Sarah had been about to put the receipt away. She laid it beside the sheet instead. There was room to write a third line on Betty’s copy, but the man had drawn a stroke through the space.

“He said they’d ring about that,” Betty said.

“About what he left?”

“About when it goes.”

Sarah looked again. The sheet said what he’d left in place. It gave no date for taking it away.

Helen rang just as Anne was reaching for the envelope under the telephone. Sarah took the call in the kitchen, where she could still see Betty at the hall table.

“Dispatch says the visit’s closed,” Helen said.

“Under which number?”

Helen read it. Sarah followed the line on the receipt as she listened. It was the number from the man’s sheet.

“Is there anything under the other one?”

“There’s a box I can open now. It says *collection*.”

Sarah took the receipt into the hall. “Can you see what they’re collecting?”

“I can see a date.”

“Read me the rest of the line first.”

Helen read the word *collection* again, then the date, then the reference from the email. There was a field for an address. Helen said it was empty.

Betty had turned her copy back round. “They’ve left it with me,” she said. “I want to know if they mean to take it from me.”

Sarah put the phone down between the papers so Betty could ask Helen herself.

“I can ask them what they mean to collect,” Helen said when Betty was done. “I can put the two references in the same message.”

“Can you put what he actually wrote?” Betty asked.

Sarah held the phone while Betty read the second line. She had to start again after the first three words. Helen waited. When Betty finished, Helen read the line back, including the words at the end where the writing went uphill.

Anne had brought the envelope from the kitchen. “Should I put the date on this?”

Betty looked at the two marks on the flap. She took the envelope from Anne and turned it over to the side with the old sum.

“Put down that somebody’s got to ring,” she said. “You can leave room for the date.”
