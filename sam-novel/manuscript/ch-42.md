# Chapter Forty-Two

At nine on Thursday Betty put the chair where she could see the socket from it. She had swept under the table the night before. There was a clean patch of floor the size of the table’s old position.

Sarah laid the printed sheet beside the envelope. Betty had asked her to print it with the address at the top so she could check it at the door without turning over.

The bell rang at nine forty. Betty heard the second note die away before she stood up. Nobody knocked.

The fitter introduced herself as Jo. She held her sheet by the bottom so Betty could read the top. The number on it was twenty-six. Betty asked if she could bring the sheet inside. Jo handed it over, and Betty carried it to the kitchen.

At the socket Jo knelt and read the plate. “Four one seven three.”

“That’s on mine,” Betty said. She had brought her copy with her and laid it on the chair.

Jo pointed to the plug monitor on the sheet, then to the white block. She asked Betty if anything else in the kitchen was connected to it.

“Only what you can see there,” Betty said. “What happens to the numbers on it when you take it out?”

“The log goes back with it. This display only shows what it’s drawing right now. I can ask the depot for a copy of the log.”

She turned the block enough for Betty to see the display. It showed zero. Jo asked her to wait through one more change of the display. The number stayed where it was. A dot at the right blinked and went out again.

“Does that mean it hasn’t been doing anything?” Betty asked.

“It means it isn’t drawing anything now.”

Betty watched the dot come back. “What’s that for?”

“It flashes while the monitor’s taking a sample. It does that even at zero.”

“Can you put *now* on the sheet? And ask for the log.”

Jo wrote *zero at disconnection*, checked her watch and put the time beside it. Betty read the line back. “Nine fifty-two.” Jo wrote *copy of log requested* underneath. After Jo pulled the plug, the display held the zero for a moment and went dark.

Anne was standing by the door with the envelope. She looked down at the space where she’d written the hours and asked if she could put a tick there.

“Wait till it’s gone,” Betty said.

Jo packed the monitor next to the unit and folded the cable over both. She left Betty a copy with the plate number, the time and a line for the two things she had taken. Betty walked to the door with her. She came back when the gate shut and asked Anne to make the tick.

They pushed the table back together. Anne reached first for the pencil and laid it on the envelope so the string wouldn’t catch. When the table touched the wall Betty pulled it forward half an inch. She could still get two fingers behind it, enough to fish the pencil out if it fell. She left the chair beside the socket while she put the two sheets together.
