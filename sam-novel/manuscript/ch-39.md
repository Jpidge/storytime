# Chapter Thirty-Nine

Betty put her shoes on at a quarter to ten. Anne had been watching the pavement through the gap in the curtain. Two vans had gone past, one with ladders on top and one with nothing she could see through the back windows.

“Would you know it if you saw it?” Betty asked.

“I’d know if it stopped.”

Betty let the curtain fall. “Come and help me with this buckle.”

The call came while Anne was still on one knee. Sarah answered. It was Helen, asking whether anyone had been.

“No one’s rung the bell,” Sarah said. She looked at Betty, who shook her head. “And no one’s called Betty.”

“They’ve taken it off the driver’s list,” Helen said. “I asked her to read it back to me. The collection is still open, but there’s no driver against it.”

Sarah got the receipt out. “Did she give you a reason?”

“She said the driver had nowhere to go.”

Betty had got the buckle through the hole. She stood up and took the phone from Sarah.

“He had sixty-two,” she said.

Helen said the woman had found that address in a box headed *return from*. She’d also found the original visit, with twenty-six on it. She could see both at once, but they were on different pages and she couldn’t change the one headed *return from*.

“Can she see what she’s getting back?” Betty asked.

“She read me four one seven three. It was on a list of things out on test. I asked her to send me the line she read.”

Betty looked towards the kitchen. The table was still away from the wall.

“Who has to change the address?”

“The depot. She’s given me their number.”

“Give it to me too,” Betty said.

Anne brought the envelope and the pencil as far as the string would let her. Betty went to the table to write. Helen read the depot number in groups, waiting after each one until Betty said she had it. Sarah could hear Betty trying the middle group back to herself before she wrote it down.

“Will you ring them now?” Anne asked.

Betty looked at the clock. “When I get back.”

She put the envelope under the telephone and then thought better of it. She folded it once, with the depot number on the outside, and put it in the pocket with the keys.

At the door she asked Sarah to leave the table where it was. She wanted to be able to read the plate without getting down on the floor again.
