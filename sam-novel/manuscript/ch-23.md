# Chapter Twenty-Three

The bus stop was a post with no seat. David put the indicator on there, then took it off again when he saw how the road bent. He went past and turned at a break in the hedge.

“Is that it?” Anne asked.

“That was where we’d have got off.”

From the top of the road Sarah could see the gatehouse roof. It was lower than she’d expected. The fence went out from either side of it and disappeared in the grass. She couldn’t see where the water began.

Helen answered on the third ring. Sarah held the phone against the window to get the call back when it broke up.

“We’re here,” she said. “Could you stay near your phone?”

“Did you find the one in the book?”

“We’ve found the book.”

Bill had come out onto the road with it under his arm. A white van stood by the fence. There was a man sitting in it with the door open and one foot on the ground. He looked up when David stopped, then looked back down at what he was holding.

Bill waited until Sarah got out to say, “He’s early.”

“Is the old one still on?”

“I asked him to leave it.”

She could hear a click below them. She walked to the top of the steps. The little door was open about the width of her hand. On the other side of it the grass was pressed down in a narrow strip that went as far as she could see.

“Does it shut by itself?” she asked.

“It’s meant to,” Bill said.

There was a flat stone wedged under it. Bill saw her looking and put the book on the top step. He went down to take the stone away, then stopped with his hand on the fence.

“Do you want it shut?”

Sarah looked at the old reader on the far side of the door. A screw was missing from the bottom of its cover. She could see the hole from where she stood.

“Is Betty out there?”

“No. That’s for him. He said he’d need to be on both sides.”

Sarah went down one step, enough to see past the fence post. Beside the old reader a loop of cable had been tied up with white string. The end was bare.

“Which cable is that?”

“The new one,” said the man from the van. He was standing at the top of the steps with the tablet under his arm. “I can show you before I plug it in.”

She moved up so he could pass. He went down without looking at the book. At the bottom he bent and lifted the stone out of the way with the toe of his boot. He kept his hand on the door until it settled into the latch.

“Would you put your card against it?” the man called.

Bill had his hands in his pockets. “Mine?”

“Hers, if she’s the one you want to test.”

Bill went up to the gatehouse. Sarah could hear him from the steps. He asked Betty if she had the card and Betty asked him if he wanted her to come. He said yes, and the man at the bottom took his hand off the door.

Betty came out first. She was carrying the card flat against her side, under her arm where the wind wouldn’t get at it. Sarah knew her when she stopped on the landing and said, “You came the way the bus goes.”

“Yes,” Sarah said. She had been going to introduce herself. Betty had already turned to look at the grass.

“You can see it from there,” Betty said. “You have to stand to one side of him.”

Sarah moved. Beyond the strip of flattened grass there was a narrow reach of water. The surface ran away from the fence. Under it the long grass leaned toward the fence, every blade that she could see.

“How far in does that go?”

“I can show you when I go out,” Betty said.

The man picked up the tablet. “Can I get the number first?”

Bill opened the book on the top step. He ran his thumb down the outside of the page, stopped, and turned the book so the man could read it from below. The man read the last four digits aloud. Sarah took out the grocery receipt. It was the one David had copied from the gate ledger. She pressed the receipt against her knee to keep it still.

The man typed with one finger. Then he held the tablet out to Bill. “That’s what it gives me.”

Bill came down two steps and looked. “That’s not the same number.”

“No. That’s what the sheet’s for.”

“Can I see?” Sarah asked.

The man put the tablet on the step between them. The number he had entered was in a box she could have tapped to change. The number from the lab was beneath it in grey. Next to it was a place to tick. Nothing had been ticked yet.

“If you write that to her card,” she asked, “can she still use it on the old reader?”

“I don’t know,” he said. He looked up at Bill. “Have you got a spare?”

“I’ve got mine.”

“I can use a blank.”

Betty took her card out from under her arm. There was a bend in one corner. She fitted it under her thumb so the card lay flat.

“Could you use a blank for the new number?” Sarah asked. “Leave hers as it is.”

The man looked down into his case. He lifted two short leads, then a packet of white cards held together with a rubber band. “I can do that. It won’t tell me if the new reader lets her in until I connect it.”

“Then can we try hers on this one first?”

He put the packet on the step. “Go ahead.”

Bill put his hand on the bar from the inside and pushed the door open. Betty went through. Bill started to follow and Sarah caught his sleeve. He looked at her hand, then at the door. He let it close.

Betty turned around and put her card against the old reader. There was a click, the one Sarah had heard from the top of the steps. Betty pulled the door toward her.

The man reached for his tablet.

“Wait,” Sarah said.

Betty had let the door close again. She was looking past them, down at the place where the grass disappeared into the water.

“Are you coming?” she asked Sarah.

Sarah looked back at Anne. She was holding the gatehouse book shut against the wind with both hands. David put a hand on its cover so she could let go if she wanted.

“I’ll be back in a minute,” Sarah told them. She went down the steps and pushed the bar. When she was through, Betty put the card back under her arm. Neither of them reached for the stone.
