# Chapter Fifty-Two

Helen rang while Sarah was looking for an envelope big enough to take the sideways sheet without folding it. Betty nodded towards the phone. Sarah put it on the table and turned up the sound.

“Have they asked you for anything?” Betty said.

“No. I was going to ask what you wanted me to do with the email.”

“The one from the depot?”

“The one to dispatch. I’ve still got it open from when they read back the instructions.”

Betty looked under the telephone. The old envelope was there, thin where Anne had put the tick. She drew it out far enough to see the words on the flap.

“Could you ask them to ring me?”

Helen waited. “What do you want them to ring about?”

Betty picked up the sheet she’d signed. For a moment she thought she could give Helen the first line and Helen could tell it to whoever answered. She put it down again.

“I want to know where I can send this. There’s something I saw on their printout and something I remember myself. If I send it to you, will it go on their record as something you told them?”

Helen said she didn’t know. She could ask them where they put things that came from the person at the address.

“Ask them to tell me,” Betty said. “I can write down where it goes.”

Sarah pulled a chair up. She brought the white envelope she’d found, and Betty put her hand on it so she wouldn’t seal it yet.

Helen rang back before lunch. Dispatch had offered to take the details down over the phone. Helen had asked if there was an address for Betty to send her own account. There was. They’d sent it to Helen, who could forward it.

“Read me the name it goes to,” Betty said.

Helen read out the name of the inbox. It had the word *visits* in it. Betty asked whether it was the one that had sent the replacement reference. Helen went back to the two messages and read both addresses. The names before the little sign were different.

Betty had Sarah write the new address on the white envelope even though they wouldn’t be posting it. Under it she put the address of the house. She read that back to Helen herself, including the number. When they’d finished, she slipped her statement inside with the sideways sheet, leaving the flap up. From her chair she could reach in and take out either one.
