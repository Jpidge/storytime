# Chapter Sixty

At eleven forty-three Mo rang. Betty had the iron on. She stood it on its heel and pulled the plug before she picked up.

“He’s saved it,” Mo said. “I’ve got the file here. It’s got the runs from when you had it as well.”

Betty called Sarah in. She held the phone while Sarah got the envelope out from under the laptop.

“Can you tell which is which?” Betty asked.

“I can tell you where this morning starts. It’s all one file when it comes out of the machine.”

Mo could send the whole file to visits through the depot. She could send Betty this morning’s rows by email. Any earlier rows would have to go through visits first because the depot had no way of telling from the file where the monitor had been on every date.

“You’ve sent us one before,” Betty said.

“I sent the part you asked for. This has got everything since the last time they cleared it.”

Betty looked at the sideways sheet. The fold ran through the date. She pressed it flat with the side of her hand.

“If you send it to visits, can you tell them which one to put it with?”

“Give me the number.”

Betty read her the collection number. Mo read it back. Sarah put a finger on the date of the depot test below it and waited.

“Can you put the date of this morning’s test in the message as well?” Betty asked. “I don’t want them thinking you did it here.”

Mo said she would put both dates in. Betty gave her the address from the reply they’d had on Wednesday. Mo read that back too. While she was doing it, the iron made a small click behind Betty. She turned round. The cord was hanging where she’d left it.

“Have you still got the bench sheet?” she asked.

“I’ll send the photograph with it.”

“Thank you,” Betty said. She put the phone down.

The email to Betty came first. Sarah checked the file opened, then went to shut the laptop. Betty asked her to leave it up a minute. The last thing Mo had put in the message was that the file had gone to visits, with the collection number in the subject. Sarah showed her where it said that. Betty folded the sideways sheet on its old fold and put it back in the envelope.

“Now we can ask them to look at both,” she said, and reached for the phone.
