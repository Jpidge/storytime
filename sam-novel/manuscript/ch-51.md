# Chapter Fifty-One

On Tuesday the first of December, Mo sent back Sarah’s two lines. There was a tick beside each one. She’d put the readings attachment aside as sent, and the depot had kept the file from the Thursday download with the entry for eleven twenty-three. Sarah printed the reply. It took half a page.

Betty turned it over. “Does it say anywhere on the other papers where I was?”

Sarah reached for the sideways printout. Betty put her hand on it first.

“I know what that one says. I’m asking where it says I was in the kitchen.”

Sarah went through the sheets on the table. There was the sheet Jo had left, Mo’s signed test and Sarah’s notes from the call. Sarah turned over the page where she’d written her questions. The back was blank.

“No,” Sarah said. “There’s nothing here about where you were.”

Betty got up and went to the oven. She turned the timer just far enough for it to catch, then brought it back to zero. It gave a little click. She kept her hand on it.

“I stopped it before it rang,” she said.

“On Wednesday?”

“On the eighteenth. I’d set it for quarter past six.”

Sarah took a clean sheet out of the tray. “Did you see the clock when you stopped it?”

Betty turned to look at the kitchen clock. From where she stood she could see the whole of its face. She remembered the heat coming out when she opened the oven. She could remember which hand had been on the door. She couldn’t remember looking up.

“I knew what I’d set the timer for,” she said. “Put that.”

Sarah wrote it down with the kitchen pencil. “Do you remember looking at the socket when you opened the oven?”

Betty took her hand off the oven and stood a moment before she answered.

“I don’t remember looking at it.”

Sarah left a line between the two entries and read them back. “I was in the kitchen. I switched off the oven timer before it rang. I’d set it for quarter past six. I don’t remember looking at the clock.” She put a finger on the next line. “I don’t remember looking at the socket.”

Betty went round the table for her glasses and read both entries herself.

“Should we put six fourteen anywhere?” Sarah asked.

Betty picked up the sideways sheet by its corner. “It’s on there. I can show you where I read it.”

Sarah put the new sheet next to the printout. At the top she’d written Wednesday the eighteenth.

“When did I tell you this?” Betty asked.

“Just now.”

Betty watched Sarah put *On Wednesday the eighteenth* into the first sentence. At the top Sarah wrote *Tuesday 1 December*. She slid the sheet towards Betty as far as the pencil string would let it go. Betty wrote her name below the last line.
