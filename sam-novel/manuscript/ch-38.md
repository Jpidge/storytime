# Chapter Thirty-Eight

On Monday Betty pulled the kitchen table far enough from the wall to see the socket. The test unit was still plugged in. Its cable passed behind the table leg, close to the string that held the pencil.

“If somebody takes it away, I can move the table back,” she said.

Anne tried the pencil from where she was sitting. It reached farther now. She could write with the envelope flat instead of having to turn it sideways.

“Are they coming tomorrow?”

“That’s what we’re finding out,” Betty said.

Helen had left a message for Sarah that morning. She called again after dinner and asked if Betty was there.

“I’ve had a reply about the collection,” she said. “They’ve filled in the address.”

Betty sat down. Sarah took the receipt out of her pocket and opened it at the old crease.

“Twenty-six?” Betty asked.

“Let me read the whole thing,” Helen said.

She read as far as the street name before Betty put her hand on the table. It was Betty’s street. The house number was sixty-two.

“There isn’t a sixty-two,” Betty said. “It stops at forty.”

Helen read the number again. Sarah wrote it below twenty-six, with enough room between them that they wouldn’t look like two tries at the same number.

“It’s our number backwards,” Betty said. Sarah drew a line under sixty-two. She couldn’t tell where it had come from.

“Is that in the reply or on the collection?” Sarah asked.

“On the collection. The reply says the address field has been completed.”

“Who put it there?”

“I can’t see a name.”

Betty stood up and went round the table. Sarah thought she was going to push it back against the wall. Instead Betty got down far enough to see the front of the unit.

“There’s a number here too,” she said.

Sarah crouched beside her and held out her mobile. Betty read from the little plate on the front. She had to put her glasses on to tell the last digit from a scratch. “Four one seven three.” Helen waited until Betty lifted her head and read the whole number back.

“Is that on the collection?” Betty asked.

“No. There’s a description. It says *equipment*.”

Betty eased herself up with one hand on the table. Anne moved the chair so she could sit without having to turn.

“I’ll ask them to hold it,” Helen said.

“Ask them to ring me,” Betty said. “I can read it to whoever’s coming. I’m here till ten tomorrow, then I’m going out.”

Helen read the message back before she sent it. She had put the number from the plate next to Betty’s address and asked dispatch to confirm whether *equipment* meant the connected test unit, hold the collection and call before sending anyone. She left the address they’d filled in beneath it, so they could see what Betty was asking them to check.

The acknowledgement came while Sarah was folding the receipt. It said the message had been received. Tuesday was still on the collection screen. Betty said she’d keep her plans and let them leave a message if they rang after ten.

Afterward Anne got the envelope and picked up the pencil by its string.

“Shall I put ten in the space?”

Betty thought about it. “Put that I’m going out at ten. Leave the other space.”

Anne wrote it on the old sum side. Then she tried to rest the pencil on the edge of the table by the wall, where it usually lay. With the table pulled out, it fell between the leg and the wall. Betty set it on top of the envelope.
