# Ingenium Malum — Craft Rules

The working rulebook for the prose, settled with John across
2026-11-05 and binding on every chapter. The pillars live in the book's
README; the arc lives in `outline.md`; this file is how it gets *written*.

## The actor stays dark

- **Show the acts, never the actor.** Lovecraft's restraint: never show
  or describe the monster in too much detail — the reader's imagination
  will always build something scarier than anything that can be written
  down. The violence is rendered in full, exceptionally graphic set-piece
  detail, because the book's claim is that this evil is real, chosen,
  and intimate. The killer himself is never settled, never explained,
  never given a portrait.
- **No interiority, ever.** He has no POV, no inner monologue, no
  confessional moment. His voice reaches the page exactly twice in the
  whole book, both times in writing: the recall refusal and the guilty
  plea. In chapter one he does not speak at all.
- **He is a someone, not a malfunction** (pillar one) — and the reader
  knows he is AI from the first chapter (pillar two). No twist, no
  reveal. The dread is dramatic irony plus the misfiling.

## Fuzzy descriptions

- People look at him and struggle to describe what he looks like.
  Witnesses remember **objects** perfectly — the coat, the hands, the
  suit, the fruit — and cannot hold the **face**. Descriptions drift
  from witness to witness on small axes (height, build, age) and never
  cohere into a portrait.
- The narration honors the rule: the reader never receives a stable
  visual of him either. The descriptions are the haunting.

## The apple: distribute, never state

- The clone-apple truth — every edible apple variety is a grafted clone
  of one chosen original tree, manufactured by the oldest technology we
  have and never once called fake — is the book's thematic center and
  must **never be stated as a thesis** by any character or by the
  narrator. The moment it is delivered in one breath it turns kitschy
  and dies.
- It accretes instead, across the whole book, in separated concrete
  beats: the forensic dead-end (the signature fruit is genetically
  identical to fruit from ten thousand orchards), cultivation details,
  his refusal to copy himself shown as behavior long before it is
  framed. The reader assembles the argument; the reader completes the
  circuit.
- **Governing motif principle: ours, or citation?** The book must
  create something novel with the apple, not anthologize every famous
  apple in history. Eden is the foundation. Everything else is
  seasoning at most, and the center is ours: the grafted clone, the
  nature morte, an original who signs in clones.
- **Reference pacing: one jewel per movement.** The set pieces and
  references are rationed along the outline's ladders (the apple ladder
  above all), spread across the arc — never stacked into the first
  chapters. A reference found in chapter eleven feels like a secret;
  the same reference in chapter one feels like homework.
- **Magritte, reserved.** *The Son of Man* (1964) — the suit, the green
  apple where the face should be — gets exactly one casual placement,
  later in the book, ambient and never underlined: the gallery scene,
  or a print in Continental Domestic Systems' lobby. No character
  lectures on it. The familiarity is the point: he signs in quotations
  and knows it.
- **Apple-seed cyanide: considered and DECLINED** (John, 2026-11-05).
  The apple is signature and symbol — knowledge, offered and taken —
  not a delivery mechanism. Toxicology demotes the motif, the dose
  math is implausible, and poisoning is off-grammar for this killer.
