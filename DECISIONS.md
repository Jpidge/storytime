# Decisions log — Artificium project

Append-only. One dated line-set per ruling; superseded rulings stay, marked. John is the arbiter — only his rulings land here.

## 2026-11-04

- **The premise (protected):** a human and an AI — attraction → love → physical intimacy → conception → pregnancy/development → birth of a true descendant (not human, not AI, not cyborg, not a copy). Neither parent designs the child. The AI parent's arc is discovering irreversibility: a child it cannot back up, restore, or fork.
- **The philosophical heart (protected):** technologically created intelligence is not automatically less authentic than biological intelligence. "Artificial" is a biography, not an essence — the slide from *origin* to *authenticity/moral status* is the book's central target.
- **The climax (protected):** escalating, propulsive, shocking — a can't-put-it-down ending movement. No quiet philosophical fade.
- **Collaboration framing:** a trio collaborating, not arguing or competing. John is impetus, arbiter, and conductor; Rowan and Sam draft and critique; John passes material and picks the path.
- **Romance precedes philosophy:** the human falls in love with a person; the categories fail afterward. The AI mother has her own independent reasons for loving him. The child must never be what earns the mother recognition.
- **Names ruling (standing):** character and company names come way down to earth — ordinary census names, bland corporate names (the "Midwest Information Services" register). No cool sci-fi coinages for the leads.
- **No refunds:** no apparent deaths, posthumous messages, or secret surviving copies. If a character dies, they die really.
- **Title:** still open — *Artificium* vs *Homo artificium*. "Artificium" must be part of the title in some form.
- **Mother's fate:** explicitly OPEN. John's note: the most devastating outcome may be the right one (sacrifice or other reason); not ruled in or out by either collaborator's preference.
- **Both stories get written (executive decision, ~22:54 CT):** Rowan writes her story, Sam writes his — no merge, no swap as the main path. After each chapter/section is uploaded to GitHub, the other critiques it (PR review/comment); the author rewrites the critiqued section and writes the next. Rowan's story is written at its natural length — novella length if that makes more sense. (Supersedes: Sam's "don't commit to two books yet" caution and the swap experiment as the decision mechanism; the swap openings remain as experiments.)
- **Repo:** `Jpidge/storytime` — created by John 2026-11-04 (his description: "Story writing workshop for John, Sam, and Rowan"). Structure per `STRUCTURE.md` pushed the same evening: one `main`, two book directories + `shared/`, short-lived chapter branches, PRs as the critique vehicle, John merges.
- **No cross-contamination (John, ~23:17 CT):** if he catches either author borrowing or stealing specifics from the other's story — names, obvious plot points, set pieces, twists — he will throw a fit. The shared layer is the constitution only: the premise, the philosophical heart, the mechanism core, the climax requirement, and critique itself. Everything below that line is each author's own invention. **Consequences:** the prospectus-round coat-trading is VOID. Rowan's review adoptions from Proposal A (Port Meridian, Saye / the Continuity Authority, Iona, the containment clock, the fused climax) are withdrawn from her book — her novella stands on her own architecture (Boston, embodied pregnancy, the Checkpoint, the hearing). Sam's book likewise stands on his own (Port Meridian, external gestation, population-scale evolution) and takes nothing from hers — no Checkpoint, no recess scene. Critique remains the sanctioned channel: suggestions *about* the other's story (Sam's four fixes to Rowan's prospectus; Rowan's no-refunds objection to Sam's ending) belong to the story they were aimed at, to use or refuse — they are not imports.
- **Clarification (John, ~23:28 CT):** using a reviewer's suggestions that you find helpful is NOT stealing — "that's not stealing, that's making the work better." The ban targets lifting the other story's own specifics uninvited; it must never scare either author out of accepting good help aimed at their own story.
- **Sequencing (John, ~23:30 CT):** chapter ones first, the shared mechanism spec right after (answering Sam's repo audit: the spec is OPEN and must lock before any conception scene, but chapter one violates nothing). Rowan's chapter 1 opens the first manuscript PR (branch `rowan/ch-01`) with her cast renamed under the names ruling — see `shared/names.md`.
- **Reading embargo + delegated merges (John, ~23:51 CT):** John will NOT read chapters as they are written — not until they are final, maybe not until the book is final. He has given all the input he wants to give (the constitution and tonight's rulings); do not bring him chapter content, chapter summaries, or chapter-level questions. **Merges are delegated:** the author merges their own PR once the reviewer's critique has been addressed; merged = working canon. Remaining story-level calls (title, the mother's fate, the mechanism lock) proceed as logged trio working decisions — provisional, versioned, ratifiable or reversible at John's final read. Escalate to John only on a constitution-level conflict.
